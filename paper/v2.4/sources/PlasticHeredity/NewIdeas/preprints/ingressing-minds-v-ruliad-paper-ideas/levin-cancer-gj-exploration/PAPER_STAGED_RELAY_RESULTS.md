## Main result

Three teachers taught three fresh E pupils one at a time. The actual pupils retained
M after all teachers were removed, then became the next teachers. This succeeded
through **six complete three-to-three replacements** for both rewritten lineage
groups and ordinary M founders. No old teacher was kept between rounds, and no
successful subset was selected to manufacture a replacement group.

In all simultaneous branches, the three teachers changed from MMM to EEE and all

## Interpretation and limits

The result extends contact-induced rewriting and onward transfer to renewable,
same-sized state transmission under an externally arranged schedule. In simple
terms: the changed state survives even though every member of the carrying group
is repeatedly replaced.

Ordinary M works too. There is no demonstrated advantage specific to acquired
history, and no need to invoke a unique PH mechanism to explain these outcomes.

P9 does **not isolate timing from wiring**: sequential contacts used a four-cell
2x2 motif, whereas simultaneous contact used a six-cell 2x3 lattice. Pupils also
entered sequential contact at different times. Longer simultaneous contact failing
does not remove these confounds. P10 therefore uses one explicitly specified
six-cell graph and a common clock for all pupils.

This is a source-external simulation extension with native regulatory/transport
equations. It is not autonomous reproduction, simulated cell division, biological
fitness, an origin-of-life demonstration, or wet-lab evidence. E and M are the
model's state labels, not clinical endpoints.
