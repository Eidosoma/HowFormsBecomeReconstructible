### D.1. Strict-eight: rule-dependent structure without an established final catalogue

The model background is compositional inheritance. Segré, Ben-Eli, and Lancet showed in GARD simulations how mutually enhanced molecular recruitment, growth, and splitting can produce compositionally biased, approximately homeostatic assemblies. Information in that construction resides in molecular abundances and their dynamical effects, rather than requiring a sequence-based template. The strict-eight program adds a particular event criterion to this model family; it should not be read as the origin of the compositional-inheritance proposal. [Segré et al., 2000](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.97.8.4112)

#### D.1.1. What the event detects

The strict-eight program began with a demanding temporal event: departure from an old anchor, followed by eight consecutive inherited fissions whose eight observed daughters are mutually coherent and remain distinct from that anchor. The mutual requirement includes all 28 daughter pairs. The event concerns a followed lineage; it does not by itself certify the entire branching family of descendants. Native similarity thresholds, anchors, and time windows belong to the measurement contract. [E01]

This event is more informative than observing a brief recovery in parent-daughter resemblance. It requires sustained local inheritance together with coherence across the window. Nevertheless, certification is an observation of a finite episode. It is not a definition of an organism, an indefinitely stable identity, or a capacity to maintain itself actively.

The distinction from evolvability is substantive. In their analysis of GARD, Vasas, Szathmáry, and Santos found that compositional transmission did not sustain the response to selection required by their evolutionary criterion. Their result makes persistence and evolutionary accessibility separate questions. Our finite event definition does not resolve that debate, and their model-specific limitation should not become an impossibility claim about every autocatalytic architecture. [Vasas et al., 2010](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0912628107)

A separate, prospectively evaluated compositional-anomaly campaign observed **3,519 strict-eight events in 204,800 natural-GARD futures**, compared with none in 102,400 combined homogeneous and fission-only null futures. Its primary recurrence criterion nevertheless failed. The existence of the event and its separation from these null mechanisms therefore have stronger support than that campaign's proposed recurrence signature. [E17]

#### D.1.2. The rulebook influences subsequent organization

The original studies motivated the idea that a catalytic matrix provides a repertoire into which different histories can settle. Independent reconstruction provides a more qualified account. S8-Core tested 96 confirmation matrices and reproduced strong own-versus-foreign location contrasts and redirection toward the active matrix's destinations. Its combined conditional-rehoming gate nevertheless failed because one of four candidate/threshold cells failed. The repertoire-definition and prospective hold-control gates also failed. These outcomes must be reported together. [E02]

The later R11 study reproduced its matrix-specificity and doorway-rehoming claims. It did not reproduce a countable repertoire, a causal-boundary claim, or matched-size scaling. Its continuous surrogate failed admission, so the comparison did not decide whether the observed structure required discrete forms rather than a matched continuous process. R12 resolved engine conformance in its tested bridge while leaving detailed routing inconclusive and the tested hold intervention a proxy rather than an established causal mechanism. [E03]

The supported interpretation is that the declared dynamics influence where lineages go. Evidence for that influence should not be promoted into a small, final inventory of all forms. Increasingly dense sampling, wandering traffic, representation choice, and model-contract differences remain relevant to what a catalogue would mean.

#### D.1.3. A destination can be real yet fail to identify its cause

Later branching work exposed a further problem. Several alternative matrix programs produced almost indistinguishable composition-level destination maps. In the R20.5 retained-data diagnostic, 97% and 99% of evaluable HIGH traffic in the two candidates collided with at least one registered wrong map. An artificial fixed-target map remained distinguishable. Thus the observer could separate sufficiently different destinations, while the biological-model alternatives being compared largely overlapped at that resolution. [E04]

A descendant's arrival at a common endpoint therefore cannot identify which of those nearly coincident programs it followed. This is an identifiability limit of the proposed attribution, not proof that the matrix has no effect. Trajectory-level measurements may help, but that possibility requires its own tests. The available R21 development report selects candidate trajectory descriptions; it is not fresh confirmation. [E04]

#### D.1.4. Arrival, holding, and active maintenance remain separate

R20's registered acquisition-propagation and active-maintenance claims were not established. Continued-versus-withdrawn support nevertheless produced positive generation-five retention contrasts in both candidates. The narrower R20.6 analysis then asked whether the correct matrix consistently outperformed mismatched alternatives from the same strict-eight state; correct-matrix specificity was not established. [E05]

The distinction matters. Removing support can reveal dependence on some continuing environmental activity without identifying the specificity of the support or showing that the lineage regulates it. A static supportive environment, an externally chosen intervention, and an internally adjusted maintenance process make different claims about agency.

#### D.1.5. Available storage capacity is distinct from acquiring its writer

The compositional-memory branch explores a complementary intervention: changing the catalytic landscape itself while launching the compared histories from a common composition. In exploratory Experiment 5C.3, target-aware matrix modifications passed the registered bidirectional recovery criteria through generation 48 on a previously used panel with fresh futures. Opposite-history matrices, original matrices, and dose-matched controls distinguish the supplied landscape's effect from the common starting state. [E18]

This is a developmental demonstration of engineered storage capacity. Its protocol requires a later untouched-panel study before a confirmatory preprint claim, so it is not included here as a main confirmation. The conceptual distinction is nevertheless useful: a landscape capable of expressing different forms need not come with a mechanism that discovers, writes, or renews the appropriate landscape from experience. The origin of that writer is a separate empirical problem.

### D.2. Bounded observation: what persists depends on what is measured

The cellular-automaton studies show a direct dependence of detected continuity on the measurement map. In the audited comparisons, all four named class-4 rules had zero raw-anchor rates but positive qualifying particle-observer results at widths 63-65 and noise 0.01. At width 128, none of those four remained in the qualifying band. The effect is therefore substantial within its tested range and explicitly scale-dependent. [E06]

Wolfram's early CA classification supplies the historical setting: simple update rules can generate markedly different qualitative regimes, including persistent complex structures. Our experiments ask a different, more specific question within such regimes-what continuity a declared observer detects across disruption. Membership in a qualitative class alone supplies neither a heredity criterion nor evidence of agency. [Wolfram, 1984](https://doi.org/10.1016/0167-2789%2884%2990245-8)

The observer does not create the underlying trajectories. It changes which distinctions are retained and which events are counted. This makes the observer part of an operational account of pattern identity without making its judgments arbitrary. A useful observer can be fixed prospectively, challenged with nulls, tested across scales, and evaluated for its ability to predict intervention outcomes.

The cross-model atlas studies supply a related lesson. Six simulated model families can be represented in common behavioural coordinates, and some placements and composed maps have retained support. Yet rank normalization aligns marginal coordinates by construction, and the original overlap statistic was heavily influenced by the composition of the reference collection. The audit therefore supports a common representation more strongly than one natural catalogue shared by all substrates. [E07]

Recognizability across models is an important starting point. A stronger identity claim requires that the comparison preserve relevant responses and capacities, not only a location in an embedding. This suggests treating cross-embodiment translation as preservation of specified relations under intervention. It remains a research objective rather than a result established by atlas overlap.

#### D.2.1. An apparent reset need not erase the underlying distinction

The continuous-GRN observer atlas makes this issue concrete. Its 24-candidate panel contained 21 complete combined-reset profiles across 13 BioModels. A response-only observer classified 14 of 24 candidates as reset, whereas the richer state and counterfactual levels admitted only two of 24. These are descriptive exploratory classifications, including incomplete and invalid candidates in the full panel denominator, rather than confirmatory reset rates. The report retains invalid or dysfunctional profiles separately. These rates are not estimates of biological reset efficacy. [E19]

Thus a measured response can disappear while other tests still distinguish the resulting condition. Later contingency tests classified the surviving effect as durable exposure state rather than qualified relational memory. A stricter history-fingerprint study admitted none of eight candidates, and the causal recurrence tests did not establish native active renewal. These findings narrow the interpretation of apparent memory without proving that every remaining state is memoryless. [E20]

#### D.2.2. The observer must itself survive a causal audit

The compositional-anomaly work also diagnosed an observer artifact: smoothing molecular counts before a composition transform introduced sensitivity to total mass and reversed the apparent growth/fission cycle. Removing that scale term restored the expected directions. A calibrated measurement is therefore necessary before attributing an observed temporal signature to the system's organization. [E17]

#### D.5.1. Compact CA renewal

The CA motif-lineage Stage 4 report records a fresh confirmation of compact carrier renewal. A carrier consisting of sixteen four-bit Walsh coefficients-a 64-bit lineage payload-passed the registered causal ladder under ordinary and moderate joint-stress conditions. Every daughter began from the same visible board; only the encoded carrier crossed the boundary. The next carrier was written from the daughter's late activity. The protocol followed sixteen generations and included no-rewrite, erasure, shuffle, read-off, and correct/opposite rescue controls. The no-rewrite control halved decoded coefficient amplitudes before requantization at each boundary: it tests renewal against declared decay, not superiority to a perfectly preserved copy. [E21]

The 64 bits describe payload representation size, not measured information content or the total complexity of the system. This Stage 4 cohort is separate from v4's source-isolated direct replication; both use 128 founder pairs but report different numerical results. The codebook, quantizer, reader, writer schedule, and update rules were supplied machinery. Later localization and dynamic-form experiments pose further questions; their failures or development-only results do not erase this narrower finite-renewal result, and the Stage 4 success does not establish their stronger claims.

The later Stage 6F writeback study separates external decodability from a working renewal route. After the incoming carrier is destroyed, a held-out-pair ridge observer identifies history from the first carrier-free 4 × 4 patch at 0.849121 accuracy versus 0.5 sham. None of twelve live writers passes screening, and the best read effect is only 0.047363. These are different endpoints. The observer can learn a cross-bit translation, whereas the tested writer assumes that visible pixels already align with the Walsh code. The result is consequently a decoding-writing mismatch within the supplied apparatus, not evidence that no possible writer could use the signal. Corrected Stage 6E's one-generation bridge, Stage 6F's failed writeback and Stage 4's earlier engineered renewal answer different questions. The retained log and follow-up protocol supply this report-based comparison; no original trajectory audit is added here. [E81]

#### D.5.2. Wagner storage and renewal

Wagner's original regulatory-network study showed that stabilizing selection could reduce the sensitivity of expression patterns to mutation. This supplies a model-family precedent for separating a phenotype from the regulatory interactions that stabilize it. The inherited latch and renewal interventions below are later additions in our program, requiring their own evidence. [Wagner, 1996](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1558-5646.1996.tb02342.x)

The retained gre-ideas log and bounded-replication audit report a passing full ten-entry inherited latch across 128 fresh rulebooks, with erasure, rescue, and content-reversal controls. This is distinct from v4's completed 240-rulebook corrected carrier campaign and from the unrun 240-rulebook full confirmation proposed within gre-ideas. The provenance supplement keeps all three identities separate. The later renewal-frontier study explicitly compared intact and renewal-off carriers under copying damage. Under mild dilution, its full ten-locus mechanism had an approximately 0.130 generation-eight advantage from continued rewriting, with positive intact effects through generations twelve and sixteen. [E22]

The same branch did not establish its stronger evolutionary claim. Renewal selection did not reliably outperform a permissive memory objective, and the tested mutated offspring fell below the prescribed robustness criterion. Consequently, the existence of a renewable engineered carrier and the evolutionary accessibility of a robust carrier require different explanations. The raw Wagner run directory referenced by these records is absent from the present checkout; this draft attributes the result to the retained log and audit, pending artifact recovery before public release.

Later interpreter-maintenance campaigns ask what happens when the regulatory machinery reading a carrier changes. The retained Architecture v1 closeout reports that switching off repair reduces distance-four retention by 22.7 percentage points in its strongest fresh arm. A crossed comparison between ordinary/repair-evolved topologies and repair on/off localizes the principal benefit to continuing protection. Yet that arm fails the complete comparative claim: its information advantage over the ordinary control has an interval spanning zero. The later dormant-start development evolves a nonzero repair trait, but its small repair-off effect and failed nomination leave robust endogenous maintenance unestablished. [E82]

The word repair has a precise and limited implementation here. A sampled mutation toward a new regulatory weight is multiplied by one minus an inherited repair strength; the model supplies this protective operation, charges a cost and can evolve its strength. There is no body-driven reconstruction of a lost reader from retained organization. These comparisons establish why interpreting machinery belongs in the reconstruction boundary, while leaving its active rebuilding as a further experiment. They are reported findings with missing original run trees, not additional raw-data confirmations. C.37 specifies the distinction. [E82]

#### D.5.3. Material replacement without form reconstruction

The dust-aggregate branch tested replacement at a different physical-model level. Grains could break apart, capture new material, and selectively retain incoming grains of the same type. Yet the challenged-renewal study did not recover the parent's larger-scale statistical form: every resolved active parent mean had negative restoration under both registered observers. Later composition studies retained narrower local selection effects without qualifying the proposed emergent-form outcome. [E23]

These comparisons show why the carrier and its reader must be identified. Neither changing components nor retaining selected ingredients alone establishes reconstruction of the relevant organization. Inheritance of acquired information requires a separate causal test that experimentally acquired history crosses a defined boundary and affects descendants; episodic hereditary continuity alone does not supply that evidence. Finite positive results establish the tested capacities and horizons; they do not imply indefinite continuation or an autonomous origin of the machinery.

### D.6. Cellular context, editable-lineage extensions and turnover

Global mean coupling remains even when local edges are cut. This outcome-informed comparison is neither a fresh-block replication nor a learned boundary policy, and establishes no universal coupling optimum. The cellular timing interventions in main-text section 7.1 use a different model and boundary. [E13]

#### D.7.3. Records, readers, preferences and approximate inference

Separate finite models motivated by the free energy principle (FEP) make a related distinction explicit. In a one-bit model, a hidden target X produces a noisy retained record R, which a separate noisy reader converts to observation Y. Maintenance changes the reader without changing record content. Random replacement of R leaves every permitted report policy at chance even with perfect reading; an intact R with an uninformative reader also leaves performance at chance. An information objective aimed at the stored bit can reward better reading of replacement noise without improving recovery of X. These are exact consequences of supplied channels and specified objectives, not evidence from the tissue simulations. [E52]

Repeated reads can reveal reader consistency but cannot, within that model, validate the record's relationship to its hidden target. Evidence from another grounded route is needed, with its reliability and independence explicitly supplied. In a three-record extension, two paid calibrations improve net task utility from **0.625 to 0.638** when their agreement determines whether to trust a later reading or purchase an independent reference. Committing the later resource choice before the second calibration removes its benefit. The improvement concerns expected correct reporting minus costs, not a percentage of information preserved. State-preference FEP and matched Bayesian planning select the same policy in this example; its value depends on the assumed persistence of the reading mechanism. [E52]

A numerical comparison asks whether richer approximate beliefs improve decisions under the same known model, objective, and ten-percent failure-risk limit. Small joint belief blocks reduce mean action error by **47.49%** relative to independent beliefs across the selected fixtures. Yet removing the blocks' correlations slightly improves the risk-prediction metric used in the proposed mechanism test. The decision criterion passes, the correlation criterion fails, and the overall qualification fails; conditional larger-scale runs are skipped. Exact inference is faster in every completed matched comparison. Lower aggregate error also coexists with some new risk violations in cases where the simpler approximation was within the limit. [E54]

Later mathematical bounds support a constructive correction without supplying exact answers to the policy. A rule using the known model and available risk intervals relaxes a restrictive action cap while guaranteeing no worse objective and compliance with the declared model-risk limit. On retained beliefs it improves **3,865 of 13,056 decisions**, leaving the others unchanged. At the primary checkpoint, mean regret falls **25.95% for independent beliefs** and **13.26% for grouped beliefs**. These are reductions in a specified objective loss relative to the exact feasible optimum, not failure-probability reductions. The same actions arise from a simpler physical-slope control; the tighter analysis improves guarantees without adding action benefit there. This is a derivation and saved-belief evaluation, not a fresh prospective inference cohort or a rescue of the original mechanism gate. [E54]

The finite preference studies expose a related modeling choice. Holding the physical task and preferred target/report/cost distribution fixed does not uniquely fix preferences over hidden states. An additional preference for literal reader-target agreement can favor costly maintenance even when an inverted signal is already usable through a decoder. A separate energy model grounds continued operation in available resources, while leaving tolerated risk, reference policy, and preference strength specified by the modeler. Matched Bayesian planning shares the resulting choices. These are constructive distinctions among declared objectives, not evidence that minimizing a score explains the origin of what a system values. [E53]

#### D.7.4. Learned sensor interpretation and decision value

A later finite task asks whether the interpretation itself can be learned. An agent calibrates two noisy binary sensors against known inputs, then answers new hidden binary questions or buys a perfect reference. Wrong reports cost 1, references 0.1, and calibration pairs 0.001; full cost includes acquisition and is averaged over 100 deployment tasks. The supplied alternatives are independent sensor errors and a joint error distribution. With 64 calibration pairs, evidence-based model revision reduces cost by **12.69%** against retaining independence in the shared-error fixture. Under partial dependence, however, the same revision improves predictive log loss while worsening task cost: additional checks cost more than the avoided errors. Model adequacy and useful action are distinct endpoints. [E58]

Adaptive acquisition subsequently lets the agent decide when to stop buying calibration. A prospective evaluation of the unchanged controller across **125 equally weighted new error laws** gives mean full cost **0.072615**, compared with **0.1** for always purchasing the reference: a **27.38% reduction**. It benefits in **86 of 89 environments where improvement is possible for an evaluator knowing the error law**. The complete-coverage criterion fails. All 36 environments where always referencing is already optimal also incur excess cost and remain in the mean, giving 86 better and 39 worse cases overall. These are exact expectations under specified laws, not 125 independently sampled experiments. [E58]

An outcome-informed follow-up fixes the same calibration histories and uses the learned joint component directly at deployment. On the primary matched schedule, mean cost falls to **0.072094**, a further **0.72% reduction relative to the original learned controller**; beneficial coverage becomes **88/89**. The change worsens 48 of the 125 environments relative to that controller and slightly worsens the older nine-environment mean. The new population was already inspected, so this mechanism comparison is not fresh validation of an improved agent. The supplied joint family and unchanged acquisition rule remain part of the explanation. [E59]

Further updating also requires a functional test. On the earlier nine-environment panel, retaining a second deployment warning and its answer when purchased raises mean full cost from **0.076088 to 0.076133** relative to holding the first-update state. All three registered improvement comparisons fail. Fewer checks save resources, but added mistakes cost more; this bounded two-update result does not establish a general harm from learning. [E60]

These models specify conditions for useful information: a relation to the task, interpretable reading, evidence that transfers, and an opportunity to affect action. In the sensor studies, variational free energy supplies exact posterior inference and model evidence; acquisition minimizes expected future task loss plus actual costs. This is also conventional Bayesian inference and planning, with supplied model families, reference access, preferences, and resources. No distinctive FEP advantage, autonomous maintenance, or origin of goals follows, and these later studies do not reverse E54's failed mechanism criterion. Appendices C.20/C.22 state the separate contracts and evaluation units.

#### D.7.5. Scope of the information-theoretic interpretation

The sensor-model sequence changes several distinct parts of the problem. Factor revision learns whether a supplied joint representation better explains paired calibration; adaptive calibration decides whether to buy another known-input observation; new-law evaluation tests the unchanged policy; joint deployment changes how the same saved evidence is used. Their endpoints are future task cost, not the amount of record information retained or a measured biological fitness. The original two-fixture acquisition-efficiency failure remains even though the later nine-law panel gives a mean efficiency gain. [E58]

The 125-law comparison retains every environment, including 36 where always referencing is already optimal. Its known-law evaluator is an information bound that pays the same reference price, not a deployable learner with free labels. The joint-deployment benefit is measured afterward on that known grid. Posterior averaging remains optimal relative to its own mixture belief at every saved history; an equally weighted external population can favor another rule. The mean joint improvement and its 48 harms against matched MAP should therefore remain together. [E59]

Further evidence has distinct value depending on its timing, use and costs. Completing calibration to 64 worsens mean cost in all five known failure/worst-case environments examined by the attribution study, though some individual conditional histories benefit. Retaining a second deployment observation reduces checks but increases more expensive errors on the nine-law panel. Neither result rules out a differently optimized learning policy. The tested models supply their goals and hypothesis vocabulary; they show conditions for useful adaptation, without explaining the spontaneous origin or inherited maintenance of those resources. [E59]; [E60]

Epiplexity offers a related motivation for declaring computational resources, but our recognition, preference, and forecast endpoints are not measurements of epiplexity. [Finzi et al., 2026, v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03220v2)

### D.9. Finite access maps, response transfer and repair selection

#### D.9.1. A retained record changes reachable destinations

#### D.9.2. Predicting a related embodiment from intervention responses

Can a description of a form's responses predict what happens when its embodiment changes? Each forecast concerned changes in qualified own/foreign-founder agreement after a joint body-and-record intervention. The predictor received actual source-body responses to that intervention, but no target-body outcomes. E003 changed local neighborhood coupling. Its learned response map beat appearance-based and intervention-only predictors but performed worse than directly using the source response: root-mean-square error (RMSE) **1.115 versus 1.005 points**. The complete criterion failed. E008 instead kept degree four and total coupling one while replacing exactly 128 of 512 undirected connections with longer links. On 32 fresh blocks containing 128 founders, its learned map had RMSE **1.085**, against **2.472** for direct transfer and **1.632** for the appearance predictor. All four required comparisons passed; the map received no graph-specific features. [E34]

#### D.9.3. Prediction and the choice of repair

A forecast becomes more useful if it helps choose an intervention before observing its outcome. E009 applied the frozen E008 map to a new task: after erasing 192 of 256 packet entries, choose which 64 to restore from an external clean backup. Every option restored the same number of exact values. Map-guided selection improved specificity over strong-value and random choices, but did not beat choosing from the measured source-body responses: **-0.006 points [-0.043, 0.029]**. The primary conjunction failed. Prediction success had not established added decision value. [E35]

Two subsequent tests also failed their full criteria. E010 found that restoring the weakest values did not outperform strong or random restoration. E011 crossed the chosen sites with applied magnitudes: stronger expression improved the weak-site repair, but did not establish its superiority over random sites at the same magnitude. These results separate where distinguishing signs occur from whether restoring them improves the declared outcome. They do not support a privileged weak-entry repair rule. [E35]

E012 then fitted two repair-specific predictors on archived E009 cases, explicitly reused as development data. A native predictor received source responses and packet geometry; a second added six features of the target's connectivity. Choices were frozen before fresh target outcomes on 64 blocks and 256 founders. The graph-informed choice recovered **1.192 specificity points [1.052, 1.339]** relative to no repair. Its advantage over the otherwise native-feature model was **0.044 [0.002, 0.087]**, and over direct source-based choice **0.141 [0.081, 0.203]**. All six registered criteria passed. [E36]

Here specificity is own-founder Q minus the mean Q for the other three founders under the same bias; recovery is its change from the unrepaired condition. E009 used one foreign reference instead, so its utility numbers are not directly interchangeable. E012's graph increment is small, with its lower interval close to zero. The native fitted model already outperformed source-based choice by 0.097 points, showing that much of the gain was available from task-specific fitting. No wrong-graph comparator was tested in E012 itself. [E35]; [E36]

E013 repeats the frozen predictors and transformations on 128 fresh blocks and 512 founders, with choices sealed before target outcomes. All seven declared gates pass. Graph-informed specificity recovery is **1.287 points [1.180, 1.399]**, with advantages of **0.068 [0.041, 0.098]** over native measurements and **0.121 [0.087, 0.156]** over source-based choice. The same graph predictor supplied with independently generated, structurally matched wrong wiring performs worse by **0.102 [0.067, 0.139] points**. Actual target wiring remains fixed. This comparison isolates a benefit of the correct graph description within the existing predictor, beyond giving one model additional feature capacity. [E42]

The decision studies use supplied source probes, observer prototypes, and a clean backup. Equal restoration budgets do not equalize every sensing or modeling cost, and the procedure repairs a packet for one fresh reading rather than renewing it through generations. E013 confirms a small, actionable benefit from correct embodiment information in this apparatus; it does not establish graph necessity for every repair, an optimal selector, or general transfer across substrates. Appendices C.12/C.16 specify the prediction boundaries and fresh confirmation.
