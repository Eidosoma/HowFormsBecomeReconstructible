## 1. Forecasting: an important change from the current manuscript

The current §7.2 correctly describes an earlier selected-record pilot as unresolved. There are now **two later fresh-cohort confirmations**; the old failure should remain, but it should no longer stand as the latest forecasting status.

The first fresh study used 108 eligible acquisition blocks, 432 selected packets and 3,456 continuation outcomes. The frozen successor diagnostic improved Brier loss over TT008 and an eight-read direct probe in both regimes. Candidate Brier losses were **0.128702 matched** and **0.085158 independent**, compared with **0.142633/0.097677 for TT008** and **0.135608/0.089320 for direct probing**. All four registered advantages passed. This study used 64 diagnostic reads versus eight for the direct rival and zero additional for TT008.

The subsequent **equal-read** study addresses that resource objection: 80 fresh eligible blocks, 319 selected packets and 2,552 continuation outcomes, with all five fixed forecasts sealed across the cohort before any future started. Successor32, direct64 and pooled32 each require **64 native reads per packet/regime**.

| Noise regime | Rival | Brier improvement from successor32 | Individual 95% interval |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Matched | Direct64 | +0.010267 | [0.006008, 0.014527] |
| Matched | Pooled32 | +0.009842 | [0.005831, 0.013853] |
| Independent | Direct64 | +0.002850 | [0.000406, 0.005294] |
| Independent | Pooled32 | +0.002810 | [0.000527, 0.005092] |

All four frozen primary tests pass. These are individual block-t intervals used in the specified joint rule, not simultaneous 95% intervals. The independent-noise comparison with the cheaper eight-read probe remains unresolved. Equal native-read counts do not establish the best use of every possible computational budget; acquisition, training and other computation remain separate costs.

**Plain-language interpretation:** measuring what a record produces after a rebuilding step can predict later reconstruction better than repeatedly measuring its current performance, even when the compared diagnostics receive the same number of native reads.

The cohort deliberately selects records from both present-expression classes; this is not an all-record population result. It supports a useful model-informed forecast, **not a distinctive RDT law or a successful repair scheduler**. Subsequent injury-transfer studies remain small, exposed development panels. The scaled-probe study has five of six favorable directions, with all six intervals crossing zero. The later matched-recovery study favors successor measurement under matched noise but loses to quadratic direct probing under independent noise. Transport to altered injuries/regimes is still open.

The latest saved-data recovery diagnostic adds a useful contrast. Among historically failing packets whose new writer probe fails, renewal beats a matched reread by **25.79 points under matched noise** and **6.12 under independent noise**. Rare failed probes of historically passing packets instead show substantial harm. These are conditional pooled probe events nested within eight exposed founders, not fresh population estimates. A simple two-state transition model loses to the existing successor forecast in both regimes. A failed read alone therefore does not identify the right corrective action.

## 2. Repair: when writing happens, and what information the apparatus supplies

### The reset qualification should accompany any new positive claim

TT031/TT032 show that the earlier correction procedure supplied substantial spatial organization through its reset. With everything else matched, own-body recognition under corrected versus uniform reset changes from **73.65% to 34.47% in the common family**, and **77.79% to 31.45% in the residual family**. Reversing the reset gives approximately 25%. Under uniform reset, own versus foreign bodies still contributes **6.15 and 6.67 points**, respectively.

This does not erase a body contribution. It changes its interpretation: the earlier large outcome is jointly supported by the surviving body and a structured supplied reset. Uniform-reset results also depend strongly on which target-relative region survives. The new multiscale lane should therefore test spatial initial conditions and hidden template information explicitly.

### Two fresh timing confirmations

TT036 confirms that stopping at 100 writing updates improves both endpoints relative to continuous writing to 1000: **+7.73 body points** and **+9.38 fresh-recipient points**, on 64 fresh blocks. Against no writing, however, the stopped arm improves the record while harming the current body. The comparator matters.

TT039 asks the stronger joint question against preservation, after allowing every body to continue to update 1900. With completely replaced packets and uniform reset, late writing during updates 901–1000 gives:

| Original-target recognition credit | Preserve replaced packet | Late writing | Difference [pointwise 95% interval] |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Body at update 1900 | 39.16% | 43.21% | +4.0527 [3.2630, 4.8425] |
| Fresh recipient | 25.00% | 39.44% | +14.4379 [13.6252, 15.2505] |

Both registered co-primary contrasts pass on **64 fresh blocks** under the declared approximate paired-block t rule. Conservative distribution-free sensitivity bounds do not establish a positive effect. These percentages are **fractional recognition credit**, not binary lineage success. The comparator preserves a **completely replaced/damaged packet**, not the original intact record.

The later body advantage mainly reflects less deterioration than preservation, not continuing improvement in every body. Individual harms remain, and negative-half retention reverses both mean benefits. The four-block TT040 follow-up also finds that the same late policy harms intact records and trades a recipient benefit against body harm with half-mixed packets. No serial capacity or autonomous timing policy is established.

**Plain-language interpretation:** leaving writing on continuously can damage useful organization. A restricted writing period can capture something useful while preserving more of the body, but the appropriate policy depends on what was damaged.

## 3. Forms: writer history and compatible sign placement

**Forms E098** changes only noise pairing between the two cue episodes supplying one subsequent rewrite, holding marginal noise, birth, injury, capture and terminal readers matched. On **32 fresh source blocks**, independent writer noise raises pruned-mixture success from **55.71% to 60.99%**: **+5.273 points [2.734, 7.812]**, adjusted 97.5% interval. Its pruning interaction is **+5.469 [2.539, 8.398]**. Both primary requirements pass; 161 gains and 53 harms remain among 2,048 dependent mixture comparisons.

This isolates a useful writer-pairing dependency beyond comparing whole rebuilding regimes. It does not establish exclusive mediation by the fallback capture rule: E099 retains the noise benefit with ordinary selected capture while its focal same-body capture comparison remains unresolved.

**Forms E102** then transfers selected donor signs and magnitudes into known records. Combined donor success is **56.934%**, versus **53.125% original** and **49.658% with the selected signs reshuffled**. Donor placement beats the shuffle by **7.275 points [5.762, 8.887]**, and the original by **3.809 [2.539, 5.127]**. The controls match support, magnitudes, denominators and sign counts before/after the declared injuries. This is new-reader evidence on exposed histories, not another fresh-source confirmation.

The tighter loop/local-weight follow-up is less decisive. **E106 fails to repeat E105's primary mixture advantage:** both sign arrangements achieve 56.89%, difference **0.00 [−1.03, 1.02]**. A secondary native-record effect remains. Thus the broad compatible-placement finding is supported, but a specific general cycle-level causal explanation is not yet established.

**Plain-language interpretation:** both the circumstances in which a record is written and where its positive/negative connections lie can matter. Matching aggregate connection counts is insufficient; the latest tighter control also prevents us from attributing everything to one structural property.

## 4. Hopfield: coordinated actions help, broader competence remains open

The lane has progressed from H039 to H089. Three contrasts deserve selection rather than importing the entire chronology.

**H079:** on 192 fresh networks, delayed feedback with repeated settling solves **428/768 tasks (55.73%)**; always-on, always-off and a single delayed sweep each solve zero complete tasks. The timing/iteration contrasts qualify, while the separate **75% capability criterion fails**. Strong quadratic and nearest-history controls solve all tasks. Waiting longer under unchanged dynamics cannot rescue most failures because they are already fixed.

**H084:** one target-free coordinated two-bit move followed by ordinary settling increases complete-task success from **368/768 (47.92%) to 436/768 (56.77%)** on another 192 fresh networks. Seventeen networks improve, none worsen and 175 tie: the registered exact directional test passes. There are 68 task gains and no primary task losses, but 400 final bit losses. The separate conservative lower bound on the population-mean gain does **not** qualify a positive mean effect. The method evaluates 1,128 candidate pairs; it is not an equal-cost efficiency result or an inherited-function confirmation.

This supplies a concrete action-scale lesson: a joint move can escape a state where ordinary single-bit settling cannot. It does not demonstrate an endogenous multiscale sensor or biological collective intelligence.

The negative controls are equally useful. **H081** reduces newly wrong bits by 63.37%, yet complete tasks decline **50.00% to 32.81%** because useful repairs are also suppressed. **H089** attempts unseen context/query combinations through two handoffs, but the neural system already fails every complete trained task at generation zero and every held-out task later. An explicitly structured parity learner succeeds. Broader inherited competence remains unestablished; the immediate bottleneck includes initial task retrieval, not only inheritance.

## 5. Cellular: the relevant correction can span state components

P34L localizes a previously successful ion substitution to the incoming cell. P34N supplies the missing known-sequence connection-cut control, but **ion-only substitution fails in a new prospective sequence**, even when applied to all three cells.

P34F completes a factorial on that known failure: RNA alone and ions alone fail; their combined substitution restores both retention and transmission. Cutting boundary coupling removes successful transmission. This is conditional combined sufficiency, not proof of molecular synergy or universal necessity.

P34V prospectively repeats the combined intervention on the next sequence: **combined rescue succeeds, the matched untreated control fails, and cutting the connection abolishes transmission**. It adds one new sequence in the same historical-source background. Numerical resolutions are verification checks, not independent biological replicates. The variables evolve after the one-time substitution; this remains a supplied intervention, not autonomous repair.

**Implication for our discussion:** the program supports electrically relevant state embedded in a coupled regulatory system, not a claim that all relevant pattern information is exclusively bioelectric. Location, RNA/ion context and communication matter. The publication-to-source-model correspondence limitation remains.

P34W's latest local document is a launch record, with no completed outcome available in this checkout or its accessible storage. Do not call it currently running merely because that older document says so, and do not assume its factorial result.

## 6. FEP: paying for evidence and choosing the endpoint

The larger **planned-recovery comparison** is complete on 864 paired blocks and 21,600 paired episodes. Recovery cost is **0.0990086**, versus **0.0993524** for the previous hybrid. The absolute interval lies below always-buy cost 0.1, but the paired difference **−0.0003438 [−0.0009951, +0.0003075]** leaves improvement over the hybrid unresolved. Stable-world cost increases. The joint requirement fails; more planning has not earned replacement of the baseline.

The latest **exact checking-tradeoff calculation** has a positive result in a deliberately simpler model: one possible hidden reversal, a correct supplied prior and perfect purchased feedback. An 18-purchase stable-episode schedule costs **0.018** versus **0.05** for the hybrid analogue, while recovery cost is **0.0724753 versus 0.0739649**. All fees are included: 64% lower stable cost and 2.014% lower recovery cost. Eighteen is the smallest feasible integer budget within the declared deterministic schedule class, not a universal minimum.

A second finding is especially relevant to the error question: the full-horizon Bayes optimum has better total expected cost but recovery cost **0.101283**, worse than always buying. Optimizing a whole episode and minimizing a recovery-window score can favor different actions. This is endpoint alignment in a specified model, not evidence against Bayesian inference or proof of a uniquely FEP mechanism.

## 7. L20/L22: the live-feedback pilot did not repeat

The latest **D193 four-history panel** is now complete and separately audited locally. It retains all 64 records from exposed histories 319100–319103, two families, reciprocal noise roles and two fresh-reader futures. This is one shared L20/L22 result, not two independent replications.

Own-state-responsive feedback versus a prerecorded sister trajectory gives:

| Family | Live feedback recognition | Recorded feedback recognition | Difference |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Common | 63.1836% | 63.1836% | 0.0000 points |
| Residual | 91.7969% | 92.1875% | −0.3906 points |

There are no focal recognition gains; two residual records are harmed. This fails to reproduce the excluded pilot's small positive lead. Both writers still observe their own recipient, so the experiment tests recipient-contingent feedback rather than body presence versus absence. The prerecorded trajectory contains 1,001 fields, whereas the separate snapshot baseline contains one; information and generation costs are not matched.

**Implication:** retain the active-body evidence already established, but do not claim that continuing live feedback necessarily contributes something a supplied recording cannot. Useful record-specific recipient matching also remains a planned policy test, not a demonstrated outcome.

For the next manuscript update, prioritize **the equal-read forecast, TT039 with TT032's reset qualification, forms E098/E102, and a compact H079/H084 comparison**. Update cellular and FEP supporting results and retain D193's negative comparison. Do not add every intermediate campaign as another main-text finding.

The multiscale lane should use these lessons before freezing its first scientific protocol:

1. Account for spatial information in reset fields, masks, shared templates and initial conditions; include uniform/foreign controls.
2. Separate the scale at which a discrepancy is sensed from the scale of the allowed action. Compare coordinated actions with budgeted simpler actions, not only with doing nothing.
3. Measure present-body, outgoing-record and later-recipient consequences separately. Reduced local error or current failure alone is an insufficient action rule.
4. Include preservation, bounded early/late writing and continuation after writing stops. Do not infer a useful detector from a successful externally fixed clock.
5. Freeze any learned selector before fresh outcomes, charge sensing and action costs, and test changed injuries and communication conditions separately from development.
