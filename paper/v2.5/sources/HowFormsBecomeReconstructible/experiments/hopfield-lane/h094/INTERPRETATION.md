# H094 — context-transition protection restores capability, with copying tied

Completed **27 October 2026 at 16:09:59 UTC**. Independent closeout at 16:35 UTC
passes. Briefly holding the network's actual internal context during the first
query sweep raises unseen-task success after two handoffs from **40/64 to
64/64**. Copying and the stronger native controls also reach **64/64**. The
result supports H093's context-transition diagnosis and establishes retained
capability in this experiment; it supplies no strict reconstruction advantage
over those comparators.

The cohort contains **sixteen independent networks with four related births
each**, not 64 independent networks. It has already informed development.
Unseen query combinations are held out of teaching; these are not fresh
networks or population qualification. H093's baseline is reused from its
sealed run.

## What changed

H094 makes one fixed scheduling change: skip updates of context sites 0–31
during the first query sweep, then release them for all later query and
continuation sweeps. The held values are the actual post-gap values, including
any errors. No earlier snapshot, true-context resupply or extra state buffer
is used. The energy formula, writer, eight-response observation limit and
other timing remain fixed. Descendants fit their actual protected parent's
outputs, so their learned records can differ from H093's descendants.

Every neural comparator receives the same schedule, including independent
and same-parent copying. Native controls retain their declared observation
and learning apparatus. The hold skips 32 coordinate evaluations per query;
it adds no inherited coefficients. This is supplied scheduling apparatus, and
the experiment does not claim equal arithmetic costs across methods.

G0 uses the pair reader; G1/G2 use an ordinary recipient after pair-assisted
parents. Query success requires at least 44/48 correct response bits, and task
success requires all eight queries in the panel. Every protected primary
answer is also exact: **512/512 queries and 24,576/24,576 bits per row/panel**.

G0 unseen gains are the four births of one network. G2 unseen gains span six
networks: **24 task gains, 98 successful-query gains and 1,897 bit gains**, with
no losses. Exact-query gains are 100 because two H093 queries passed the
tolerance while retaining incorrect bits. G2 taught tasks gain 23 across six
networks, with no losses.

Both independent and same-parent copying reach **64/64 tasks and 512/512
queries** on both G2 panels. The quadratic, nearest-history and parity native
learners do likewise, independently and from the same parent packet. All
paired task, query and bit contrasts against these eight comparators are
zero. The two linear learners remain at zero complete tasks; beating those
weaker controls does not pass the joint comparator screen.

All **256 G2 same-parent native packets** independently match reconstruction's
packets. Across **576 unique neural reads**, the response-pair step makes
**zero moves**. G2 ordinary recipients therefore show no inherited benefit
from pair-assisted rather than ordinary parents.

## Saved traces explain the improvement

Independent closeout recounts the declared transition diagnostics directly
from saved traces and completion packets. It adds no dynamics or fits.

At G0, own unseen context is correct in **512/512 queries** before the query in
both conditions. H093 retains it in 423 after the first sweep and 494 at the
recipient endpoint. H094 retains it in **512 at both stages**, even after
context is released. Donor context similarly improves from 408 after the first
sweep and 496 at the endpoint to **512 at both stages**. Founder preparation
and gap traces match H093 exactly.

The later generations show why holding context is distinct from supplying
the answer. At **each of G1 and G2**, eight primary unseen queries lose context
in the gap. The first-sweep hold preserves those wrong values: **504/512**
contexts are correct both before and immediately after that sweep. All eight
recover after release, leaving **512/512** correct by query sweep eight and at
the recipient endpoint. The taught panel has the same stage counts.

All protected primary learned completions and retrieved responses are exact
at G0, G1 and G2. Thus the experiment removes both final retrieval errors and
the downstream learned-answer errors observed in H093. Because the intervention
is applied at every generation, this is a joint trajectory result; it does
not separately estimate the contribution of repaired parental teaching versus
protection at the child's own read. No residual primary error remains to
motivate another predictor or settling-time search on this task.

H093's original energy-repair finding remains historical evidence. H094
changes the update schedule, not that energy formula. It does not prove that
all unwanted minima have disappeared or that a one-sweep hold will suffice
for other tasks, networks or perturbations.

## What this supports next

The fixed intervention is now a candidate for confirmation on fresh networks,
with all six verdicts and the equally equipped controls preserved. Success
here is evidence about a specific transition mechanism, not RDT forecasting
performance or a general advantage of reconstruction over copying.

Richer inherited tasks remain a separate priority. The current stationary
task reaches the ceiling for reconstruction, copying and several native
learners, so adding more of the same cases cannot demonstrate strict task
superiority at that ceiling. A useful harder design would introduce a declared
state or task change between handoffs and measure adaptation and retention
separately. Copying must receive the same disturbance, observations and
available adaptation apparatus. Complexity alone is not a predicted advantage;
the study needs a concrete mechanism by which retained function and copied
record can diverge. No H095 has been launched.
