# Leads from E033 while trajectory verification is pending

Later update: E033 passed independent replay at 19:48 UTC and artifact closeout

This exploratory review follows the user's “any leads?” question. At the
19:21 UTC status check on 27 September, simulation had finished and independent
trajectory replay was still running. The numerical findings below are provisional.
No successor experiment was launched and no scientific input was changed.

The strongest lead is **longer settling during renewal itself**, before the
body writes its outgoing record. E033 changes only terminal receiving, so it
cannot establish this benefit. The same-total-duration schedule (1024 settling
steps within 2048 total steps) produces the same scattered both-cue success
rates as delayed receiving with 2816 total steps: 347/512 at h8 and 188/512 at
h16. These are equal aggregate rates, not a claim of identical outputs.
Extra time without extra settling produces essentially no improvement.

A bounded next intervention could start with every saved h8 candidate packet,
replay one damaged renewal with original versus delayed settling, and write
with the unchanged preserve-strong/refresh-weak policy. Use identical damage,
birth/noise streams, 2048 read steps, immediate capture and the existing 0.5
cutoff. Test both outgoing records with the same terminal receiver and retain
both original and delayed terminal schedules as a crossed comparison. That
separates improved rebuilding from improved final expression. The unchanged
renewal branch should reproduce the saved E031 g9 boundary exactly. This is
a design lead, not a frozen protocol or executed intervention.

If that produces better outgoing records, the next test is a fresh cohort
using the fixed delay at every renewal, observed through eight and sixteen
boundaries. Compare the old and changed renewal schedules under common final
readers, preserving the original absolute-reliability endpoint separately
from any improvement contrast. A one-step success would not establish durable
serial maintenance.

The saved endpoint classification supports focusing on cue responsiveness.
Using the previously defined E032 classes, h16 scattered normalized outputs
under original versus same-duration delayed reading change as follows:

| Class | Original | Delayed, same total duration |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Both cues correct | 114 | 188 |
| Both fully reversed | 14 | 19 |
| At least one output strongly same-signed across opposing cues | 333 | 247 |
| Other failure | 51 | 58 |

Of the 333 originally locked-output cases, 79 become correct. Three other
failures also become correct, while eight originally correct cases fail,
giving a net gain of 74. Improvement is therefore not universal. At h8,
113 originally locked cases become correct; 118 total failures recover and
15 original successes fail. These are descriptive paired counts, without a
new confirmatory hypothesis test.

A second, more speculative lead is a writer that emphasizes activity changing
between cues and suppresses activity shared by both cues. The current capture
averages coactivity over cues, so cue-independent activity can enter the
record. E032/E033 measure endpoint bias and do not establish that this writing
mechanism causes the remaining failures. A fixed, body-only contrast rule
would need its own controlled intervention, without access to original packet
values or desired output labels. Defer changing the writer until the simpler
renewal-timing intervention has been assessed.

The endpoint review retains all 512 lineages, all 16 panels and all three
injuries for the own-packet arm. E032's vector and independent scalar classifiers
agree exactly; success labels match saved E033 cases and all corresponding
summary means. Source output hashes were checked before and after. This checks
the new descriptive arithmetic, not the trajectories whose audit is pending.
