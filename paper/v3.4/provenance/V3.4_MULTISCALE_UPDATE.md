| E168 / C.123 | E2E007 | Weakening record influence under record injury improves historical specificity and fresh-recipient reconstruction. | Under transient body injury it instead lowers current patch agreement; stronger reading also has a small terminal cost. Matching is approximate and pooled. |

All six are development studies on exposed source cohorts. SM007, SM008, SM009 and E2E005 reuse AR005 blocks; E2E006 and E2E007 reuse AR006 blocks. Each has sixteen blocks. Related cases, body–pattern pairs and futures are nested within those blocks. SM007 has two futures; the others have one. These are not six fresh confirmations or precise record-conditioned probability estimates.

The strongest synthesis is that a mismatch signal needs an appropriate action. Increasing the influence of an intact record can help reading; increasing the influence of an injured record can propagate its error. The latter mechanism can affect an actual successor after the shared field is removed. A shared signal also does not automatically identify the member that needs help or the store that is wrong.

E2E007 provides the main new table, with body patch agreement, record specificity and a separate field-free recipient shown side by side. Its injury interaction is not described as a uniformly beneficial stronger-reading effect under body damage: the transient injury heals quickly without it. The nonpreferred-pattern subgroup and the pilot changes remain explicit in SI.
