## Causal interpretation and limits

The reciprocal intervention identifies the first post-fork incoming protein
value as a causal determinant of the neighbour route's success in these
specified backgrounds. Later draws alone cannot explain the P22 contrast:
both formerly failing tails support a qualified chain after the lower first
value is substituted, and the formerly successful tail fails with either
higher first value. This does not show that later replacements never matter
or that the same first value determines outcomes in arbitrary states.

Only three first-protein values and seven sequence combinations were tested.
The result does not locate a dose threshold, establish monotonicity, identify
a unique molecular memory carrier, or estimate a general success probability.
The sequences were previously studied; the hybrids are planned recombinations.
Both histories agree, providing no evidence here for a written-history
advantage. These finite, externally scheduled model experiments do not
establish autonomous reproduction, cell division or a biological cancer
treatment.

P25 closes the remaining reciprocal-swap lead from the P22 follow-up review.
The previously proposed next question is how short the successful early
pause can be, and how late it can start. A distinct follow-up could map the
neighbour route's first-protein boundary. Neither new protocol nor simulation
was registered or launched during this closeout.
