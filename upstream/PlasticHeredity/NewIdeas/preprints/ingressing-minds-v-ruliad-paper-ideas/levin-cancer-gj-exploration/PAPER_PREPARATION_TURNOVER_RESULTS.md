All **12 registered single-interval rescue contrasts are negative**. At fixed
later=100, lengthening the first interval to 300 or 1,000 does not rescue final
renewal. At fixed first=100, lengthening all later intervals to 300 or 1,000
also fails. On this sampled grid, renewal succeeds only when the first and
later intervals are both in {300, 1,000}. This establishes two constraints on
these schedules, not an exact threshold or a general monotonicity result.

Longer preparation nevertheless has a local effect. With first=300 or 1,000
and later=100, all founders produce EEE after replacement two: the first
newcomer survives that next challenge. Replacement three then yields EEM,
and replacement four yields MMM, which persists through the remaining
replacements and both isolated holds. Protecting the first newcomer through
one more replacement is therefore insufficient for complete renewal.

With first=100, the written and ordinary prefixes begin EEE. At later=100
or 300, replacement two yields MME and replacement three yields MMM. At
later=1,000, all three are already M at the second endpoint. The stock prefix
at first=100 begins MEE and reaches MMM at the second endpoint for all three
later intervals. These intermediate founder differences do not change the
common final-retention grid. P17 had separately shown that the actual written
and ordinary 100-unit newcomer retains E when isolated instead of undergoing
continued turnover. Its isolated retention does not ensure resilience during
the next neighbourhood change.

At later=300 with first=300 or 1,000, every replacement endpoint is EEE and
the final trio retains E, but some incoming cells fail the late-occupancy
criterion. In the written source, first=300 fails that criterion in windows
3, 4 and 6; first=1,000 fails it in windows 3 and 6. With later=1,000 and
first=300 or 1,000, all six windows qualify for all founders. Thus initial
preparation of 1,000 is not required for the stricter measure on this grid:
300 followed by 1,000 is sufficient.
