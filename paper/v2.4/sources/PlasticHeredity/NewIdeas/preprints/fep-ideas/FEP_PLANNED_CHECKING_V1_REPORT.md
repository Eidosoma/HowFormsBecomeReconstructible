# Planned checking v1: better predicted cost, without fewer purchases

Completed and verified **18 October 2026**, under the prospective
[specification](FEP_PLANNED_CHECKING_V1.md). This is a finite analytic comparison,
not a sampled autonomous-recovery experiment.

Anticipating the existing checking rule improves model-expected cost in **42 of
144 constructed contexts**, with 102 equal within tolerance and none worse.
It reduces expected purchases in **none**: 132 are equal and 12 increase.
Thirty of the 42 cost improvements are small, between **.000010251 and .000018295**
over the entire remaining window. The intended idea of fewer routine purchases
is therefore unsupported in these contexts. Better timing and, in some cases,
additional useful purchases explain the cost result.

This does not change the previous [paid-checking verdict](FEP_PAID_CHECKING_V1_REPORT.md):
sampled reversal cost improved **19.19%**, but its **.100231757** estimate and
**[.099320111, .101143404]** interval did not establish cheaper recovery than
always buying at .1. Stable cost increased **7.72%**. That joint success
criterion remains unmet; no new physical recovery result is claimed here.

## What changed

The 401-component structured generative model, priors, Bayesian/VFE inference,
free warnings, cost .1 per purchased correct answer, cost 1 per mistake and
four-step planning limit remain fixed. All policies begin each comparison with
the same posterior. The existing constraint permits at most one consecutive
task without purchased feedback.

Three policies are evaluated under their actual future behavior:

| Policy | Treatment of the checking rule |
| --- | --- |
| Unconstrained (`free`) | Finite-horizon cost optimum without the rule; a lower bound |
| Previous hybrid (`hybrid`) | Unconstrained CT4 proposal, overridden when a purchase is due |
| Planned checking (`clock`) | Includes the rule and its clock in every future branch |

The 144 contexts cross three calibration profiles, three prescribed evidence
prefixes, all four current warnings and tasks 97–100. Only even-numbered prefix
tasks contain purchased labels. Remaining horizons are 4, 3, 2 and 1. Using the
end of the episode ensures that the old CT4 policy's future horizon really
shrinks this way. These are externally constructed histories, not trajectories
generated by any compared policy, and have no assigned population frequency.

Costs below are **totals over the remaining window**, not per-task rates.
Every future purchase is charged. The prospective comparison tolerance is
1e-10; equalities and unfavorable purchase differences are retained.

## Separate learning from the checking clock

Buying a label both supplies information and resets the clock that determines
when another purchase becomes compulsory. For the same coherent continuation,
the future benefit separates exactly into

```text
clock benefit = W(b, clock=1) - W(b, clock=0)
information value = W(b, clock=0) - E_label[W(b_after_label, clock=0)]
answer score - purchase score = immediate risk - .1
                               + clock benefit + information value.
```

The first term measures the value of avoiding an immediately compulsory future
purchase. The second measures how much the new label helps future task decisions.
Both are nonnegative analytically. Clock benefit reaches **.089071044** in
these contexts. Retained-label information value exceeds tolerance in **24**
contexts and reaches **.029069960**; its minimum is −5.55e-17 from roundoff.
Erased-label information value is **exactly zero throughout**.

The decomposition concerns the current action scores, not a decomposition of
the overall improvement between controllers. Their future policies can differ
even when their present actions agree. Erasure also changes later decisions,
so a retained-minus-erased outcome difference is not a unique causal allocation.

The largest retained-label cost improvement occurs in
`contradictions-t99-c0-y0` (also tied by the warning-flipped context). This is
an explicitly selected extreme, not a typical effect. The hybrid answers now
and buys next: cost **.159651874**, expected purchases **1**. Planned checking
buys now: cost **.131406363**, expected purchases **1.129969729**. Its root clock
benefit is **.044701511**, and information value is **.023892125**. Buying sooner
and occasionally again costs more in fees but lowers predicted mistakes enough
to improve the total. With erased labels the same context improves only to
**.155298489**, with **1.082904146** expected purchases.

## Exact control: savings with no learning and the same purchase count

The separately specified known-law example has no unknown parameter. With
current warning 01, risk .08 and next-task mean risk .018:

| Policy | Two-task cost | Purchases |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Unconstrained | .098 | 0 |
| Hybrid: answer now, buy next | .180 | 1 |
| Planned: buy now, answer next | .118 | 1 |

The exact saving is **.062**, with zero information value and clock benefit
**.082**. Both agreement-warning controls show equal hybrid/planned cost
**1/9**, and all four constant-risk controls show equal cost **.11**. This
demonstrates a scheduling mechanism, not new empirical recovery or learning.

The checking cap still requires at least **floor(T/2)** purchases across T
tasks starting with clock zero: at least **50** in 100 tasks. Accounting for
the cap inside planning cannot remove that floor.

## FEP interpretation and the next decision

The FEP connection remains the same explicit generative model and its
previously qualified conditional variational-free-energy/Bayesian belief
updates. Actions acquire observations that can change those beliefs and improve
future decisions. This calculation makes the actual acquisition constraint part
of planning and distinguishes information value from a scheduling benefit.

The action objective is still expected task cost, with purchased feedback fully
priced. Its information term is task value of information, not Shannon mutual
information or a newly derived canonical expected-free-energy objective. The
ordinary Bayesian explanation is compatible with the project's FEP foundations.
There is no structure discovery or unique-FEP advantage claimed here.

The next useful test is **autonomous physical recovery with the fixed planned
controller**, compared with its paid-checking predecessor and always buying,
on a fresh prospectively specified paired cohort. Keep stable and non-reversal
controls, every onset, full-episode costs, purchase counts and acquisition/
interpretation/economic diagnostics. Test whether it works economically rather
than assuming it buys less. The previous structured controller can remain an
additional reference if specified before that cohort.

That sampled experiment is **not launched by this analytic study**. The
finite-horizon guarantee is internal to the model and terminal windows; it
does not establish dominance under model misspecification or during 100-task
receding-horizon operation. No new cohort, gap search, retuning, manuscript
integration or sister-project write occurred.
