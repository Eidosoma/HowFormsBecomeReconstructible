### 4.1. Surviving organization repairs a transferable record

The **reciprocal-repair experiment** (D073) compares matched damaged records and half-body fission. Both arms write from recovering activity; one keeps the damaged reader fixed, while the other lets newly written values feed back into recovery. Only the final record enters fresh-body assays. Neither the fragment nor its working reference accompanies it. [E09]

Live feedback raises recognition from **88.65% to 90.67%**, an increment of **2.03 points [0.85, 3.59]** across sixteen tissue blocks. A separate **arrangement intervention** (D074) preserves or scrambles the surviving body while matching both damaged records and body-value distributions. Preserving arrangement improves later own-message recognition by **1.78 points [1.10, 2.51]** across sixteen further blocks. Each interval has nominal 98.33% coverage within a three-contrast family; fresh readouts are nested within tissues. [E09]; [E10]

These interventions identify surviving organization as a contributor to record usefulness elsewhere. The stronger donor-redirection criterion fails its required consistency across tissues. Within those experiments, arrangement could supply corrective content or help exploit content still present in the damaged record. SI C.1–C.2 specifies the four half-plane messages and the matched controls.

A later **identical-record test** removes the within-pair message distinction from the incoming record while retaining it in surviving bodies. Ordinary rewriting leaves recognition at **50.00%**. Capturing the injured body's activity in the existing reference before repair yields a development lead (D184–D186); the reference then participates in body dynamics and writing. Only the newly written record reaches a fresh recipient. [E90]; [E91]

The **fresh early-capture confirmation** (TT021) tests this intervention on **64 new tissue blocks and all 1,024 enrolled records**. Captured-body recognition reaches **75.66% versus 50.00%** for ordinary repair: **+25.66 points**, with a registered one-sided 99% lower bound **+22.16**, exceeding the five-point margin. Switching from the own body to its partner reduces intended-message recognition to **24.24%**, while partner-message recognition becomes **75.66%**. The common incoming record is unchanged. This identifies message-specific information supplied by the surviving body and transferred through the outgoing record. Recognition averages four receiving draws with fractional tie credit; tissue blocks are the independent units. [E92]

The result does not establish that continual memory updating is necessary. A protected snapshot reaches **75.15%**; updating adds only **0.51 points** and fails its two-point requirement. With the body-reading stream held fixed, captured writing exceeds matched averaging by **5.91 points**, a separately qualified effect. The predicted harm to individually informative records is not confirmed. The five claims retain their approximate, family-adjusted inference; SI C.46 also reports conservative sensitivity bounds and the operational amendment that completed two unstarted blocks after a storage halt. D074's earlier failed criterion remains unchanged. [E92]

Serial capture is a different test: repeated capture incorporates injury fill and remains below preservation even after correcting for the known fill (SI C.51). In the **fresh record-corruption test** (TT028), body-based writing raises recognition after complete packet replacement from **25.00% to 77.61%** across 64 new blocks. However, the practical partial-corruption contrast misses its declared margin, and writing intact records lowers recognition from **89.24% to 85.12%**. Its supplied mask, fill correction and reset are part of the intervention. [E97]; [E100]

The **reset ablation** (TT032) shows why those supplied conditions matter. On the 64 now-exposed source blocks with complete replacement, changing only the spatial memory offset reduces own-body recognition from **73.65% to 34.47%** in the common family and **77.79% to 31.45%** in the residual family. Under uniform reset, own bodies still outperform foreign bodies by **6.15 and 6.67 points**, but the effect depends strongly on which target-relative half survives. Thus the large correction outcome has a substantial supplied spatial contribution; the remaining body effect is real within this apparatus but is not a general, reset-independent reconstruction mechanism. This follow-up does not retrospectively decompose TT021's separate early-capture effect (SI C.59). [E105]

Can writing help the body as well as its record under this less informative reset? The **fresh writing-window test** (TT039) uses uniform reset, complete packet replacement, and 64 new blocks. All bodies continue to update 1900; writing occurs early, late, throughout the first 1000 updates, or never. In the primary residual-family comparison, late writing during updates 901–1000 raises body recognition from **39.16% to 43.21%** and fresh-recipient recognition from **25.00% to 39.44%**. The paired changes are **+4.05 [3.26, 4.84]** and **+14.44 [13.63, 15.25] points**, respectively. Both frozen co-primary tests pass under the approximate block-t rule; the intervals shown are pointwise two-sided 95%. Conservative distribution-free sensitivity bounds do not establish positivity. [E106]

### 4.2. Writer placement and reader history

The **writer-placement test** (D135) and its fresh-founder confirmation (D140) begin paired chains from identical records. A shared guide, fitted from sixteen separate development records, correlates each packet against two supplied family templates across all 256 circular translations. It inverse-translates the incoming field itself; it neither replaces nor blends its values with template pixels. The 4096-byte guide belongs to the apparatus, outside the inherited 2048-byte record. [E29]

After a fresh body reads for 300 updates, the packet and guide become unavailable as direct writing inputs. Activity, internal reference and a moving average remain. A new theta field starts at zero and is updated for 1000 steps. One arm applies it at corresponding sites; the other uses one uniformly sampled nonidentity circular translation, fixed during that generation. The successor record is derived from the final reference, $\Pi_{0.3}[\tanh(R_{\rm final})]$, mean-centered and normalized to standard deviation 0.3. Final theta itself is not transmitted. Three rewrites precede a fourth, non-writing read (Figure 9; SI C.8).

The primary endpoint is **qualified preference**:

\[
P_q(V)=\frac{100}{256}\sum_i\mathbf1\{|V_i|>0.2\}(u_i-a_i),
\]

where u and a indicate sign agreement with the inherited and alternative targets. All sites remain in the denominator; the targets disagree at 128 of 256 sites, so scores range from **−50 to +50 points**. A change can reflect signs, amplitude qualification, or both.

Across **eight new founder blocks**, aligned feedback improves terminal preference by **2.89 points [1.72, 4.17]**, a 95% whole-block interval. A secondary sign-only readout gives **3.11 points [1.92, 4.39]**, with an unadjusted pointwise interval. The direction survives threshold removal, without assigning separate causal shares to signs and amplitudes. The pooled fresh-cohort comparison with protected copying remains unresolved. [E29]

### 5.3. Signs, magnitudes and timing

For ideal pure-contrast capture, $w_{ij}=d_i d_j$ with $d_i=(x_i^+-x_i^-)/2$. Nonzero edge signs factor as $s_i s_j$, where $s_i=\operatorname{sign}(d_i)$; cycle sign products are positive. Reorienting activity by s converts normalized coupling into a positive weighted average, canceling the receiving cell's absolute contrast magnitude. Structural balance is established signed-network mathematics, not a new theorem here. It also does not guarantee success: connectivity, ports, finite reading time and writing history remain consequential; fallback and preservation mixtures need not be balanced. SI C.23 gives the derivation. [Altafini, 2013](https://doi.org/10.1109/TAC.2012.2224251)

**Record-feature interventions** (E054) probe all retained E051 records in fresh common readers without further writing. Permuting magnitudes within sign classes reduces matched-history success from **86.52% to 12.11%**, but independent-history success from **95.90% to 94.34%**. A post hoc whole-block paired interaction is **72.85 points [68.55, 76.95]**, a pointwise 95% interval over 32 blocks. Similar baseline success therefore relies differently on magnitude organization across these histories. [E76]

Incompatible signs are more damaging than removing the same edges: sign flips give **13.28%/16.02%** for matched/independent histories, versus **81.25%/94.92%** after deletion. Coordinated sign switching also transforms reset and noise while preserving ports; its unchanged outcome is a symmetry check, not robustness to arbitrary sign corruption. These are fresh-reader feature assays, distinct from E051's earlier terminal outcomes. SI C.32 retains policy interactions and adjustment. [E76]

The **fresh compatibility confirmation** (E065) tests signs and magnitudes jointly on **32 new blocks**. Final records support **85.25%** success. Combining their final signs with earlier magnitudes reduces this to **30.86%**; matching the earlier signs to those magnitudes restores **76.17%**. The matching-sign benefit is **45.31 points [39.26, 51.66]**, and the sign-by-magnitude interaction **51.95 [45.12, 59.38]**, with family-adjusted 98.3333% whole-block intervals. Final support and later-only weights stay fixed. "Earlier" denotes a recent capture before three further rewrites, not the original taught record. Thus useful components can form an incompatible combination; externally supplying archived components is not autonomous repair (SI C.48). [E94]

The **sign-placement intervention** (E102) asks whether donor signs help because of where they sit. On known source histories with new readers, a combined sign/magnitude transfer gives **56.93%** success, versus **49.66%** after rearranging only the selected signs while preserving their counts, magnitudes and injury-survival groups. The **7.28-point [5.76, 8.89]** benefit and the net improvement over the original both pass their adjusted 97.5% interval requirements. A tighter local-weight/loop comparison, however, gives **56.89% in both primary arms** on fresh reader draws. Placement matters in the first intervention; a general cycle-level explanation remains unresolved (SI C.63). [E109]

A **capture-timing confirmation** (E057) shows that ongoing activity is not always ready to write. Under the primary matched-noise history, allowing 512 recovery updates after an activity pulse improves the pulse-versus-sham contrast by **2.34 points [0.59, 4.30]** across 32 fresh blocks. The interval has family-adjusted 98.75% coverage. Delayed capture needs no unstressed record, whereas a separate magnitude-substitution control receives one (SI C.39). [E84]

The copying comparisons set the limit. In the four-message reference chain, recognition falls from **100% at generation one to 27.3% at twelve**, near 25% chance, while protected copying retains 100%. A guide-free acquired-threshold renewal comparison remains unresolved. [E12]; [E27]

Rewriting can also change tolerance to a later injury. In an **intact-body renewal test** (E020), three immediate-capture replacements give **85.9%** success in a scattered-injury terminal recipient, versus **80.1%** from the protected original record. The secondary difference is **5.9 points [2.1, 10.0]**, a pointwise 95% whole-block interval across 32 fresh blocks. Both records give 100% observed success in intact recipients. Renewal bodies are uninjured and copied bytes remain protected: the benefit concerns later functional reconstruction, without establishing better preservation of founder-specific information or renewal under recurring injury (SI C.17). [E45]

Reconstruction can therefore counter specified loss, improve challenged expression or use supplied structure, while also erasing distinctions that copying preserves. These studies do not establish storage loss or prior structure as necessary or sufficient conditions for benefit, or compare equal total costs. [E40]; [E45]; [E63]

### 5.4. Recovery and the cost of improving the immediate read

Can ordinary activity recover from an incompatible combination without another reference intervention? The **fresh mixture-recovery test** (E068) generates **32 new source blocks** and then follows three ordinary rewrites. Success rises from **30.37% to 50.78% after one rewrite and 59.47% after three**. The absolute gain is **29.10 points [23.44, 34.83]**, with a family-adjusted 98.3333% whole-block interval. Matched cumulative-erasure copying falls to **4.39%**, while erased starts remain at zero despite permission to write. No archived record or teacher enters after the initial mixture. The three registered contrasts pass; recovery remains partial compared with **86.62%** from native records, within the supplied paired-cue, exact-zero-writer apparatus (SI C.49). [E95]

Immediate improvement can nevertheless impair renewal. Saved-cohort experiments first found that pruning selected returning connections helps the current read but harms the next one. The **fresh reconstruction trade-off test** (E077) confirms both directions on **32 newly generated source blocks**. Immediate success rises from **55.76% to 63.18%**, **+7.42 points [4.69, 10.35]**; after one rewrite, the pruned route instead gives **55.86% versus 62.79%**, **−6.93 points [−10.25, −3.71]**. The selected edges are removed once and can return during writing. These are aggregate effects: only eight of 87 immediate beneficiaries later become harmed cases. [E96]; [E98]

The same confirmation tests a compatible substitution. On the pruned successor's background, importing only selected magnitudes from the unpruned successor lowers success to **27.64%**; importing their signs as well gives **63.77%**. Both prespecified directions pass, using **98.75% whole-block intervals** across four contrasts. The two substituted records share support, absolute weights and normalization, isolating the effect of matching sign placement in that comparison. This identifies an actionable compatibility relation without establishing the unique mediator of the original pruning harm. A later fresh test of a filtered magnitude pattern remains unresolved; favorable exposed-cohort follow-ups do not change its verdict (SI C.52). [E98]

The **writer-noise intervention** (forms E098) then holds the starting records, injuries and final readers matched while changing noise pairing between the two cue episodes used for one rewrite. On 32 fresh source blocks, independent rather than matched cue noise raises pruned-mixture reconstruction from **55.71% to 60.99%**, a **5.27-point [2.73, 7.81]** gain. The pruning interaction also passes its adjusted 97.5% interval requirement. This isolates a dependence on the conditions of writing, rather than comparing entire rebuilding histories. It does not establish exclusive mediation by the fallback branch of the writer; noise changes other activity too (SI C.62). [E108]

The registered one-sided bound clears the five-point margin. [E83]

Thus recipient knowledge can improve repair choice. ### 7.1. Capacity depends on the future and the recipient

A matched **recipient-reader intervention** (E023) makes the apparatus dependence explicit. Normalizing incoming drive to surviving connection strength, while holding inherited records and recipient conditions matched, raises injured-recipient success from **27.5% to 99.6%**, a gain of **72.07 points [68.16, 75.98]** with a 95% whole-block interval across 32 independent blocks. The same record can support different outcomes with a different reader; this is not additional transmitted information (SI C.23). [E61]

Saved histories show why present expression is insufficient. Of E041's **307 ever-failed histories, 236** succeed at the additional terminal assay; **192 of 205** always-passing histories succeed there. Separately, 243/281 first-failure histories with enough follow-up recover within three subsequent bodies. These are descriptive counts, not repeated estimates of each record's conditional capacity. Injury and birth conditions vary across generations. [E64]

Repeated fresh readings sharpen the distinction. For 64 selected failed bodies from eight exposed blocks, outgoing-minus-incoming success is **+7.03 points [1.03, 13.48]** under the original injury, but **−6.13 [−14.68, 2.24]** under fresh injuries. The fresh-injury benefit prediction fails. Conversely, another saved-state intervention repairs all 46 selected later failures under exactly their original injury, reset and noise, isolating immediate rescue from a luckier draw. Neither of those assays follows repeated futures through successive rewrites; the later TT018 experiment does so (section 6.4). Their separate selection and inference limits remain in SI C.27/C.44/C.47. [E72]; [E89]; [E93]

The cellular panels also locate continuity in a communicating group whose members change. In **detached-patch tests** (P15/P16), continuing surrounding-tissue instruction is unnecessary once the patch is prepared. A three-cell chain retains and transmits E while each original position is replaced twice. Only the tested slow single-cell schedule succeeds; faster, paired and whole-trio replacements fail, and blocking RNA transport abolishes the successful route. Ordinary-E origins succeed too. These are retained deterministic panels with supplied replacement schedules, not independent population replication or autonomous reproduction (SI C.18). [E48]; [E49]

Cellular readiness provides a complementary causal distinction. Trios with the same coarse EEE label differ after preparation; transferring either the prepared newcomer's complete state or the prepared surviving neighbor's state rescues transmission, while leaving both unprepared fails. RNA and protein substitutions have different sufficiency at the two locations and across backgrounds. Ordinary and written origins agree. Specific internal differences matter beyond classification, without identifying a unique memory molecule. [E65]; [E66]

A completed **communication-timing panel** (P32) succeeds across all sixteen related history/sequence/source/resolution variants with a five-unit immediate RNA-transport pause, the shortest tested. Continuous communication and blocking the entire challenge fail. Delayed pause starts also fail beyond the tested window. Final retention and onward transmission coexist with temporary label loss; numerical resolutions and related histories are model panels, not independent population replicates. SI C.24/C.40 gives the complete schedule and controls. [E85]

New **ordinary-origin substitution panels** sharpen the context dependence. One failed lineage needs combined RNA/ion substitution among the tested arms; in another, ions alone suffice. The ion-only communication-cut control is now complete, but ion-only substitution fails in a further prospective sequence. On that known failure, RNA alone and ions alone fail while their combination restores retention and transmission. Combined rescue then succeeds on one additional prospectively fixed sequence, with untreated failure and loss of transmission when boundary coupling is cut. The combined arm's success does not establish that both components are necessary in that new sequence, where single-component arms were not included. Initial state substitutions are supplied once and variables then evolve freely; related histories and solver resolutions are not independent population replicates (SI C.65). [E102]; [E111]

The source-model correspondence inventory is now explicit: primary ion, RNA and protein entries are separated from derived RNA entries and fixed inputs; added schedules, substitutions and numerical changes are listed. Exact correspondence to the source publication's table geometries and labels remains unresolved. The inventory improves documentation without converting these extensions into a validated reproduction of every source-paper result (SI C.7/C.56). [E102]

### 7.2. Reconstructive Dynamics Theory as a candidate

**Reconstructive Dynamics Theory (RDT)** takes the complete cycle as its object: record → development → body → writing or repair → successor record. Its conjecture is that correction of disruptive variation and preservation of acquired distinctions can jointly predict continuation under declared disturbances. Converging on a common prototype can restore an appearance while erasing historical differences; retaining differences can still fail to build a functioning successor.

Candidate measurements concern correction of perturbations, proximity to loss of the acquired distinction, and the disturbance introduced at each renewal.

A **prospective response-transfer test** (E008) already supports a bounded step toward prediction across embodiments. A frozen map uses measured intervention responses in a source tissue to predict agreement changes in a rewired recipient. Across 32 fresh blocks, prediction RMSE is **1.085 points**, versus **2.472** for direct transfer and **1.632** for appearance-based prediction. Its MSE advantage over appearance is **1.488 squared points [1.144, 1.823]**, a 95% whole-block interval. Source responses are supplied measurements; recipient outcomes are withheld until predictions are frozen. An earlier connectivity contrast fails its direct-transfer comparison. The positive result concerns one reading in related tissue models with shared coordinates, not serial continuation, unrelated substrates or a universal form atlas (SI C.12). [E34]

A useful theory must predict untouched continuations or interventions beyond strong comparators with comparable information and resources. Persistent reader or writer state must be counted in its conditioning variables. Section 2 defines what to estimate; it does not supply this law. TT008 and the repair confirmations are within-apparatus advances, compatible with conventional prediction and error correction.

The new equal-read forecast in section 6.1 is a bounded predictive advance beyond the earlier unresolved selected-record pilot. It does not resolve the distinct prospective tissue challenge-versus-passive comparison or the failed repair-benefit predictor. Changed-injury transfer remains development, and one simple two-state reduction loses to the existing successor forecast. A useful predictor within one apparatus is not yet a general theory of which distinctions rebuilding will preserve (SI C.57/C.61). [E55]; [E103]; [E107]

The decision-model branch separates prediction from the costs and objectives of action. Paid checking improves response to change in the larger model, but a later planned-recovery comparison still fails its joint economic criterion. In an exact 100-task model with perfect paid evidence, an explicit recovery preference reduces recovery cost by **76.5%** relative to a hybrid control while matching its stable-world cost. A ten-task noisy follow-up improves recovery by **25.0%** but increases stable-world cost by **14.9%**; its matched noiseless control also fails the stable-cost requirement. Correct inference and improved recovery do not automatically yield an economical maintenance policy. These supplied decision models are neither record-renewal experiments nor evidence of a uniquely FEP advantage (SI C.66). [E104]; [E112]

The next standard is prospective prediction across changed histories, damage and reader–writer conditions, including the full cost of diagnostic work. Repeated continuations estimate record-conditioned capacity (section 6.4); compatibility, recovery and the fresh pruning reversal constrain explanations of it. The two theory lanes formulate and challenge candidates within this program. Ongoing tests are not assumed to succeed, and the stopped larger continuation campaign contributes no outcome. [E93]; [E94]; [E95]; [E98]; [E103]

### 7.3. When body and memory disagree: sensing and action across scales {#multiscale-error}

What detects a departure from a pattern, and what turns that detection into a useful correction? Three roles must be separated: an observer's error score, a signal available within the system, and the operation that changes its state. An investigator can measure disagreement with a target without supplying that comparison to the dynamics. Restoration alone therefore identifies neither an internal comparator nor the process choosing a corrective action.

Our reference-based tissue model supplies one explicit mechanism: a term proportional to $R-V$ influences body activity, while activity can update R. The native mean removal, clipping and update order remain consequential (SI C.1). This is an implemented comparison between dimensionless fields, not an identified biological anatomical-error circuit. In the Hopfield comparison, local coupling and supplied cues guide updates without an output-target comparison during reconstruction. These mechanisms illustrate different ways to obtain restoration; they do not require every restoring system to calculate the same scalar error. [E08]; [E99]

The new **multiscale track** has completed fourteen development studies. Its early sheet experiments separate sensing from action at sites, 4 × 4 regions and coarse sheet divisions. Coarse signals can ignore fine record corruption, but also miss fine legitimate distinctions. A gate using recent body movement can avoid copying some injuries; sustained injury can instead be treated as a new target. An apparent voting advantage disappears when correction dose is matched. These results establish neither a generally better scale nor an autonomous source of goals. They identify dependencies of supplied mechanisms (SI C.68). [E114]

When body and record disagree, either could be changed. The **relational-record experiment** (LR002) makes this choice explicit in a 32 × 32 sheet. A body reads a noisy record whose neighbor relations can relearn from the record or from body activity. In a compatible stripe-forming body, after 4000 updates at bond-flip probability 0.01, body-driven teaching retains **97.02% record-sign agreement**, versus **50.01%** without repair, **49.70%** with record relearning and **50.13%** with record relearning plus local loop checks. These are means over sixteen development blocks, not percentages of successful lineages or a fresh-successor assay. [E115]

The same route can teach an error. With the imposed environmental field present, body-driven stripe maintenance falls to approximately **61%**; incompatible body dynamics can also rewrite the acquired arrangement toward their preferred pattern. In **gated teaching** (LR003), a site-level inconsistency signal restores about **96%** agreement in the clean stripe condition, versus about **70%** for a connection-only gate. Yet noise opens the gate to the distorted body's errors. The tested local signals therefore do not reliably identify which source should be corrected across these conditions. This is not an impossibility theorem for two interacting records; temporal information, source reliability or further evidence could change the result (SI C.69). [E115]

| Information or operation | What the selected tests show | What remains separate |
|---|---|---|
| State/reference mismatch and recent motion | Available signals can direct body or record correction, with different failure modes. | An investigator's target score is not automatically available to the controller. |
| Neighbor and loop consistency | Relational checks can constrain repair; a broader site gate can improve a tested clean condition. | Internal consistency does not guarantee preservation of the acquired pattern. |
| Joint rather than individual actions | A coordinated update can improve complete-task retrieval where ordinary settling stalls. | Action availability does not demonstrate endogenous multiscale sensing. |
| Successor assays | Present improvement can coexist with later harm. | Coupled body/record maintenance is not yet renewal through fresh replacements. |

The Hopfield branch supplies a separate action test. On 192 fresh networks, delayed iterative feedback completes **55.73%** of tasks where the tested constant schedules complete none, but misses its 75% capability requirement. On another 192 networks, a target-free coordinated two-bit move raises task completion from **47.92% to 56.77%**. Its registered directional test passes; the separate conservative lower bound does not qualify a positive population-mean gain. The pair search evaluates 1128 candidates, and ordinary quadratic and nearest-history controls solve every task. Suppressing newly wrong bits can also reduce complete-task success by suppressing useful repairs. These are bounded retrieval interventions, not demonstrations of inherited flexible competence (SI C.64). [E110]

Finally, the body can supply a **kind of organization without retaining its particular placement**. In the stripe-forming variants, characteristic spacing can return after resource restoration while historical alignment remains near chance; a retained reference can restore that alignment. Sparse positional cues also depend on developmental order: 64 instructed cells in a 32 × 32 sheet yield **99.82% sign agreement** with a suitably oriented growth front versus **68.71%** when the same boundary is used during simultaneous development. Disorder can reverse the advantage in favor of scattered cues. Growth here is a supplied activation schedule, and these are not equal-time comparisons or biological cell division (SI C.70). [E116]

Together these development results make reciprocal maintenance testable: embodied organization can help sustain the information that supports it, while the same feedback can propagate damage or overwrite historical distinctions. The next confirmation must retain both outcomes, freeze the controller and information budget, and test new patterns and correlated damage before assaying fresh successors. SI P.1 distinguishes that remaining program from completed development. A pending third-source voting study contributes no assumed result. [E114]; [E115]; [E116]
