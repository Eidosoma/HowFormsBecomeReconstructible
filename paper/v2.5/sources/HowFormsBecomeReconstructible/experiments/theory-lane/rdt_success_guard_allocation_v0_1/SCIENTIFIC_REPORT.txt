Limits and ordinary controls
The 21 source blocks and 84 founders' packets were previously exposed. The block subset
was selected by a frozen hash of IDs, with fresh continuation and subset streams.
This is development evidence conditional on an exposed panel, not fresh-founder
confirmation. The confidence interval remains broad. There is no RDT-specific inference.
Protected records, the known task, recurring cues, reader and writer remain supplied.

Average occupancy, terminal success, uninterrupted performance and recovery remain
separate. Under independent noise uninterrupted success was 47/84 for the guard,
50/84 for random allocation, and 55/84 for always promoting. Those point estimates
do not support a claim of more reliable uninterrupted continuation. Terminal success
was 70/84, 59/84 and 61/84 respectively; these are descriptive endpoints.
The guard-minus-always-promote average difference was +5.9524 points, with descriptive
two-sided 95% interval [-1.3391,+13.2438]. Under matched noise the guard-minus-random
difference was +2.1164 points, interval [-3.4694,+7.7022]. These secondary comparisons
cannot substitute for the one fixed primary contrast.

Among 33 packets that failed the shared g15 read under independent noise, the guard
had 289/594 successful later reads versus random allocation's 205/594, with at least
one subsequent success in 20 versus 14 packets. Among the 51 initially successful
packets, those counts were 909/918 versus 862/918. The two strata contribute 84 and
47 of the 131 additional successful reads. These are descriptive decompositions.
The guard and protected-retain first-recovery times are identical by construction
because both retain the original packet until the guard's first successful read.
That identity supplies no additional evidence for a recovery mechanism.
