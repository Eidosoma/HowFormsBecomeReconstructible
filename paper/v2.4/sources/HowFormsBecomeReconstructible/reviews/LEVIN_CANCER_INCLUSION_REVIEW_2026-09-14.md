# Levin-cancer branch: evidence worth including

**Reviewed:** 14 September 2026. **Target:** Where Do Forms Come From?, working manuscript v0.6. **Status:** inclusion assessment and proposed prose; not a new simulation, independent replication, or revision of the manuscript/PDF.

**Incorporation update, 14 September:** after the author's request to write these findings into the paper, v0.7 incorporates the selected P8/P9, commitment, boundary-access and conditional-memory findings. E14 is extended, E25-E26 are added, and methods section C.7 states their contracts and source limits. The assessment and proposed prose below remain the pre-incorporation record; the accompanying source snapshot records that inspection rather than a new raw-data audit.

**Recommendation:** add the P8–P9 rewriting-and-replacement sequence to the existing cellular-relay account, and use the commitment experiments as a concrete example of why appearance and future response must be distinguished. P4's communication-boundary intervention and H4b's conditional usefulness result provide shorter supporting points. Keep acquired-reference writing, reciprocal body–record repair, and reconstruction loss as the three central tissue cases.

The useful addition is that **a cell's present label, its capacity to retain that state, and its capacity to transmit it are experimentally separable—and transmission depends on the interaction between carriers and recipients.** This directly serves the paper's four-question framework. It does not establish a new general theory of cancer or collective intelligence.

## 3. Established lineages can be rewritten and then renewed: P8–P9

**P8:** six M-state cue cells contacted three E-state targets. All three targets subsequently retained M after cue removal and two isolated holds, then induced retained M in one fresh E recipient. This complete chain succeeded in all four target backgrounds: fresh E, isolated age-matched E, and the actual round-six carriers of ordinary-E-founded and restored-E-founded lineages. The latter are the actual continuing carriers, not fresh cells assigned their labels. [Results][p8]; [protocol][p8protocol].

The stage-specific controls are particularly useful. Blocking RNA transfer or the group boundary during writing prevented the targets' rewrite. Blocking those routes only during onward transfer preserved the targets' newly acquired M but prevented the fresh recipient from acquiring M. This separates the causal requirements for acquisition and transmission. P8 reports 61 cases, 351 integrations, and agreement in 29 finer comparisons; these counts include controls and refinements.

**P9:** the three actual rewritten targets, taken before P8's one-recipient probe, instructed three fresh E pupils sequentially. Every old teacher was removed; all three retained-M pupils became the next teachers. Both rewritten ancestries and ordinary M founders completed **six three-to-three replacements**. This is neither three donors shrinking to one nor a group replenished with selected successful pupils. [Results][p9]; [protocol][p9protocol].

| P9 condition | Outcome for both rewritten ancestries and ordinary M |
|---|---|
| Sequential contacts | All three new cells retained M in each of six replacement rounds. |
| Simultaneous 20,000-unit contact, with matched final round duration | Failed at round one; teachers became E and pupils retained E. |
| Simultaneous 60,000-unit contact | Also failed at round one; teachers became E and pupils retained E. |
| Sequential RNA block or boundary cut | Failed at round one; teachers retained M, pupils retained E. |

Retention required the target label at the live endpoint and in at least 95% of late-quarter samples in both final 10,000-unit isolated holds. Teachers retained their continuously changing live states between encounters. Previously taught waiting pupils also evolved; they were not frozen. All 16 finer comparisons reportedly agreed across the 34-case panel.

**Interpretation:** the lineage can change its transmitted state, and the resulting transmission can outlast repeated replacement of every carrying cell. The conditions of encounter affect whether that continuation succeeds. Ordinary M also succeeds, so acquired history supplies an entry route to transmissible state without demonstrating a special transmission advantage.

**Boundary:** sequential contacts used a four-cell 2×2 motif; simultaneous contact used a six-cell 2×3 lattice. Wiring and pupil exposure histories differ. P9 therefore establishes success of the staged protocol, not an isolated timing effect. P10 proposes a common six-cell graph and clock to address that distinction; it supplies no completed evidence in this review. Cells, contact arrangements, prepared bath and schedules are externally supplied. There is no autonomous division, self-organized scheduling, inherited flexible task policy, or clinical endpoint.

**Correction to the current narrative:** section 8 presently stops with P7's unequal-group successes and failed three-to-three contact. That is accurate about P7, but incomplete as an account of the branch. Preserve P6/P7 as the failed conditions that motivated P8/P9, then add the successful same-sized staged relay with its limits. P9 does not invalidate P5's separate twelve-round E relay or convert it into a six-round M replication.

## 4. Appearance can precede or follow commitment

The [commitment-window experiment][window] supplies an unusually direct example of the distinction between recognition and continuation:

| Checkpoint | Label when cues were removed | Subsequent cue-free outcome and two replacement rounds |
|---|---|---|
| E-return after three short contacts | M | Settled to E and transmitted E. |
| E-return after four contacts | X, the intermediate classifier interval | Settled to E and transmitted E. |
| M-return after six contacts | M | Reverted to E; failed to transmit M. |
| M-return after eight contacts | M | Retained M and transmitted M. |

The six- and eight-contact M states have the same **coarse label**, not the same complete molecular state. Classification uses `miR200_total − Zeb_mRNA_total`: E above 2, M below −2, X otherwise. X does not establish a third attractor. Commitment means success under the declared removal, retention and replacement assay, not irreversibility under every future challenge. Contact histories also differ in age; the comparison does not isolate contact count from elapsed time or molecular delivery. [Protocol][windowprotocol]; [classifier][classifier].

The subsequent [component experiment][components] provides a causal intervention. In the six-contact background, substituting either the eight-contact **ZEB-protein level alone** or the **two-RNA block together** enabled retained M and M transmission through both fresh-pair rounds. Substituting only the four-ion block did not. All eight factorial combinations were tested at each of two numerical settings; the reported outcomes agreed. Code inspection confirms those block substitutions and explicit preservation of the different source ages. [Protocol][componentsprotocol]; [implementation][componentcode].

This identifies alternative sufficient state changes in a fixed contrast. It does not identify one unique memory molecule, prove either block individually necessary, show that ions never matter, or establish experimentally feasible molecular transplantation.

**Value to this paper:** this is a concrete reason to include future responses in an operational signature. It is stronger than merely noting that different hidden variables exist: selected interventions change the future retained/transmitted state. However, it remains a retrospective within-model case. It does **not** satisfy the reviewer's proposed test that frozen signatures predict held-out challenges better than appearance-based groups, nor establish cross-embodiment identity.

## 5. Where cells communicate matters: P4

P4 placed the same actually acquired and retained M patches at edge and center positions, separating expression powers within the patch from those across its boundary to E helpers. Of 96 eligible deterministic conditions, 78 achieved full patch restoration plus retained onward E in a fresh recipient. Increasing boundary expression power rescued **18 of 48 paired comparisons**; the other 30 already succeeded at both levels. Altering internal expression alone changed **none of 48 paired binary outcomes**. RNA blockade and complete boundary cuts prevented restoration in their matched controls. [Results][p4]; [protocol][p4protocol].

The native expression factor is state dependent, and its regional manipulation affects transport more broadly than one isolated molecular flux. The result identifies the role of the boundary intervention in these settings, not a universal molecular explanation. Center placement rescued six conditions but also increased boundary contacts, so position and connectivity were not isolated.

This supports a specific embodiment point: **a stable group may fail to change because corrective influence cannot adequately reach it under the tested coupling conditions.** It does not imply that all communication should be increased. P2 supplies the complementary failure: some restoration attempts instead converted E helpers to M. In that panel, 15/24 conditions achieved restoration plus onward E, while four spread M to helpers. Neither fraction estimates biological treatment response. [P2 results][p2].

## 6. Useful inherited state is not yet flexible collective agency: H4b

H4b used four new random environmental streams, twelve epochs, paired founder polarities, and independently enabled parent-transfer and current-cue routes. Primary scores averaged target-state matching over epochs 2–12, before assigned contact penalties. [Results][h4b]; [protocol][h4bprotocol].

| Environment | Rewritable inheritance | Cue only | Difference |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Slow, reliable | 93.18% | 79.55% | +13.64 percentage points |
| Fast, reliable | 72.73% | 79.55% | −6.82 points |
| Slow, sparse cues | 77.27% | 57.95% | +19.32 points |
| Slow, noisy cues | 81.82% | 68.18% | +13.64 points |

Differences use the source's unrounded means. These are scores on four streams, not estimated survival probabilities or evidence of statistical significance. Updating won against cue-only in all four slow/reliable histories, lost in three and tied in one fast history, won in three and lost in one sparse history, and won in all four noisy histories. Against inheritance-only, the noisy condition split two wins and two losses. Costs were assigned dimensionless contact penalties, not measured energy or fitness.

A prespecified, unfitted binary rule—copy the previous pair when parent transfer is enabled; replace the label with an available cue when cue transfer is enabled—matched all **6,592 compared pair-checkpoints**. The [analysis code][binarycode] contains this rule explicitly and does not feed hidden truth into it. All realized erroneous cues caused wrong final labels in the rewritable policy at those events; this is not learned bad-cue detection.

This is evidence that inherited information can bridge missing observations when the world persists and become stale when the world changes. It gives an operational usefulness result without establishing autonomous goals or collective reasoning. Exact agreement is restricted to H4b's observations and setting: P6's separate simple-copy comparator matched only 161/198 coarse checkpoints, principally overpredicting successful M transmission. We should not generalize H4b's baseline adequacy to every interaction in the branch. [P6 results][p6].

## 7. Additional negative cases worth retaining

The [commitment-robustness panel][robustness] preserved M before handover in 30 cases, but only 14 renewed it through both replacements. In 16 cases, donor loss left prior retention intact while onward M transmission failed. These totals include controls and finer variants, not independent samples.

[H1][h1] found no single-donor rescue in its tested duration/communication grid. Normal pairs succeeded; weak pairs failed. Some strong-contact failures changed the original donor from M to E, whereas weak contacts could preserve donor M without changing the E recipient. A failed handover can therefore involve inadequate recipient influence, loss of the donor's state, or both.

[H2][h2] made a related separation: two E encounters rewrote single acquired-state M incumbents to retained E, while matched pairs resisted. The rewritten singles then failed onward E transmission; ordinary E singles failed too. This is useful counterevidence to equating successful rewriting with successful inheritance. It is not a unique weakness of acquired state.

These contrasts are closer to the paper's question than adding many additional positive relay counts. They remain different mechanisms and assays from L17's gradual information loss; no common numerical renewal score has been established.

## 8. Proposed manuscript prose

### Section 8: extend the existing P6/P7 paragraph

Later calibrated experiments established rewriting followed by renewed transmission. In P8, six M-state cues converted all three established E carriers to retained M; after cue removal, those actual carriers induced retained M in a fresh E recipient. Blocking communication during writing prevented acquisition, whereas blocking it only during onward contact preserved acquired M in the carriers but prevented transfer. P9 then used the actual rewritten groups to instruct three fresh E cells sequentially and replace every teacher with those pupils. Both rewritten ancestries and ordinary M founders completed six full three-cell replacements. Simultaneous contacts failed even with longer exposure, but contact geometry and pupil histories also differed, so the comparison does not isolate timing. These results demonstrate acquired-state transmission under specified encounters and complete carrier replacement, without establishing a special historical advantage or autonomous organization of the relay. [P8][p8] [P9][p9]

### Section 9.2 or 3.2: concrete commitment example

The cellular commitment experiments distinguish present classification from future continuation. Six-contact and eight-contact states were both M-labelled, yet only the latter retained and transmitted M after cue removal. Conversely, an E-return state still labelled M at withdrawal later settled to and transmitted E. Replacing either the ZEB-protein level or the paired RNA totals in the six-contact background with their eight-contact values enabled retained and transmitted M; the tested ion-only substitution did not. These are alternative sufficient interventions in different complete states sharing a coarse label. They motivate response-based descriptions of form, while leaving prospective prediction on untouched challenges and cross-embodiment equivalence untested. [Commitment window][window] [Components][components]

### Section 9.6: boundary access

In the cellular topology experiment, increasing expression power across the boundary between an acquired-state patch and corrective neighbors rescued 18 of 48 paired restoration-and-transfer comparisons. Changing expression within the patch alone altered none of the matched binary outcomes. These interventions distinguish access to corrective influence from internal coupling in the tested setting. They do not establish a universal benefit of stronger communication: other restoration conditions instead changed the helpers toward the patch's state. [P4][p4] [P2][p2]

### Section 8: usefulness and agency boundary

An environmental follow-up found conditional value in inherited, updateable state. Relative to cue-only response, it improved mean target matching in slow, sparse-cue and noisy-cue worlds but reduced it under rapid change. A prespecified binary copy/update rule matched every compared pair-checkpoint. Thus useful persistence did not require a richer decision rule to explain these observations. The task and contact opportunities were supplied, and neither biological fitness nor autonomous goal formation was measured. [H4b][h4b]

Use these selectively; their purpose is to sharpen the current argument, not make this branch a fourth long central case.

## 9. Relation to Levin and the open ontology

These results make relational embodiment experimentally concrete: recipient physiology, retained state, group boundaries, and the encounter protocol jointly determine whether a state is restored, changed, or transmitted. Their support for the paper's operational interpretation is the causal separation of those roles.

They do not uniquely favor Levin's philosophical framework over conventional regulatory dynamics. Both E and M are available states of a supplied model, and the experiments do not show novel forms originating in another realm. The stronger possibility of independently existing, active patterns remains open, as agreed in the manuscript; these experiments supply no discriminating test of it.

Nor should the cancer-related source name turn E/M classifications into cancer outcomes. This branch measures regulatory labels, retention and transmission. It does not demonstrate treatment response, malignant growth reversal, metastasis prevention, or flexible tissue-level repair competence. Any biological translation needs a separately validated mapping and biological tests.
