What the comparison supports
Rewriting once on first success produced307 extra successful reads over protected
retention, while continued guarded updating added56 more. Thus the first-rewrite
policy captured84.57% of the observed ongoing-guard improvement over protected
retention in this panel (307 of363 extra reads). This percentage is a descriptive
comparison, with no separate precision or equivalence claim. It does not decompose
an individual lineage's causal trajectory into independent additive mechanisms.

The ongoing guard made1643 rewrites; the new policy made98, with14 packets never
rewriting. The1545 additional guarded updates generated the positive whole-policy
contrast. The primary policies therefore differ in rewrite count as well as continued
feedback. This design does not isolate every contribution of count, timing and
adaptivity. Matched random allocation still matches the ongoing guard's1643 rewrites
exactly within each block and generation, and gives a descriptive guard advantage
of +8.0853 points, two-sided95% interval [+1.8149,+14.3557]. It is supplied with the
contemporaneous guard group total K and is a coupled diagnostic comparator.
It does not match the single-rewrite policy's count or schedule.

Both primary policies execute the same number of reads and candidate constructions
in this assay. Declining promotion does not recover that computation. The much lower
rewrite count is not a measured wall-time or total-compute saving. Known task labels,
protected records, recurring cues and the reader/writer apparatus are supplied.

Descriptive recovery decomposition
Thirty-four packets failed the shared pre-action g15 read. They contributed269 later
successes for the guard and251 for the single-rewrite policy, out of612 opportunities,
a difference of18 reads. The78 initial passes contributed1393 versus1355 successes
out of1404 opportunities, a difference of38. Those strata contribute0.8929 and1.8849
percentage points respectively to the entire-cohort primary difference. The residual
benefit of repeated rewriting in this comparison must not be conflated with the
prior guard-versus-random contrast that motivated it.

The guard, single-rewrite and protected policies have identical first-success times
by construction: their records and forcing are identical until the first successful
read. In the initial-failure stratum,20 packets had a later success in each of those
three policies. That shared first recovery is a structural identity, not independent
evidence of a recovery mechanism. The guard and single-rewrite policies then differed
in later occupancy, terminal success (19 versus18) and uninterrupted success (6 versus4).

Interpretation and limits
The study supports an added finite-continuation benefit from continued ordinary
success-conditioned rewriting after an initial successful rewrite, on these exposed
founders under this disturbance distribution. One rewrite accounts for much of the
observed gain over protected retention, but does not match the ongoing guard on the
primary endpoint. The result neither qualifies a compact RDT predictor nor establishes
an RDT-specific mechanism, autonomous apparatus maintenance, transfer to a new reader
or a general law. It supplies no new matched-noise or noise-interaction test.
The earlier allocation replication and noise-precision results remain supported;
the six-test replication remains unresolved; historical-guard and conditional-benefit
failures remain closed. This result does not retroactively qualify those candidates.

No successor has been launched. A next claim or experiment needs a separate frozen
prediction, comparison and budget. This completed panel is now exposed evidence for
any subsequent design; a fresh-founder replication would be a separate study.
