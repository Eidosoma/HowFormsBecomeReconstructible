# E121 — strong independent-noise histories, but no immediate-benefit reversal

Reviewed 2 November 2026. All 32 fresh blocks completed their own histories in
four noise/tolerance regimes. The supplied coordinated teaching curriculum
remained common; the regime-specific rules began with autonomous renewal.
No lineage or failed case was removed.

## Primary result

The primary deletes all restored connections regardless of their capture route
(PR), in histories renewed with independent ongoing cue noise and 1e−4 fallback
tolerance. Under the common assessment reader:

| Boundary | Intact success | Pruned success | Pruned minus intact, points [pointwise 95%] |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Immediate | 91.553% | 91.016% | −0.537 [−1.074, −0.098] |
| After one rewrite | 91.455% | 86.230% | −5.225 [−6.689, −3.809] |

**Fourteen of fifteen gates pass; immediate benefit fails.** The operation
causes a small immediate loss and a larger successor loss. This is useful
evidence about restored connections, but it is not an example of improving
present function at the successor's expense. Acquisition, common/native
usefulness and retention all pass; failure of the primary is not due to
nonfunctional records. Under native readers the corresponding intact/pruned
scores are 92.285%/91.846% immediately and 91.602%/87.109% after writing.

Pointwise intervals above are those used by the frozen primary conjunction.
The automated report additionally supplies simultaneous profile bands, which
are wider and do not resolve the small immediate loss. The conclusion that
immediate benefit is absent from the established findings holds under either
convention.

## Regimes and intervention identity

Independent-exact intact mixture histories score about 94.5%; independent-
tolerant ones about 91.5%, at both boundaries under the common reader.
The matched-exact histories score about 45–51%; matched-tolerant ones about
2–3%. These are complete-history comparisons in this apparatus, not estimates
of the isolated effect of the next writer. Pulse noise and source streams
also differ from the original E077 histories as declared in the protocol.

The fallback-defined intervention PF is **empty in every independent-exact
mixture record** and therefore produces identical outcomes. Its zero effect
cannot test the consequences of an applied pruning operation. Under independent
tolerance it often selects no edges and changes little. In contrast, PR
selects restored edges regardless of fallback use and is nonempty throughout.
In the independent-tolerant mixture it removes about 75 edges and re-isolates
21.89 nonport cells on average.

Matched-exact histories retain the reversal: PR gives +4.980 points immediately
and −3.857 after rewriting; PF gives +5.225 and −3.662. Their low absolute
performance remains visible rather than being used to replace the failed
independent-tolerant primary. Independent-exact PR also harms successors
(−4.541 points); it does not establish an immediate gain.

## Individual harms and limits

For the primary PR comparison, 2,048 paired assessment contexts contain
**one gain and 12 harms immediately, then 11 gains and 118 harms** after writing.
There are 32 independent sources; the assessment draws and descendants are
dependent observations. Native-reader and separate-history results, all no-op
counts, signed transfer, normalized coupling and capture diagnostics remain
in the frozen outputs.

The strong functional lead is independent-noise renewal with useful absolute
performance. It does not extend the particular immediate-benefit/later-cost
claim across regimes. Further mechanism work must distinguish capture route,
re-isolation and relative strength; these results alone do not identify a
unique mediator. Shared resets, float64 storage and a zero normalization floor
remain. Finite-grid robustness and a second substrate remain separate questions.
