## Prediction and its limits

The sole primary forecast is a strictly positive repeated-minus-one-time difference in uninterrupted success across generations 15–32. A nonpositive estimate fails it; a positive estimate with a descriptive interval including zero is unresolved. Terminal, recovery and other secondary endpoints cannot rescue the primary. No practical-effect, equivalence or noninferiority margin is declared.

Before design, the independent-noise repeated arm had 234 first-reconstruction successes and all 234 remained successful through generation 32. Given the required identical first reconstruction, one-time repair cannot outperform that arm on uninterrupted success for these fixed trajectories. The uncertainty is whether and how many later failures occur after withdrawal. Thus the strictly positive forecast is falsifiable at zero, but the weak nonnegative ordering follows from exposed controls and is not a new empirical prediction. Report this constraint prominently. A small or zero gap would motivate a separately frozen fresh-cohort test before claiming one repair is sufficient. Any positive result concerns the observed ongoing need for supplied correction; it does not establish autonomy.
