## Plain-language result

A smaller original group was easier to rewrite in the two acquired-state
backgrounds: two successive E encounters changed a single original M cell
to E, and that cell kept E after the cues were removed. The matched two-cell
exposure groups resisted the same cue schedule.

But the rewritten single cell could not pass E onward to fresh M recipients.
Thus easier rewriting and renewable transmission are different properties.
This panel establishes the former contrast, not a successful one-cell
hereditary rewrite.

## Complete baseline pattern

All regular conditions use the same one-carrier readout after exposure.

| Source | One E encounter, one incumbent | Two E encounters, one incumbent | One/two encounters, two incumbents | No-cue, same-state, blocked controls |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Protein-stabilized M | Retains M | Rewritten to retained E; E renewal fails | Selected original retains M | Retain M |
| RNA-stabilized M | Retains M | Rewritten to retained E; E renewal fails | Selected original retains M | Retain M |
| Ordinary M | Retains M | Retains M | Selected original retains M | Retain M |

Both acquired single incumbents remained M at the first contact endpoint,
then became E at the second. Their isolated hold intervals were entirely
E-labelled in the late windows. They were the original cells, not an E cue
mistakenly forwarded. Yet both fresh M recipients remained M in each readout
round: neither prompt nor eventual settled E renewal succeeded.

Ordinary E controls separately confirmed that two E donors can renew E at
these settings. A single ordinary E donor failed, as did a pair with all
readout communication blocked. The single-donor failure is therefore not
unique to the newly rewritten state. The E pair control is prepared E, not
proof that two acquired E cells would behave identically.

All eight acquired-route active comparisons and all three reference controls
were repeated with finer numerics; their outcomes and endpoint labels agree.
Across all 44 executions, four regular cases acquired/retained E (two baseline
plus their fine repeats), but none renewed that acquired E. Only the ordinary
E pair and its fine repeat renewed E. Counts include numerical repeats, not
independent biological samples.

## Limits and next question

This is M-to-E only and informed by H1, not independent validation of a
hypothesis selected from H1. Cue number/time is matched, not actual delivered
molecular dose or energetic cost. A second incumbent can reshape a shared
cue through reciprocal communication. These contrasts do not establish
an adaptive advantage of a narrow bottleneck.

The acquired and ordinary M histories also have different complete states;
the difference is not a hidden memory beyond those states or an inherited
new learning rule. No new attractor, autonomous reproduction, evolutionary
fitness or wet-lab mechanism is established.

A natural next question is whether two SEPARATELY rewritten original E
incumbents can transmit together, then survive a longer relay. H3 explicitly
joins the protein-route and RNA-route incumbent as a new, externally assembled
cross-route pair, without duplicating either cell. This does not establish
that one route autonomously produces a pair. The reliability panels also test
known M/E pairs, failed single carriers, repeated donor shocks and held-out
schedules. See [RELIABILITY_SCREEN_PROTOCOL.md](RELIABILITY_SCREEN_PROTOCOL.md)
and [RELIABILITY_CONFIRMATION_PROTOCOL.md](RELIABILITY_CONFIRMATION_PROTOCOL.md).
