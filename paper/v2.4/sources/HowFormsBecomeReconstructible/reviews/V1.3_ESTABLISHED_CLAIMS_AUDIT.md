# v1.3: audit of established claims

13 October 2026. Editorial audit of the evidence already selected for v1.2, as requested. No pull, new outcome intake, simulation, fit or new uncertainty estimate.

## Assessment

The central thesis is already well supported and well represented: acquired history, present expression and successor usefulness are separable, and interventions on bodies, records and their reading/writing relationships change reconstruction. The omissions are mainly established **uses and implications** of this framework that became less visible when the main paper was shortened. They are present in the ledger and supporting material, rather than missing from the research record.

Four additions deserve main-text space. They do not require a new theoretical claim, a longer campaign history or another experiment. The readable abstract and five-question structure remain; all sixteen main figures and five supporting figures are unchanged.

### The most consequential correction: what rewriting can improve

The E020 comparison prevents a two-case summary from becoming too restrictive. The established evidence is not exhausted by “rewriting beats a decaying copy” and “a guide supplies family structure.” With the copied bytes protected, repeated expression and capture can yield a record that performs better in an injured terminal recipient. This still uses supplied teaching, a staged reader, immediate local capture and protection. It is neither an autonomous origin result nor evidence that more founder-specific information survives.

The appropriate synthesis is: **the value of rewriting depends on what is preserved, what changes during reconstruction, and the conditions under which a successor must perform.** E020 supports one particular favorable comparison; E004/E014 and the reference-chain failures retain the opposite cases. No necessary-and-sufficient law or equal-total-cost comparison follows.

### Prediction, repair selection and repair scheduling need different claims

E008 supplies a response-prediction success. E013 supplies a small, separately confirmed repair-selection success. TT008 improves probability forecasts while its paired decision task ties simpler controls. P08 does not establish a better repair-timing policy. These results are compatible because their choices, information inputs, objectives and budgets differ.

E013's chosen objective also matters: it optimizes **founder-specificity recovery**, not maximum total agreement. Its report gives greater own-agreement recovery for the strongest-entry policy, but much less specificity recovery than the graph-informed policy. That observation reinforces the existing distinction between overall reconstruction and particular-history preservation; it is not a new significance test between total-agreement outcomes.

- **E002 / E33:** exact finite maps distinguish changed access from changed fast-state destinations under a fixed encompassing law. Already present in the discussion and not a test that an independent possibility space changes.

## Claims that remain unestablished

No editorial promotion is appropriate for general autonomous maintenance, unprescribed inherited competence, a universal form catalogue, an independently active nonphysical source, or RDT as an established predictive law. Nor do the retained studies prove universal superiority of rewriting, general numerical robustness, or a universal optimal repair schedule.

D186 remains an exposed early-capture development lead, despite its informative content-redirection result. D182's ordinary-writer focal result remains mixed while its named secondary lead recurs on fresh founders. D099's large writing effect does not pass its complete benchmark. The E053 noninferiority conjunction remains failed. None is upgraded by this audit, and ongoing lanes provide no additional outcome.
