# TT022: serial durability of body-derived capture

The [protocol](PROTOCOL.md) follows TT021's positive one-read result through
**eight body replacements**, with **four independent futures per starting record**.
Four already-exposed TT021 blocks supply all 64 record positions in each of two
record families. Seven policies retain ordinary repair, repeated capture, a
new held snapshot each cycle, fixed reading, matched averaging, one-time capture,
and unchanged input. Each new body receives only its own outgoing record.

Final fresh-recipient recognition and uninterrupted body performance are
separate outcomes. All failures, adverse changes and message strata remain.
This is bounded development on exposed starting cases, with four population
blocks; it is not a fresh-population confirmation or a precise per-record
capacity estimate. The starting cohort was already exposed; serial futures were
generated under the frozen development design and are now exposed as well.

The [checked result](INTERPRETATION.md) is negative for extending the one-read
benefit through repeated capture. After eight replacements, repeated capture
scores **48.438% versus ordinary repair's 50.391%** on common records. Keeping
the first captured record without later writing gives **68.750%**. Informative
records show a larger loss: repeated capture **48.438%**, ordinary **80.469%**,
and one-time capture **89.453%**. The four-block development design supplies
no significance verdict or fresh-population confirmation.

All four blocks finished in about 75 minutes. A final report-order assertion
stopped the original execution; [report recovery](REPORT_RECOVERY.md) preserves
that failed run and completes the saved closeout separately. The scientific
summary reproduces byte-for-byte, and only report row order changes. All 328
frozen inputs, 10,100 original files and 68,032 saved array receipts verify.
No simulation was rerun. See the [report](checks/report_recovery_v1/REPORT.md)
and [closeout](CLOSEOUT.json).

The completed [first-rewrite diagnostic](FIRST_REWRITE_INTERPRETATION.md)
locates a large loss after the first repair: informative-record recognition
in the next body is **61.328% with capture/write versus 89.844% with the initial
packet preserved**. The effect depends strongly on the retained target half.
Capture gives 100.000% when the positive half survives, 35.938% when the negative
half survives, and 54.688% when both polarities survive. The corresponding
preserved-record controls give 89.062%, 92.188% and 89.062%.

The native −1 replacement fill is copied into memory and supplies a spatial
pattern that can oppose or compete with the task. Saved-array decomposition
verifies this geometric contribution; its causal role in the outcome still
needs intervention. All seven policies and all cases remain in this post-hoc
analysis. [Diagnostic closeout](FIRST_REWRITE_CLOSEOUT.json) passes with no new
dynamics. The proposed [six-condition counterfactual](FIRST_REWRITE_NEXT_STEP.md)
has now been authorized, qualified and frozen as [TT023](../TT023/README.md).
It completed in 11 minutes 10 seconds on two CPUs, with independent replay
and saved closeout passing. Removing nominal fill raises informative-record
next-body recognition from 61.328% to 87.891%; preserving the incoming packet
still gives 89.844%. This supports the specified intervention for one episode
on these exposed cases. Serial durability remains open; see the
[TT023 interpretation](../TT023/INTERPRETATION.md).

Both fixed and average policies compute and save native-writer and averaging
traces, inheriting only the declared output. The fixed policy discards its
averaging sidecar; the average policy discards its native-writer sidecar. The
per-cycle metadata retain both timings. The benchmark includes both costs,
and there is no computational-efficiency or writer-only serial claim.

See [execution](EXECUTION.md), [qualification](QUALIFICATION.json) and
[baseline provenance](BASELINE_MANIFEST.json). The full run used two
single-thread workers and was inspected at the user's request. All serial
outcomes are now exposed. TT023 is the subsequently authorized follow-up;
no automatic polling or retry is configured.
