## Decision and question

Stop elaborating the already-studied cohort. Give one fixed candidate a genuinely
fresh test: does the **residual-minus probe** improve prediction beyond matched
passive observation after both predictors lose their reading-difference slopes?

This is the former control−, not D141's original 25%-site shuffle and not D151's
competing-axis attenuation. Selecting it now is development-informed selection,
not a retrospectively registered winner. It must keep its exact D151 definition;
there is no new dose, direction, seed, functional-address or challenge search.
The original shuffle, attenuation and control+ remain in the secondary panel.
A positive finding would qualify only this fixed external diagnostic/model.

There is ONE primary comparison: tied:sham_minus versus tied:passive. Confirming
generic predictor simplification (tied versus separate passive) is important but
secondary. It cannot substitute for an unsuccessful primary challenge comparison.
Starting-only and one-copy baselines must remain prominent and fairly tuned.

## Training and predictor rules

Use only the 16 repeat-covered development founders 90000–90015, all four lives,
four public family/writer groups and four saved D142 futures. Former D141 test
founders are now development. All earlier founders/results remain historical
evidence; those without these four labels are not silently added to fitting.

The training target is each case's arithmetic mean of its four saved outcomes.
Every observer receives the same labels. Use the D152 group-sensitive ridge
class, D155 seven starting/six-per-reading features, four unpenalized intercepts,
training-only group centering/scaling, common/deviation penalty ratio one,
SD<=1e-12 replacement by 1, and prediction clipping to [-1,1].

The primary pair uses D160's tied RAW slopes under its unchanged original
19-feature penalty. This is not restandardized mean encoding. Preserve the
unequal-scale geometry and verify against an independent group-coordinate SVD.

Select one COMMON alpha from {.001,.01,.1} by minimizing the equal-weight mean
of the two primary observers' saved whole-founder-out absolute four-future MSE.
Ties within 1e-12 prefer larger alpha. This symmetric rule does not maximize the
candidate's advantage or give its comparator a different selected setting.
It is a NEW deployment choice made after development, not an unbiased estimate
of test performance. All three settings' scores remain visible.

Select starting-only and one-copy alpha separately by their own saved
whole-founder-out MSE using the same grid/tie rule. Do not deliberately give
the cheap baselines the primary pair's setting if their own setting is better.
No new grid, nested retuning, test normalization or outcome-dependent choice.

In the later preparation stage, fit each fixed observer/setting on all 16
development founders, verify independently, and seal before ANY new founder
generation. Retain all 17 existing observer definitions × three settings (51
models) as a fixed secondary context panel. No test-stage retraining. Training
on 16 rather than the planning folds' 15 founders is disclosed and means the
planning effect/variance is only a proxy for the deployed models.

## Sealing and primary estimand

After generating the whole fixed cohort's founder/diagnostic data, save and
hash every model's forecasts for ALL cases in a write-once global seal. The
barrier must prohibit ANY actual primary or control lineage outcome before
that seal. The fitted models are already sealed before founders. Recheck all
feature/input hashes at the transition. No rolling forecast/outcome batches.

The continuous outcome remains qualified own-minus-alternative preference:
strict abs(V)>.2, positive sign V>0 and denominator 256. No new survival or
shape-preservation threshold. The primary loss is average INDIVIDUAL-future
squared error, averaged equally over four futures, four lives and four groups,
then equally over fresh founders. Gain = passive loss minus candidate loss.

The difference of these losses equals the difference of errors to the four-
future mean; check that identity, but retain the absolute individual-future
errors and noise contribution. A lower error to an averaged target is not
itself new information. Four futures and 16 cases are NOT independent founders.

Proposed uncertainty rule: 20,000 percentile bootstrap samples of WHOLE fresh
founder blocks, central 95% interval, seed 16120260920. Keep every life, group,
observer and future together. Positive lower bound: fixed-probe predictive
gain; negative upper bound: predictive harm; otherwise unresolved. No arbitrary
minimum-success percentage, equivalence claim, subgroup rescue, optional
stopping, sample-size extension or challenge change after seeing outcomes.

Report absolute MSE, MAE/RMSE in preference points, bias/calibration, relative
MSE gain, every block, every group and all four futures. Primary ratio uses the
observed prospective passive denominator; planning percentages use the saved
development denominator, and are not forecasts of that ratio's exact interval.
Primary inference is conditional on the one frozen trained-model set and host
distribution, not an estimate over repeated independently trained learners.

## Precision calculation, not retrospective power

D161 reuses all 16 development founders' fixed out-of-fold predictions. For
each fixed comparison, compute the 16 mean-future loss differences, then their
sample SD s (divisor 15). The hypothetical normal half-width at N new independent
founders is 1.9599639845*s/sqrt(N). Report SD multipliers 1, 1.5 and 2; the latter
are openly chosen sensitivity scenarios, NOT estimated confidence bounds.

The proposed resolution is ±0.25% of development passive MSE, with multiplier
1.5. Solve N=ceil((1.9599639845*1.5*s/(.0025*reference_MSE))²), then round UP to
a multiple of 128, minimum 128. Retain alternatives N=16/32/64/128/256/512/768/
1024 and resolutions .25/.5/1/2 percent. This is an operational precision target,
not a biological meaningful-effect threshold, an equivalence margin, 80% power,
or assurance that a small real advantage will be detected.

The same data motivated many model choices, s comes from only 16 overlapping
CV fits, and deployed coefficients will change. Thus these are approximate
planning scales, not development confidence intervals or prospective evidence.
No estimated effect is inserted into a power calculation. Unknown distribution
tails/variance shifts can make real uncertainty larger. Outcome precision is
assessed only at the fixed completed N; never extend N until a sign appears.
