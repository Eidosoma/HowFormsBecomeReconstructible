# P02 and the sufficiency of current expression

This note derives a boundary on the P02 claim. It supplies no new physical law.
The empirical proposal must earn its complexity against familiar latent-state
and nonlinear prediction models.

## What the claim must distinguish

Let Z_t contain the two cue-end bodies, the current injury mask, the observed
current-output summaries, and the apparatus phase at the generation-14 forecast
boundary. The summaries retain information from the native observation window;
we do not assume that its means can be recovered from the final body alone.
Ports, task rule, writer, reader, generation and disturbance contract are
declared conditioning variables. The cue-end bodies determine the next O
capture. No subsequent body is observed by the predictor.

The external apparatus continues to supply the cue, task repertoire, initial
body/reset distribution, reader/writer algorithms, resources, and damage/noise
schedule. These experiments do not test endogenous apparatus maintenance.
Within a lineage context the branches use the same future randomness. If its
realization is conditioned on, that realization belongs to the encompassing
state/contract; if it is averaged over, its distribution belongs to the future
kernel. A seed identifier is not an admissible predictive feature here.

Write K_t for the conditional transition-and-reconstruction kernel, and q for
terminal success under the declared fresh scattered assay. The probability of
terminal success is the expectation-operator expression

    C_t(z) = (K_t K_{t+1} ... K_{T-1} q)(z).

This is terminal success. Uninterrupted success requires the success-weighted
sub-Markov kernels from P01. The two endpoints are not interchangeable.

The P02 assertion is that some compact record statistic r(z) predicts C_t beyond
the declared expression e(z) and strong conventional summaries. It is stronger
than observing that a binary phenotype omits information. An ordinary Markov
model with hidden state already permits this phenomenon. Likewise, changing K
while keeping the initial body fixed demonstrates an apparatus intervention,
not different state-specific continuation under one apparatus. That is why
this package excludes the repeated-T future and uses the common-O boundary.

## An exact sufficiency implication

Assume that for each t, the distribution of e(Z_{t+1}) given Z_t=z depends only
on e(z). Equivalently, for every measurable expression set B there is a reduced
kernel Kbar_t such that

    K_t(z, e^{-1}(B)) = Kbar_t(e(z), B).

Also assume q(z)=qbar(e(z)). These are assumptions about the declared expression,
kernel and terminal assay, not facts established by current simulator outputs.
They are a sufficient condition for exact reduction; they are not necessary
for every possible one-endpoint coincidence.

For any bounded expression function f, integration gives

    K_t(f composed with e) = (Kbar_t f) composed with e.

Start with q=qbar composed with e and apply this identity backwards through
the finite horizon. Induction gives

    C_t(z) = (Kbar_t ... Kbar_{T-1} qbar)(e(z)).

Thus equal expression entails equal continuation probability under these
assumptions. A properly established probability difference at exactly equal
expression would reject this expression-sufficiency account. It would not
identify the particular missing variable, prove a new theory, or reject all
conventional state-space explanations. Conversely, equal continuation for one
endpoint does not prove full expression-level lumpability.

## What approximate matches can establish

The 0.02 output caliper bounds an observed discrepancy, not the discrepancy in
latent success probabilities. If C_t=g_t composed with e and g_t had a known
Lipschitz constant L in the infinity norm, matched outputs would imply
|C_t(z)-C_t(z')| <= 0.02 L. No such L has been established. Near a basin boundary,
small expression differences could have large consequences even for a purely
expression-based account. We therefore retain continuous outputs, their
quadratic products, current margin/pass, history and unsigned access in the
forecast comparison. This is a specified set of rivals, not a proof that all
expression-based rivals have been exhausted.

One terminal Bernoulli observation per branch does not estimate each state's
conditional probability. Shared future randomness makes within-context
comparisons useful, but cannot turn outcome discordance into an exact capacity
inequality. Post-treatment matching can also select a nonrepresentative subset;
it does not identify a direct causal effect of spacing after removing expression.

## The candidate's scientific burden

Signed 64-step cue transfer is computed from the actual O packet using a linear
normalized reader. It is available at the boundary, but it is neither the
nonlinear native assay nor an estimate of native continuation probability by
definition. It can fail through nonlinear dynamics, future rewrites or loss of
information not represented by its scalar. Unsigned access and expression-only
nonlinear summaries can account for its apparent predictive benefit.

The fixed primary comparison asks whether signed transfer reduces excluded-
block Brier error after those rivals. A positive result is a development lead;
a negative result rejects this particular predictive increment in this domain.
No result changes the earlier negative short-probe P01 results. This later
common-future analysis is not a replacement depth chosen to rescue P01.

The most damaging feasible next test of a promising candidate is a frozen
evaluation on genuinely unexposed contexts, with repeated future disturbance
draws for each matched state and a stronger expression-based predictor trained
without the test contexts. To identify a record mechanism more directly, one
would additionally need a feasible record intervention that changes the
candidate while preserving the current-expression criterion and apparatus.
No such intervention, prospective margin, sample size or new simulation is
claimed or launched by this package.
