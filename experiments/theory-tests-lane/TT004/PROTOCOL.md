# Observation variability and rare loss uncertainty

TT004 is a bounded development follow-up authorized by the user's “lets go” after TT003. It reuses the exposed eight-packet pilot and performs exact finite-probability calculations. It generates no native trajectory, does not change the RDT candidate, and does not qualify a full-model or population forecast. The design is informed by TT003's variable fresh assays and saturated primary fits.

For a packet w, define q_c(w) as the probability that a fresh body expresses category c under the frozen TT003 nuisance distribution. Renewal changes w through a packet transition kernel. A clone assay samples q(w) without changing the continuing packet. Conditional on w, duplicate assays are independent; their common packet must not be confused with a shared random recipient state. Paired-cue randomness remains part of each assay. This study does not separate damage, birth and update noise experimentally.

## Native repeated observations

Use all eight TT003 packets, all primary and duplicate assays at depths 0/1/2, and all protected-copy observations. Preserve the TT003 COMPLETE identity, source labels, summary and observation arrays. Do not open other native studies' outcomes.

For each fixed starting packet, pool its four independent fresh-assay groups (primary depth 0, its duplicate, protected-copy depths 1/2), giving 64 observations. Estimate all three category probabilities. Also report the duplicate-disagreement probability at each of the three rewriting boundaries, with 16 path pairs per packet. Form one family of 48 intervals: 8*3 category probabilities plus 8*3 disagreement probabilities. Use equal-tailed exact binomial intervals with marginal error .05/48. The union bound gives at least 95% simultaneous coverage under the fixed sampling contract; dependence between intervals is allowed. These are conditional probabilities for exposed selected packets, not population effects or prospective confirmation. They do not constrain future rewriting.

For ancestral-rule success indicators a_i,b_i in each duplicate pair, report descriptive within-packet and between-packet variance components. With s_i=a_i+b_i, estimate E[p^2] by mean(a_i*b_i), E[p(1-p)] by mean(1[a_i!=b_i])/2, and (E[p])^2 by sum_{i!=j}(s_i*s_j)/(4*n*(n-1)). The between-packet estimate is their appropriate difference. Retain negative estimates as finite-sample artifacts; do not clip them or report them as negative physical variance. No variance-component confidence interval is claimed. At depth zero the source packet is fixed, so its true between-packet component is zero.

## Exact observation counterexample

Construct two time-homogeneous packet models with an initially robust source. In one, renewal always produces the same packet with independent Bernoulli(.75) expression in each assay. In the other, renewal independently produces a deterministically readable packet with probability .75 and an unreadable packet otherwise. Both yield identical single-assay histories at depths 0/1/2 and identical later single-assay probabilities. Duplicate assays after renewal disagree with probability .375 in the first and zero in the second. Verify the complete finite distributions and their representation by the candidate's single-observation HMM. This distinguishes meanings of a hidden state; it does not show that the existing HMM cannot represent variable assays. Reuse TT001's unrestricted-future counterexample to keep structural assumptions explicit.

## Exact uncertainty and abstention benchmark

Evaluate two restricted models contained in the frozen two-mode candidate:

1. Irreversible readiness loss. The source is ready, identity never flips, and each renewal loses readiness with probability q, with no recovery. For short length L=2, each independent path survives with probability s=(1-q)^L; k surviving paths is Binomial(n,s). Terminal A at horizon H is s^(H/L). Propagate exact and Wilson binomial intervals for s through this monotone function. This uses the path-survival statistic and does not claim optimal use of observed failure timing.
2. Symmetric identity flips with perfect readability. The source identity is A and each renewal independently flips identity with probability q in [0,.5]. All identities are observed, so the 2*n transition flips are binomial. Terminal A is [1+(1-2*q)^H]/2. Intersect binomial intervals with [0,.5] and reverse endpoints under this decreasing map. An empty intersection is retained as model rejection and abstention, never clipped to a point.

Use n in {16,64,256,1024}, H in {8,32}, and q in {0,.0001,.001,.005,.01,.025,.05,.1,.25,.5}. Sum over every possible count using its exact binomial mass: no Monte Carlo coverage error and no simulated long outcomes. Report coverage, expected interval width, expected squared probability-estimation error, and all-success/zero-flip event probabilities. Compare equal-tailed Clopper-Pearson intervals with Wilson score intervals as a standard approximate comparator. The former has a finite-sample coverage guarantee under each stated binomial model; neither protects against model misspecification. Record unconditional coverage and coverage conditional on issuing a narrow interval separately.

Issue a model-conditional interval only when the interval is nonempty and its width is at most .10; otherwise abstain. This .10 width is an illustrative development precision target, not the native RDT acceptance margin. A confidence procedure's unconditional 95% guarantee does not imply 95% coverage after conditioning on issuance. Report the joint probability of issuing and missing the true value. Do not use this submodel procedure to issue native full-rewrite forecasts.

Also calibrate selection explicitly. For the fixed count set selected by the ordinary exact interval's width rule, invert equal-tailed binomial tests conditional on membership in that set. This truncated binomial family retains monotone likelihood ratios. Evaluate its conditional coverage by summing all selected count masses. Keep widened or empty intervals, report how often corrected intervals exceed the .10 target, and do not apply another width filter after correction. Conditional coverage after any further selection would require a new calculation. The selection set is a deterministic function of n, H and the frozen rule, not of the unknown parameter.

Compute best-case zero-short-loss sample counts needed for a two-sided 95% lower bound on horizon-32 survival to exceed .8/.9/.95 in the absorbing-loss model. These are conditional, zero-loss arithmetic requirements, not a native power or sample-size recommendation. The all-success n=16 result is an explicit diagnostic, irrespective of whether it is favorable.

## Validation and execution

Check interval boundaries against an independent binomial-tail inversion and SciPy's documented implementation. Enumerate coverage at all planned parameter values, including the selection-adjusted procedure. Expected widths are conditional on nonempty sets; empty-set probabilities remain separate. Independently enumerate hidden-state paths to verify both submodels and the duplicate-observation counterexample. Check the variance identity and unbiasedness on small mixtures of Bernoulli-pair laws by complete enumeration; retain an explicit negative-estimate example. Check unsupported/empty confidence sets and any loss of conditional coverage after selection. Preserve all failures.

Use one process and one numerical thread. Benchmark the complete calculation on an excluded technical panel before development, retain frozen inputs and source identities, and refuse overwrite. Forecast development time as 3*benchmark total seconds+30 because the benchmark and development use the same numerical panel. Over 600 seconds requires a persistent detached process outside the short-lived command sandbox, one post-launch persistence check, and no automatic polling. Otherwise finish in the foreground.
