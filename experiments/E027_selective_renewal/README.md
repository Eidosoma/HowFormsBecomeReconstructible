# E027 — preserve surviving connections while rebuilding missing ones

Status: **complete; primary superiority and full qualification failed.
Independent audit and artifact closeout passed.** The frozen verdict is

Preserving every surviving nonzero weight while filling empty coordinates
performed substantially worse than full rebuilding. After three damaged
renewals, with scattered terminal damage and the local-strength receiver:

| Policy | Both-cue successes | Success, % [95% whole-block interval] |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Preserve surviving weights, fill empty coordinates | 285/512 | **55.66 [49.41, 61.72]** |
| Full strength-compensated rebuilding | 407/512 | **79.49 [74.61, 84.18]** |
| Copy with cumulative matched erasures | 284/512 | 55.47 [49.22, 61.52] |
| Selective policy with reading blocked | 284/512 | 55.47 [49.41, 61.52] |

The primary selective-minus-full difference was **−23.83 percentage points
[−28.52, −18.75]**, failing its required lower bound above zero. Selective
renewal exceeded cumulative-erasure copying by only **0.20 points [0.00, 0.59]**,
and its matched reading-blocked control by **0.20 points [−0.39, 0.78]**.
Neither secondary contrast passed its declared requirement. These small
differences do not establish equivalence or the absence of all useful infill.

Nine of thirteen additional qualification flags passed. The four failures were
third-renewal scattered both-cue reliability, the original selective gate
conjunction, the ten-point copying advantage and the positive reading-dependent
infill advantage. Selective renewal passed **22/24** original gates at horizon
three; both rule-specific scattered accuracy gates failed. All twelve retained
full-rebuilding reading/writing-dependence gates passed.

The deficit also appeared with the other terminal injuries:

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Intact | 90.63 | 95.12 | −4.49 [−7.62, −1.37] |

Full rebuilding beat cumulative-erasure copying by **24.02 points [19.34,
28.52]** on the scattered endpoint, a secondary result consistent with the
earlier copying-advantage studies. It passed all 24 original individual-cue
gates, although its stricter scattered both-cue lower bound of **74.61%** was
just below 75%. Protected copying and intact renewal retained 100% observed
under local normalization; this is not population certainty.

All four policies in the first table had 100% observed normalized both-cue
success after one renewal. Selective scattered success then declined by
**44.34 points [38.28, 50.59]** by the third renewal. Its pre-capture body success
was **100%, 99.61%, 90.63%** across generations; these body scores cannot replace
fresh-receiver outcomes. Blocking selective reading retained copied values by
design. Its square-lesion deficit to selective renewal was **1.56 points
[0.39, 2.93]**, a secondary effect that does not rescue the failed main test.

E026's favorable final hybrid does not establish that preserving every nonzero
value throughout renewal can generate that hybrid. This prospective test rejects
the claimed superiority of this specific policy. A next mechanistic lead is to
test whether newly filled connections need subsequent rewriting to mature.
The policy freezes even tiny nonzero repairs, but the present comparison does
not isolate that as the cause from other changes in the renewal histories.
No successor has been launched.

E026 found that adding restored connections to a saved copy raised scattered
success from 62% to 83%, while full rebuilding achieved 80%. Its best hybrid
borrowed connections generated by full rebuilding. This experiment tests a
policy that must generate its own connections during three actual renewals.

At every replacement, the new policy preserves each surviving nonzero incoming
weight and fills physically available exact-zero coordinates using current-body
activity. This includes copying tiny nonzero values, with no strength threshold
or transmitted ancestry mask. Weak new values may therefore remain weak until
erased; that is a declared limitation of the policy being tested. The copying
component explicitly retains incoming values, so this is a combination of
copying and local writing rather than pure activity-only recapture.

The prospective cohort used **32 fresh blocks 24000–24031**, 128 founders and
**512 lineages**. All cases were retained. The single primary compares selective
renewal with full strength-compensated rewriting on scattered-terminal both-cue
success after three damaged renewals, using the same normalized receiver.
Its paired 95% whole-block interval must have a lower bound above zero.
The full qualification also requires absolute reliability, original controls,
an advantage over cumulative-erasure copying and a contribution from reading

On 23 September, [E028's completed diagnostic](../E028_precursor_rewriting/README.md)
isolated a contribution from generation-two restored connections in these saved
full-rebuilding histories. Keeping them during third-generation reading and
refreshing their values retained 79.49% scattered-terminal success; freezing
their old values reduced this to 55.47%, and erasing them before reading reduced
it to 55.66%. Both paired benefits were about 24 points, with positive lower
bounds above ten points. The same weak target coordinates strengthened sharply
when retained, while the current body's success rate was unchanged. This
supports testing a policy that continues updating new repairs; it does not
establish such a policy or revise E027's failed prospective verdict.
