## What the pulse actually changed

Flipping the central quarter of V preserves its pointwise absolute values,
but produces a substantial displacement: mean instantaneous difference RMS
is about **99% of the field's RMS amplitude** in both families. A 25% patch
intervention should therefore not be described as a tiny physical disturbance.
Reference R, ema and theta remain identical immediately across the event.

The fixed target-minus-partner projection of V drops from **0.16761 to
0.08537** in the common LL arm, and **0.35178 to 0.17589** in residual LL.
The first recorded update partially restores it to **0.09241 / 0.19137**.
The recorded-feedback arms show very similar immediate changes. These are
native projection scores, not percentages of recovered information.

At the endpoint, residual LL V reaches **0.41772**, versus **0.33381** without
the pulse; recorded-feedback V also improves, **0.40021 versus 0.33370**.
The pulse improves residual target projection in V, R, the writer drive and
outgoing theta in every history under both routes. Common endpoint projections
decline on average at each stage, with harm in three histories and benefit in
one. Thus the different family responses are already visible upstream of the
fresh reader's categorical decision.

No saved recipient checkpoints lie between the first post-pulse update and
the endpoint. We cannot infer a recovery time, monotonic recovery, or a specific
attractor transition from these snapshots.

The experiment therefore exercised the intended difference between feedback
that follows the recipient and a prerecorded reference.

## The residual lead is visible continuously; the common lead is fragile

A reader margin is the target template score minus the highest score among
all three competitors. This differs from the designated target-minus-partner
projection gap. A positive margin identifies the winning target; its magnitude
is neither calibrated confidence nor a measure of complete form preservation.

| History | Common margin interaction | Residual margin interaction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 319100 | −0.00194897 | +0.00220341 |
| 319101 | +0.00062098 | +0.00345468 |
| 319102 | −0.00082413 | +0.00328803 |
| 319103 | −0.00094381 | +0.00197326 |
| Mean | **−0.00077398** | **+0.00272985** |
| Median across record means | −0.00074338 | +0.00227838 |

These apply the same four-cell interaction as D195 to a continuous diagnostic.
Residual interaction remains positive in both roles, both reader futures and
every history omission; common interaction is negative in both roles, both
futures and every omission. Individual residual records still include negative
margin changes (minimum −0.01079604), so recurrence is not universal benefit.
The residual target-minus-partner projection interaction is positive in three
histories and negative in one; it is less consistent than the margin result.

After the pulse, residual own feedback has a mean margin advantage of
**+0.00250871**, also positive in all four histories. Yet its original primary
preference score is **exactly tied** with recorded feedback at **90.72265625
points**, with two record gains, one harm and 61 ties. A small continuous
advantage is therefore a lead to test, not a replacement for the primary result.

Common preference interaction remains **+0.1953125 points**, entirely from
one paired draw. In that case (319101, record 15, role B, future 2, draw 0),
both routes start correct. Their margins fall from **+0.029372 / +0.023831**
to **+0.003355 / −0.005207**. Own feedback loses less ground and remains on
the correct side of the decision boundary; this is not improved absolute
reconstruction in that case. The negative common mean margin prevents treating
the single label gain as a broad improvement.

## What the changed cases show

Across all five focal comparisons, **36 raw paired reader draws** change at
least one categorical outcome: 17 common and 19 residual. Seven of these
change an own-versus-recorded comparison, including the retained harm; six
have a nonzero categorical interaction. Every changed draw's four cells,
four template scores, winner weights and margins are in the tables, alongside
the full-panel distributions. These draws are not independent founder histories.

The residual interaction combines two kinds of change. At 319100 record 14
and 319101 record 15, own-feedback draws that had lost catch up with recorded
feedback. At 319100 record 9 and 319103 record 6, own feedback ends correct
while recorded feedback remains just below the target decision boundary.
The former record has disturbed margins **+0.001092 / −0.008005**; the latter,
**+0.004058 / −0.004568**. Other competing templates, rather than the designated
partner, decide these two outcomes. This is why all four scores matter.

The residual harm at 319100 record 14, role A, future 1, draw 0 remains:
own feedback has margin **−0.024068**, recorded feedback **+0.003382**.
A positive interaction elsewhere in that record does not remove this loss.
The score crossings are much larger than the original 1e-12 tie tolerance;
they are real readout differences, while still being small and sparse.
