## Question and fixed starting points

three-to-one onward transfer. P7's simultaneous three-to-three arrangement
failed. P9 asks whether the same three teachers can teach three newcomers
one at a time, and whether the three actual newcomers can replace every
teacher for six consecutive externally arranged handovers.

Main founders are ALL three actual cells at `writing_isolated_2` in P8's
`rewrite__ordinary_E__M_open` and `rewrite__restored_E__M_open` branches.
These are the rewritten round-6 E lineage carriers, BEFORE P8's probe; the
already tested one-cell probe is not expanded or duplicated into founders.
All 17 live endpoint variables and archived cell identities are preserved.
Ordinary prepared M founders test whether acquired history is necessary;
ordinary prepared E founders are a sequential-teaching negative sham.
Do not claim an acquired-history advantage if ordinary M also succeeds.

P8's coarse historical prefixes are fixed inputs for BOTH new resolutions.
The four earlier P8 histories are not all retested: this panel focuses on
the two established lineages plus ordinary M and E comparators. Prior
results are reused ancestry, not additional independent replications.

## One sequential round: elapsed time and state continuity

Round time starts at zero (add 80,000 units per preceding round).

1. From 0 to 20,000: the three actual teachers occupy the first three
   row-major positions of a 2x2 orthogonal nonperiodic sheet; fresh E pupil
   0 occupies the fourth. Save every teacher's live final state, unreset.
2. From 20,000 to 40,000: those same teachers, with their changed states
   and unchanged ordering, contact fresh E pupil 1 in the same geometry.
   Pupil 0 meanwhile evolves in complete isolation for 20,000 units.
3. From 40,000 to 60,000: the same continuously updated teachers contact
   fresh E pupil 2. Pupils 0 and 1 meanwhile each evolve fully isolated for
   another 20,000 units. Their waiting dynamics are simulated, not frozen.
4. Remove ALL teachers. From 60,000 to 70,000 and again to 80,000, keep ALL
   three actual pupils completely isolated, with no neighbor reading or
   junction transport. Keep their fixed arrival order, never select winners.
5. If all three meet the retention rule below, they are the next round's
   teachers. No old teacher, ordinary M helper, copied successful pupil,
   resetting, or pooling across independent branches can supplement them.

Waiting and contact integrations describe separate isolated subsystems over
overlapping logical intervals. They run sequentially on the computer, not
as extra biological elapsed time. Every phase records that interval and
every handoff is checked against the full LIVE endpoint, not the last stored
sample. The prepared bath is shared as a fixed reference, not a transferred
conditioned medium. No discarded teacher needs a subsequent simulated life.

In simultaneous conditions use a 2x3 sheet, teachers first row and all three
fresh E pupils second row. Remove all teachers after contact. For 20,000-unit
contact, simulate pupils' remaining 40,000 units in complete isolation; the
60,000-unit contact needs no extra wait. Both then receive the same two
10,000-unit isolated retention holds. Thus EVERY new round ends at 80,000.
The longer simultaneous control also matches total teacher contact time.
Pupil ages/exposure histories and topology still differ; this tests a staged
protocol, not a pure timing effect or a general majority-vote law.

## Registered stopping and outcomes

A cell retains M only if it is M at the live endpoint AND in at least 95%
of late-quarter samples in BOTH final 10,000-unit isolated holds, using the
existing native two-unit E/M classifier. Report every pupil's contact,
waiting and final state, and every teacher's start/end state at each contact.
Separate failure to teach from loss during waiting or retention.

Stop a branch after its FIRST round with fewer than three retained M pupils,
or after six successful rounds. Finish all three pupils and both holds in
the failure round before stopping. Record the failed/mixed group and all
unattempted rounds. This is a preregistered stop on failure of the complete
relay, not a technical error, selection of successful cells, or evidence
that partial groups could never recover under another protocol. Apply the
same rule independently at both numerical resolutions and in every control.

Six-round renewal requires ALL three initially E pupils to acquire and retain
M in EVERY round; a successful single pupil or shrinking chain is not enough.
RNA/cut controls must fail to acquire M in round one (all pupils retained E),
and the ordinary E sham must likewise remain E. Qualification additionally
requires all relevant finer outcomes to agree. Simultaneous outcomes are
comparisons, NOT success gates: if they succeed, report that plainly rather
than requiring a special advantage for staging.
