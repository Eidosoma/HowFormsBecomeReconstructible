# P34G closeout and interpretation

P34G completed October 16 at 23:35:04 UTC in **11.11 hours**. All four
cumulative gates and the final audit passed: 46 cases, 470 phases, and 23
q128/q256 comparisons. The subsequent completion review passed all 213
integrity checks and verified the 365 registered sources, dependencies, raw
phase hashes, 24 assembly checkpoints, actual-parent links and deadlines.
The final audit and registration hashes were rechecked October 17.

The result is **context-dependent component rescue**. RNA-plus-ion substitution
retained E and transmitted E on both new sequences. Protein-only substitution
succeeded on one, but its matched control also succeeded there. On the other
sequence, both the control and protein-only arm failed, while RNA-plus-ion
and full-block substitution succeeded. Thus RNA-plus-ion substitution added
benefit in one of two new sequences; protein-only added benefit in neither.
No tested substitution worsened these two binary outcomes relative to its
matched control.

| Sequence | Open comparison | Protein only | RNA + ions | All three blocks |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| new_34001 | Retention and transmission fail | Both fail | Both succeed | Both succeed |
| new_34002 | Both succeed | Both succeed | Both succeed | Both succeed |

All four failed-parent numerical cases (two sequence/arm combinations) retained
their actual-parent transmission assays. Those assays also failed. The two
numerical resolutions were not counted as independent cases.

Cutting the protein-arm donor–recipient connection abolished transmission on
new_34002. On new_34001 that arm already failed with open contact, so its failed
cut result adds no evidence that cutting caused a loss. P34G did not cut the
successful RNA-plus-ion route; that is an explicit gap addressed in P34R.

All retained EEE parent arms transmitted successfully under their open assay.
This panel therefore does **not** establish different successor usefulness
among parents matched on the EEE label. It demonstrates a difference in which
supplied component substitutions recover retention and subsequent transmission.

P34T's known-sequence success for protein-only rescue remains valid. P34G
shows that success cannot simply be generalized to every incoming sequence.
The two prospective sequences are conditional on one ordinary-history source
bundle. They support a bounded extension, not a population success rate,
historical specificity, autonomous repair or biological efficacy. P34A remains
unqualified. The maximum primary scaled discrepancy was `5.49352388e-05`, below
the unchanged `0.01` rule; numerical agreement remains finite-resolution evidence.

The [source-model correspondence inventory](SOURCE_MODEL_CORRESPONDENCE_2026-10-16.md)
is complete as a local audit, with publication-output provenance gaps retained.
The model does not transport ZEB protein through its junctions. Local protein
or RNA state replacement must not be described as demonstrated molecular
delivery, and the publication's Tables 1–3 are not claimed to be fully reproduced.

The next diagnostic, [P34R](p34_rescue_split/PROTOCOL.md), separates RNA-only
and ion-only substitutions on the selected known sequence new_34001 and adds
a connection cut for the successful combined route. It asks whether either
single supplied block suffices under that schedule. Its outcome-informed
sequence selection is explicit and contributes **zero** new held-out cases.
All new failed parents will again receive their transmission assays.
