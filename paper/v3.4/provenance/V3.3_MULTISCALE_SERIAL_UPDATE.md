## Interpretation to retain

The advance is the connection from a supplied correction policy to an outgoing record and actual descendants. It closes the earlier absence of any integrated serial test. It does not yet supply a well-resolved conditional capacity for each starting record: one or two futures are insufficient for that purpose.

The principal controls prevent stronger readings:

- **Correcting a new injury differs from recovering old lost history.** E2E003's newborn cue and backup preserve information available before that generation's damage. Already inherited errors can be copied into them.
- **A cue is not an independent ancestor.** E2E002's normal cue is rebuilt from the incoming packet. Its separate ancestral-cue route is an extra inherited channel and must be counted as such.
- **Preservation can also preserve error.** E2E004's record-led rule keeps both the acquired history and a damaged patch. Its omitted-second-copy rule breaks the proposed universal ordering.
- **Better local repair need not preserve the whole history.** Under misread cues, E2E003's accumulated-patch score rises while its specificity contrast is slightly negative.
- **Protected start is not protected copying throughout.** E2E001's pre-injury record undergoes the same later renewal; it is an initial-condition comparator.
- **Different successors are different apparatuses.** E2E001 uses body-led renewal; E2E002 uses voting. E2E004 supplies the direct paired rule comparison.
- **Development criteria are not fresh qualifications.** The E2E cohorts were exposed previously; AR006C's within-body qualification does not transfer automatically to serial tests. E2E003's favorable departure from its equivalence band remains a failed criterion.

Historical specificity here is the native score relative to shifted alternatives, not a success probability, a bit count, or the separate E014 own-versus-foreign estimate. The new main table labels the two native scores separately.
