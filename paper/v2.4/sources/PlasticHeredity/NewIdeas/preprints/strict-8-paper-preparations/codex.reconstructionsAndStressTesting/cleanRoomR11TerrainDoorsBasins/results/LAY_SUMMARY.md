# R11 lay summary

R11 asked whether the chemistry behaves like a featureless cloud, a small shelf of reusable forms, or a structured landscape with preferred routes and sticky regions. It also separated *where states tend to go* from *how often a break happens*.

The registered verdicts are: matrix specificity `REPRODUCED`; doorway rehoming `REPRODUCED`; hold law `WORLD_LEVEL_ASSOCIATION_ONLY`; countable repertoire `NOT_REPRODUCED`; separation from matched continuous nulls `SURROGATE_NOT_ADMITTED`; overall terrain interpretation `SURROGATE_NOT_ADMITTED`; causal boundary `NOT_REPRODUCED`; independent-engine support `SEMANTICALLY_SENSITIVE`; matched-size scaling `NOT_REPRODUCED`.

A positive matrix or doorway result means the catalytic rulebook genuinely helps locate or route later states. It does **not** by itself mean nature contains a small fixed set of books. A positive countability result requires the number of forms to level off and remain stable; a separate positive null-separation result requires that the same observer not call matched continuous processes the same thing. Only the joint evidence can support a discrete-repertoire reading. A rugged-terrain result means there is reproducible structure without evidence for a short, final catalogue.

The hold test asks whether worlds whose natural post-break daughters can stay coherent are also worlds that later produce strict eight-generation episodes. The specificity and state-level tests decide whether that is a portable mechanism or mainly a world-level marker. The causal tests go further by perturbing candidate boundaries and looking for dose, duration, ignition, and hysteresis signatures.

The Rust mirror is deliberately a separate simulator implementation. Agreement supports the mechanism at the specification level; disagreement flags sensitivity to implementation details rather than being averaged away. The scaling verdict is kept separate: it asks whether apparent form capacity survives matched 2x and 4x systems and cannot overturn the core engine-conformance result.
