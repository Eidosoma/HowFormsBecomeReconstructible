## Central framing

The paper could centre on **how a possible form becomes an embodied pattern that can help sustain its own continuation**.

This gives the ruliad, bounded observer and Platonic space a shared experimental question while keeping their philosophical differences visible. The newer repair work adds a dimension to the earlier rehoming story: **the organization that emerges can influence what gets preserved and expressed next**.

Four questions organize the proposed framework:

1. Which patterns are possible?
2. Which can a particular embodiment access?
3. Which can a bounded observer distinguish?
4. Which can maintain continuity through disruption or replacement?

The proposed conceptual roles are:

| Concept | Role in the paper | Boundary to preserve |
|---|---|---|
| Ruliad | Wolfram's framing of computational possibility and observer sampling | Our finite model families are restricted analogues, not the full ruliad |
| Platonic space | Levin's proposed space of forms, goals and active or agential patterns | Its independent nonphysical existence is an interpretation, not an experimental finding here |
| Embodiment | The particular rules, body, resources, couplings and readers through which patterns act | Engineered repair machinery and retained information must be accounted for |
| Bounded observer | The scale, time window and measurement that determine which distinctions are detectable | The measuring observer is distinct from the reader that causally affects the system |
| History and plastic heredity | How acquired information influences a lineage's subsequent behaviour through change | Recurrence, copying, repair and renewal require different evidence |

Embodiment should be the centre of the narrative and the conceptual figure. The ruliad and Platonic space remain distinct framings; the figure should not imply a demonstrated physical transfer from an abstract realm.

## Draft abstract

How do possible forms become recognizable organizations that persist through disruption and replacement? Wolfram's ruliad and Levin's proposed Platonic space offer different accounts of the possibilities underlying physical and computational patterns. We examine these accounts through plastic heredity, bringing embodiment, bounded observation and lineage history into a common experimental framework.

Drawing on simulations of catalytic assemblies, cellular automata, regulatory networks and collective voltage dynamics, we distinguish four questions: which patterns are possible, which a particular embodiment can access, which an observer can distinguish, and which can maintain continuity through change. Our studies identify bounded examples in which measurement choices alter detected continuity, spatial references guide restoration, and reciprocal feedback between a body and a damaged record improves the record's subsequent expression in a fresh embodiment. These findings motivate an operational notion of active form: an embodied organization whose activity contributes to maintaining or reconstructing the conditions for its later expression.

We distinguish recurrence, copying, repair and renewal, and examine how corrective information is distributed across records, bodies and their readers. The results support specific mechanisms of embodied pattern maintenance while leaving the proposed independent existence and agency of Platonic patterns unresolved. This framework connects questions about the space of possible forms to experimentally tractable questions about persistence, collective organization and the requirements for continuous learning.

**Editorial note:** This is a synthesis-paper abstract. A paper centred on a smaller set of new experiments would name those systems and their principal quantitative results more precisely. The final scope and claim wording should follow the current audits of the experiments selected for inclusion.

## Possible section structure

### 1. Introduction: how does a pattern continue?

Start with a concrete problem: an organization survives damage or replacement even though its components and immediate state change. Introduce plastic heredity and the question of what, exactly, continues.

### 2. The ruliad, Platonic space and bounded observation

Explain the distinct proposals. Wolfram supplies a framing of computational possibility and observer dependence; Levin asks whether patterns themselves can be causally instructive or agential. Establish where our experiments can inform that discussion.

The updated four-concept infographic could become a starting point for the paper's conceptual figure.

### 3. Embodiment: rules, bodies, records and readers

Make embodiment the central addition. Define the machinery that realizes a pattern and the history that conditions its behaviour. Clearly distinguish the **reader that affects the system** from the **observer that measures it**.

### 4. An experimental framework for pattern continuity

Define recurrence, copying, repair, renewal and rehoming. Explain how interventions—damage, scrambling, donor swaps, reader removal and fresh-body tests—identify what carries information and what uses it.

### 5. Finding forms: accessibility, rehoming and observer dependence

Present the strongest evidence that available forms, routes into them and detected identity are different properties. Include the limits of cross-system atlases and the dependence of some observer results on scale.

The [L04 observer audit](codex.lawsConfirmation/laws/L04_two_key_compressibility.md) and [L03 atlas audit](codex.lawsConfirmation/laws/L03_one_catalogue_many_doors.md) provide bounded starting points. A shared representation must not be promoted automatically to a universal natural catalogue.

### 6. Active form: restoration and reciprocal repair

Make this the experimental centrepiece: spatial references guiding restoration, body–record feedback improving later readout, and surviving organization contributing corrective information.

The central process is:

**Record → embodied organization → repaired record → expression in a fresh embodiment.**

Keep one successful recovery episode distinct from sustained renewal. Use the source controls to identify where the corrective information resides.

### 7. What kind of space do these patterns inhabit?

Explore alternative interpretations: dynamical possibilities, classes of behaviour distinguished by experiments, historically acquired organizations, and Levin's stronger Platonic interpretation. Ask whether these interpretations yield different predictions.

An implemented computational mechanism does not by itself refute Levin's interpretation. Conversely, a pattern's causal contribution to repair does not establish an independently active nonphysical entity.

### 8. Toward collective intelligence and continuous learning

Develop the forward-looking argument: how might a collective preserve useful organization while changing its components, readers or strategies? Present flexible agency and cumulative learning as further capabilities to explain.

Collective cell-state control is a longer-term motivation. The present simulations should not be presented as demonstrations of biological treatments.

### 9. Conclusion: the conditions for continuation

State the bounded contribution and the remaining conceptual gap between restoring an organization and an organization flexibly pursuing its own continuation.

## Speculative thread to keep exploring

The most interesting transition may be **from preserving a pattern to preserving a capacity**.

A future collective might retain its ability to repair, learn or coordinate while substantially changing the form through which it does so. This would extend the discussion from static forms toward **patterns of competence**.

Questions for later brainstorming:

- What would count as the same competence across different bodies or rulebooks?
- When does reciprocal maintenance become flexible agency?
- Can an organization change its strategy while retaining an acquired goal or useful capability?
- What distinguishes continuous learning from indefinitely preserving an old record?
- Which interventional tests could discriminate among competing accounts of pattern identity and activity?

These are open questions and possible interpretations, not claims established by the current repair experiments.

## Follow-up discussion: capacities, goal-directedness and agency

**Recorded:** 2026-09-11. These sections capture the three subsequent conversations: connecting agency and capacities to our research; examining goal-directedness and its gaps; and explaining the origins of a system's “wants” in simple terms. They are exploratory interpretations of existing work. No new simulation or independent raw-data reanalysis was performed for this discussion.

### Capacities as the bridge

**Capacities could be the bridge between persistent patterns and stronger questions about agency.** The focus shifts toward what an organization remains able to do as its state, components and surroundings change.

| Idea | Working meaning |
|---|---|
| **Capacity** | What a system can reliably do across specified conditions: restore, discriminate, learn, coordinate or reproduce an effect |
| **Goal-directedness** | How its responses help maintain or reach an outcome despite disturbance |
| **Agency** | How it organizes its interactions in pursuing outcomes, including the flexibility and autonomy of that activity |

Goals can be supplied, learned or maintained internally. Their origin is a separate question from how effectively a system pursues them. An externally supplied goal can still be pursued competently; an elaborate internal model does not by itself establish competent pursuit.

Levin's [TAME framework](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/systems-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnsys.2022.768201/full) treats homeostatic feedback as a minimal component of agency. Stronger accounts of autonomous agency, such as [Barandiaran, Di Paolo and Rohde](https://ezequieldipaolo.net/wp-content/uploads/2011/10/defining-agency.pdf), additionally ask whether a system maintains its own organization and regulates interactions according to conditions that matter to its continuation. These are complementary interpretive lenses rather than a single agreed definition.

For this project, a profile of capacities is more useful than assuming one scalar level of agency. The [L06 audit](codex.lawsConfirmation/reviews/L06_RETAINED_REPLAY_AUDIT_2026-09-02.md) supports a bounded pooled correlation between two measurements, but not the claimed single latent competence axis.

### A memory can become a target for present activity

E49 provides the clearest example discussed here. A slow spatial memory of the simulated sheet's previous state helped restore a displaced configuration. The restoration benefit appeared in all 12 tested tissues and largely disappeared when the reference coupling was disabled.

**Remembered organization can supply a target for present activity.** This is a bounded example of goal-directed restoration. Whether the system can preserve that target while changing how it reaches it is a further question.

The [E49 audit](codex.lawsConfirmation/reviews/L09_RETAINED_AUDIT_2026-09-02.md) reports mean profile recovery of .539 with the reference, versus approximately -.022 in the passive arm and -.025 with reference coupling disabled. These are model-specific recovery scores, not biological repair probabilities.

### Experience can change readiness to respond

In E33, prior stimulation increased the probability of a later rewrite from approximately **18.2% to 24.1%**. Fourteen of sixteen tissue configurations had a positive difference. E47 later reproduced a positive absolute contrast under its own weaker criterion; it did not rescue the original compound threshold.

The supported interpretation is **history-dependent priming**. The experiment does not establish that the changed response was adaptive learning.

A useful distinction for the paper is:

> A changed response becomes evidence of learning a useful capacity when it improves performance on a relevant subsequent challenge.

Source: [L07 history-dependence audit](codex.lawsConfirmation/reviews/L07_RETAINED_AUDIT_2026-09-02.md).

### Inheritance can help a new learner acquire a capacity

DoorKey supplies a concrete example. A small inherited record favoured certain early actions. Those actions changed the learner's experience, and a learned benefit remained after the advice was removed.

Matched controls reproduced the effect with an external controller supplying exactly the same state-conditioned action additions. The supported mechanism is therefore **early action guidance influencing later learning**. It is not a separately demonstrated inherited learning rule or learning disposition.

The reliable benefit concerned finding and opening the door. Final goal completion remained sparse and mixed. The later learned-consequence reader failed its primary behavioural criterion, and the common-state continuation diagnostic did not establish a replicated advantage in post-door navigation.

The positive conceptual connection remains:

> A lineage can pass on information that helps a descendant develop a capacity through its own experience.

Sources: [64-byte carrier confirmation](codex.lawsConfirmation/reviews/DOORKEY_VERTICAL_MULTIFOUNDER_CONFIRMATION_2026-09-04.md), [matched-behaviour audit](codex.lawsConfirmation/reviews/DOORKEY_MATCHED_BEHAVIOR_DISPOSITION_2026-09-04.md), [learned-consequence result](codex.lawsConfirmation/reviews/DOORKEY_CONSEQUENCE_READER_BEHAVIOR_2026-09-07.md), [common-state continuation](codex.lawsConfirmation/reviews/DOORKEY_COMMON_STATE_CONTINUATION_2026-09-07.md).

### A capacity may include maintaining the information that enables it

The D073/D074 results summarized above show that reciprocal body–record interaction can improve the usefulness of a damaged record in fresh-body readout, and that preserving surviving spatial organization contributes to this improvement.

Our proposed interpretation is **maintaining the conditions of future competence**. One recovery episode gives a foothold for this idea; it does not demonstrate sustained maintenance of a capacity through repeated renewal.

### Three directions for collective intelligence and continuous learning

1. **Identity through competence.** A collective could change shape or membership while retaining its ability to repair, coordinate or learn. Its responses to challenges might capture continuity better than resemblance alone.
2. **Coordination as a capacity.** Coupling placement affects which combined patterns the tested models can express. Weakening particular joins reduced interference relative to matched internal placements in the bounded L23 comparisons. Autonomous regulation of those relationships remains an open step. See the [matched edge-phase audit](codex.lawsConfirmation/reviews/L23_EDGE_PHASE_CONTROL_2026-09-10.md).
3. **Learning while preserving competence.** Continuous learning would involve acquiring new useful responses while retaining machinery for interpreting records, coordinating activity and recovering from disruption. Current studies demonstrate pieces of this picture rather than the integrated capability.

The relevant space of possibilities could contain **ways of doing things**—repair strategies, learning dispositions and coordination policies—as well as recognizable forms. Embodiment determines which can be realized; bounded observers test particular aspects of those capacities. This interpretation does not establish a nonphysical origin for them.

Possible new section title:

**From Pattern Persistence to the Maintenance of Capacities**

Its exploratory question:

> Can an embodied organization preserve and extend what it is able to do, while changing the form through which it does it?

## Going deeper into goal-directedness: what we know and what is missing

### Current evidential position

We have credible evidence for **goal-directed regulation in engineered models**. Evidence is thinner for systems that **change their means while preserving a goal**, and thinner again for establishing, revising or collectively maintaining their own goals.

Levin and Resnik's [Mind Everywhere, Part Two](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13196611/) motivates an experimental standard: a goal-directed interpretation should improve prediction, interaction or discovery of capabilities. It should earn its place through useful tests.

| Aspect | Evidence discussed | Bounded interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Remembered configuration guides correction | E49 reference coupling improves spatial restoration | Feedback restoring an earlier organization |
| Control depends on current state | E16 inner pilot selects rule variants according to composition | An early construction of minimal self-regulation, with a policy optimized separately for each task |
| Information influences action | FEP–PH1.8 informative sensing helps relative to matched planner controls | Information and contingent decisions work, but the tested planner is weaker overall than simple reactive feedback |
| Conditions for later expression are repairable | D073/D074 body–record interaction | A possible support for continued competence, demonstrated over one recovery episode |

E49 and D073/D074 have retained audit support. For E16, this conversation inspected the implementation, expectations and historical discovery log rather than a comparable independent outcome audit. Sources: [E16 expectations](inner_pilot/EXPECTATIONS.md), [implementation](inner_pilot/src/inner_pilot/pipeline.py), [discovery log, Experiment 16](DISCOVERY_LOG_EIDOSOMA_SCIENTIST.md).

### Gap 1: correcting the body versus changing what counts as correct

Let the body be `x`, its remembered target be `g`, and the measured error be their difference. Error can shrink because:

- the body moves toward its target;
- the target changes toward the body; or
- both change toward another configuration.

Only the first straightforwardly demonstrates restoration of the original target. The others could represent adaptation, forgetting or drift; interpretation depends on subsequent behaviour and the relevant function.

The coupled body–reference models contain exactly this ambiguity. The [L15 timescale audit](codex.lawsConfirmation/reviews/L15_TIMESCALE_AUDIT_2026-09-02.md) shows why shrinking body–reference mismatch does not identify which variable moved. Its simplified comparator/reference analysis is not a solution of the full nonlinear model, but it exposes the measurement problem.

**What would close the gap:** follow the body, original target and evolving reference separately, then test what the system subsequently defends. A target change should be distinguished from both successful restoration and simple loss of the old information.

Deeper question:

> When should an organization repair itself, and when should it revise its expectations?

### Gap 2: convergence versus achievement

Resemblance, target occupancy and convergence between trajectories are useful observables, but they do not all measure the same outcome.

E36's equifinality implementation measures whether texture summaries from different perturbations become more similar to one another. Endpoint convergence alone does not establish return to the intended target. See [E36 measurement code](stress_wire/src/stress_wire/pipeline.py).

Likewise, recognizing a target message from a fresh body can succeed without reconstructing fine spatial anatomy. Recognition and anatomy should remain separate endpoints.

**What would close the gap:** specify the target before the challenge and measure success directly, alongside similarity and convergence. For a future functional collective, success could mean restoring an operation even when precise shape changes.

This is where capacities offer outcomes beyond resemblance.

### Gap 3: changing means when the usual response fails

This is the largest gap in **flexible goal pursuit**.

The early E16 inner pilot recovered target occupancy after several new initial distributions. Its path statistic, however, tracked only total probability in the target set. Similar occupancy curves cannot establish that the underlying trajectories followed one route. The historical “one canalized route” interpretation should therefore be kept narrower than a claim about complete physical or state-space paths.

Conversely, different-looking trajectories do not establish intelligent compensation; noise can generate diversity. A failed multi-path threshold is not proof that all minimal regulation is absent.

Later audits exposed related problems: activity maps were treated as repair paths, and the trap's “escape” endpoint could register success in a non-moving control. See the [L12 route audit](codex.lawsConfirmation/reviews/L12_RETAINED_REPLAY_AUDIT_2026-09-02.md) and [L11 trap audit](codex.lawsConfirmation/reviews/L11_RETAINED_REPLAY_AUDIT_2026-09-02.md).

**What would close the gap:** hold the goal fixed, obstruct a previously useful action or route, and determine whether the system compensates through another available means.

Following a common route can still be goal-directed. The stronger question is what happens when that route becomes unavailable.

### Gap 4: appropriate regulation of corrective effort

Earlier work asked whether activity stopped when repair was complete. The [L10 audit](codex.lawsConfirmation/reviews/L10_RETAINED_REPLAY_AUDIT_2026-09-02.md) found that the apparent timing relationships did not establish this: some activity persisted beyond observation, and some settling preceded measured arrival.

The relevant criterion is **appropriate regulation of effort**, not the cessation of all activity. Maintaining a state may require continued work.

**What would close the gap:** show that corrective activity changes with remaining error, diminishes when extra correction is unnecessary, and resumes after another disturbance. Preserve event order and distinguish an unobserved stopping time from an observed one.

### Gap 5: useful revision versus damage

Goal-directed learning needs a basis for deciding which changes should become future targets or instructions.

The D090 L24 follow-up is informative. A lingering reference continued feeding an ended signal into the written record. Replacing it reduced carry-over from a brief pulse, but also reduced transmission from the sustained signal. The intervention changed memory persistence; it did not selectively preserve useful learning while rejecting damage.

Source: [recovery-reference study](codex.lawsConfirmation/reviews/L24_RECOVERY_REFERENCE_2026-09-11.md). This is a controlled signal-history result; “useful” should not be generalized beyond the supplied task or treated as a value judgment made by the system itself.

**What would close the gap:** identify a mechanism that selects updates according to relevant consequences for function, external feedback or learned context. Autonomous valuation has not been established by the current results.

### Gap 6: the scale at which a goal is maintained

Spatially distributed correction is a useful beginning for collective intelligence. A stronger result would show that **the collective preserves a function by changing the contributions of its parts**.

Illustrative future case: a model collective loses members, the remaining members alter their roles, group operation is preserved, and replacements are later incorporated. The goal concerns function across those changes rather than fixed coordinates for every component.

The current patterned-sheet and coupling results do not establish that integrated capability. We also need to locate how much corrective information enters through predefined spatial references or external evaluation.

**What would close the gap:** demonstrate compensation across parts, identify the communication enabling it, and show that the maintained outcome belongs to the collective level being claimed.

### Cross-cutting gap: workable actions and effective use of information

The system must have actions that can improve the chosen outcome within the available resources and time. Otherwise, a failure cannot cleanly identify a deficit in sensing, inference or goal pursuit.

FEP–PH1.6's privileged but bounded rollout planner did not find a general advantage in the tested action interface and objective. This does not prove that no useful policy exists. See the [FEP–PH1.6 final report](../fep-ideas/EXPERIMENT_FEP_PH16_FINAL_REPORT.md).

FEP–PH1.8 supplies a more specific separation. Informative sensing and observation-contingent decisions activated. The epistemic planner improved inherited completion over its matched utility planner by .02059, but was worse than passive-cue reactive control by .08577. Four of eight strict gates passed; the overall hypothesis remained unsupported. See the [FEP–PH1.8 final report](../fep-ideas/EXPERIMENT_FEP_PH18_FINAL_REPORT.md).

The lesson is that **having information, a target and a model is separate from using them effectively**. These are results about engineered controllers in reconstructed GARD, not intrinsic GARD inference or a biological demonstration of agency.

### Central unresolved claim for the paper

**Preserving a goal while changing the means** could be the central unresolved claim, coupled with distinguishing genuine repair from target drift.

> Can an embodied organization retain a meaningful target, compensate when its usual methods fail, and preserve that competence through change?

These gap-closing ideas are discussion points, not registered protocols or authorization to launch experiments.

## In simple terms: where do a system's “wants” and agency come from?

**In our experiments, what a system “wants” comes from its organization and history. Its agency comes from its ability to act toward that outcome when conditions change.** This is a functional shorthand for the systems in which regulation has been demonstrated, not an attribution of felt desire to every recurrent pattern.

There are several ways a target gets there:

- **We supply it:** a reward, desired pattern or training objective.
- **It inherits it:** a record influences what a new embodiment tends to do. That record may contain action advice rather than an explicit goal representation.
- **Its own past becomes the reference:** an earlier configuration is remembered and helps guide recovery.

The voltage-sheet experiment makes the third case tangible. The body is disturbed, but a memory of its earlier pattern remains. Feedback between that memory and the current body drives restoration. In that limited sense, the system “wants” to recover its earlier organization: **the remembered pattern gives its activity a direction**.

Agency concerns how much the system can accomplish from there. Can it correct a disturbance, compensate for missing parts, or find another method when its usual response fails? We have evidence for some correction and repair. Flexible pursuit remains a major gap.

For living systems, a deeper possibility is that **some conditions matter because the system needs them to keep functioning**. Evolution, development and experience can shape mechanisms that maintain those conditions. A collective's goals could arise through relationships among cells that make them correct disturbances to a shared function. This is a useful hypothesis for our work; our simulations have not demonstrated that whole chain.

Levin adds a further hypothesis: patterns of agency have an independent reality in a structured Platonic space. Our experiments help explain how particular patterns become expressed, remembered and causally effective within embodiments. They do not establish that proposed independent source. Conversely, identifying a computational implementation does not by itself disprove the interpretation.

The especially interesting possibility for this research is:

> A system whose ongoing activity helps preserve—and eventually improve—its own ability to act.
