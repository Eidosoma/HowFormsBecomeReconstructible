## Addition to the discussion

Section 8.1 adds a short comparison with Hazan and Levin's *Computational Framework for Identifying Ion Channel Mutation-Compensating Interventions*, bioRxiv version 1 posted 25 September 2026, DOI [10.64898/2026.09.20.753002](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.20.753002). The same reference is added to the main bibliography, `references.bib` and the literature map. The [source record](V2.0_COMPENSATION_SOURCE.json) identifies the locally inspected PDF.

The paragraph makes three points:

- Conductance compensation can recover a measured firing response without restoring the original configuration; the Kv7.2 mutant M-current remains unchanged in the cited model.
- Recovery on that assay does not establish equivalence under new challenges or persistence after the intervention is withdrawn.
- Our acquired-record, reader–writer and replacement experiments address complementary questions about later reconstruction. Together, the approaches motivate testing present performance and subsequent recovery separately.

The comparison is a computational precedent, not evidence that the cited study demonstrates acquired-record inheritance, reciprocal repair, autonomous maintenance or therapeutic efficacy. It uses the explicit Kv7.2 case rather than extending the stronger channel-exclusion claim to the R859C analysis. No global priority claim is added.
