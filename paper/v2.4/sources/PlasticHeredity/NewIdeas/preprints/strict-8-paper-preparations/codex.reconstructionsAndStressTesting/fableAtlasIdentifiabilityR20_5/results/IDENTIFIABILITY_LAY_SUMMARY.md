# R20.5 lay summary

The strongest clue is now confirmed descriptively: most supposedly different biological destination maps point to almost the same place. For HIGH traffic that could be evaluated against all three registered wrong maps, 97% in T2-02 and 99% in T2-03 collided with at least one wrong map. HIGH and NOOP overlap heavily, as do HIGH and the foreign, LOW, homogeneous, and permuted programmes. The artificial fixed-target map remains completely distinct, so the measurement can tell destinations apart when the simulation actually supplies different destinations.

This means Stage B was asked to identify which nearly identical map a descendant followed. Its failure is real, but it does not distinguish 'no matrix influence' from 'several matrix programmes share the same composition-level endpoint.' The small HIGH coherence effect may concern convergence speed or trajectory rather than destination identity.

The overlap remains at the nearby 0.88 and 0.92 thresholds and becomes nearly complete in the enriched cohort. One narrower HIGH-versus-NOOP interval was heterogeneous across T2-03 matrix pairs, so the formal descriptive label remains MIXED rather than being upgraded.

This is a post-hoc explanation, not a rescue of the failed rehoming claim. The best next registered test remains Stage C's strict-8 acquisition and maintenance experiment, followed by a future round that demands separable maps before testing routing.
