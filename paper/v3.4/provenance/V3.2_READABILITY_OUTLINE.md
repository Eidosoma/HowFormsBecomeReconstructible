## Reader's route

The broad question is how forms arise, acquire history and persist through change. The experimental route distinguishes a return caused by shared dynamics from a return that depends on acquired information, then asks what bodies do to that information and how its future usefulness can be measured and improved.

## Writing decisions

- Keep the title, four chapters, all twenty main figures and five SI figures. Preserve image bytes, identifiers, ordering and selection rules.
- Keep a broad abstract opening, approximately 250 words overall, with the pruning reversal, distinct one-read and eighteen-rebuild repair effects, and the numerical/generalization boundary. Leave the new-study inventory to the chapters.
- Introduce a simple account of the whole argument before its detailed parts. Move the long boundary and claims inventories to the end of the main narrative; retain the conditional capacity definition in the early framework.
- Make measured-figure titles state supported findings. Keep schematic and saved-example titles distinct from inferential claims.
- Organize each chapter around a question, decisive evidence, material counterevidence and a transition. In section 4.1, use the order information source → spatial conditions → writing time, rather than campaign chronology.
- Give decisions about when to rewrite their own section 6.3. Repeated-future capacity becomes 6.4 and candidate theory becomes 6.5. Update references in the current manuscript and source guide.
- Retain failures that change the interpretation: copying advantages, finite-resolution loss, wrong-history acceptance, harms and unresolved extra value of ongoing rewriting. Detailed development history remains in the unchanged SI and ledger.
- Preserve numerical estimates, uncertainty, inference units, information boundaries and qualification decisions. No scientific reanalysis or new evidence intake is part of this pass.
