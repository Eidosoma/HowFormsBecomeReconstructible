## One rule, two controls

The candidate **cue_hysteresis** has response feedback off on the first query
sweep and full strength thereafter. At a response coordinate let s be the
current bit, u the current context-plus-query field, and h the full ordinary
update field. The ordinary decision holds at |h| <= 1e-12. After the first
sweep, veto an otherwise proposed flip when s*u > 1e-12 and
`-s*h - abs(u) <= 1e-12`. Thus a cue-aligned bit requires feedback exceeding
twice the input-field magnitude to flip, apart from the stated tolerance.
A bit opposing its input keeps the ordinary flip rule. This asymmetry may
preserve a repair after it has overcome the cue, but can also retain errors.
The barrier multiplier is fixed at **one**, without a threshold sweep or fit.

Two predeclared controls distinguish selectivity from generic weakening:

- **half_feedback**: first sweep off, response block coefficient 0.5 thereafter,
  with the ordinary update rule.
- **all_hysteresis**: first sweep off, full feedback thereafter, with the same
  `-s*h - abs(u) <= 1e-12` veto for every proposed response flip, irrespective
  of input alignment.

Context updates remain ordinary. Each policy receives only the learned record,
current state, observed query clamp and committed order. No target, correctness
label, future state, fitted predictor, ancestral archive or extra observation
enters a reader packet. All policies share preparation. The three interventions
share the delayed/off first sweep; always-on keeps its original first sweep.
Zero/one-sweep intervention views use the identical delayed endpoints.
Vetoes hold a state and cannot increase the current symmetric-record energy;
switch work, clamp work and neural energy changes remain separate.
