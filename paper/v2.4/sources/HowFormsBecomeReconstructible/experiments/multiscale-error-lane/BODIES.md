## 0. Orientation: the model in brief

A 16x16 noisy bistable sheet (Allen-Cahn/Ising-like, conserved mean) holds a pattern only if it reads a slow stored reference R through a correction gain, with sensors and actuators acting at site, region (4x4) or sheet (quadrant) resolution. The six findings mapped below are numbered as in the brief: (1) a setpoint helps only where native physics forgets; (2) every corrector has a scale; (3) majority votes make the sensing scale decisive; (4) rewriting needs provenance; (5) copying beats capture and protected copies win; (6) the planned next steps.

## 1. A setpoint helps only where native physics forgets

The model's sign change with noise predicts that a body with weak self-maintenance should keep reading a protected record, while one with strong self-maintenance should stop, because from then on the record can only add error.

**Chromatin memory with and without a DNA record.** Laprell, Finkl and Müller (2017) inserted Polycomb Response Element (PRE) DNA beside a reporter, producing an extended H3K27me3 domain and silencing. Excising the PRE let the mark dilute with each replication until silencing was lost; in replication-stalled cells it persisted. Their conclusion: the mark carries a sequence-independent memory, but marking new nucleosomes needs PRC2 recruited to PRE DNA. Coleman and Struhl (2017) showed the other half: marked nucleosomes can keep a HOX gene OFF through several replications even when copying the mark to new nucleosomes is reduced or abolished. The chromatin body holds for a few cycles, a memory time set by dilution; holding longer requires the DNA record. **Supported** (two labs, consistent). In fission yeast the noise term is directly visible. Audergon et al. (2015) and Ragunathan, Jih and Moazed (2015) tethered the methyltransferase Clr4 and released it: wild-type cells actively removed the mark, but with the putative demethylase Epe1 inactivated the state persisted through many mitotic divisions and through meiosis with no sequence-specific initiator. With turnover the loop forgets and needs its record; without turnover the record is unnecessary. **Supported** (two independent groups).

**Switches that stop needing the signal.** In *Xenopus* oocyte maturation, p42 MAPK/Cdc2 positive feedback turns a transient stimulus into an irreversible state, and blocking the feedback makes the response transient (Xiong and Ferrell, 2003). **Established** as a principle; **supported** here. Memory time is set by noise-driven escape: in the yeast galactose network only the Gal3p loop gave two stable states, and raising the inhibitor Gal80p converted unstable into persistent memory by lowering the single-cell switching rate (Acar, Becskei and van Oudenaarden, 2005). **Supported.** The *lac* system agrees with a caveat: induction is all-or-none at the single-cell level (Novick and Weiner, 1957) and the hysteretic phase diagram was mapped with the non-metabolizable inducer TMG (Ozbudak et al., 2004), but secondary reviews note bistability was not shown with lactose itself. **Supported, condition-dependent.** In the vertebrate neural tube, cross-repressive transcription factors reading the Sonic Hedgehog gradient render cells insensitive to fluctuations in signalling and confer hysteresis (Balaskas et al., 2012), which is the design the model recommends for a self-holding body. **Supported** for the hysteresis; **analogy** for the link to the sign change.

**A working controller defending a wrong reference.** Inactivating mutations in the calcium-sensing receptor cause familial hypocalciuric hypercalcemia, and the homozygous form neonatal severe hyperparathyroidism (Pollak et al., 1993). The loop still works but defends a higher value, and harm grows with the size of the error. **Established** (mechanism); **analogy** (the regime dependence is untested here).

**A homeostat with nothing to damage.** Bacteria hold a stable size distribution with no stored target: under the adder rule they add a roughly constant increment between birth and division whatever their birth size, so deviations shrink over generations (Taheri-Araghi et al., 2015). **Supported.** This is the regime in which the model says a setpoint is unnecessary; Section 8 returns to how many physiological "setpoints" may be of this kind.

## 2. Every corrector has a scale

**Pooled sensors restore pooled quantities, not shape.** After two-thirds partial hepatectomy, the model since Higgins and Anderson (1931), the liver is restored by growth of the remaining lobes rather than by rebuilding those removed (Michalopoulos and DeFrances, 1997); what returns is the liver-to-body-weight ratio, Michalopoulos's (2017) "hepatostat". The sensed variable is pooled, so lobe shape is below the sensor's resolution and is neither defended nor repaired. **Established.** Metcalf's grafts separate two scales: with several thymus grafts in one mouse, graft number did not affect how large each grew, each following its own surviving tissue (Metcalf, 1963), while several spleen grafts in splenectomised mice levelled off near the mass of one normal spleen (Metcalf, 1964). The thymus carries a local record of its own size; the spleen is regulated by a body-wide pooled sensor. **Supported** (classic, widely cited, old). A coarse actuator can also buy a local corrector time: damaged *Drosophila* imaginal discs induce the secreted peptide Dilp8, which delays developmental timing, and silencing *dilp8* rescues the delay (Colombani, Andersen and Léopold, 2012). **Supported.** The model has no such element.

**A coarse record steers; local self-organization builds the detail.** Green and Sharpe (2015) set out how positional information and reaction-diffusion combine. In the digits, progressively reducing distal Hox dose on a *Gli3*-null background gave increasingly severe polydactyly with thinner, more densely packed digits, which their model ties to Hox dose setting the wavelength of a Turing-type mechanism (Sheth et al., 2012): the coarse code sets a parameter, the local system makes the elements. In planarians the coordinate system sits in another tissue: position control genes are expressed in nearly all body-wall muscle cells, their expression changes after injury even without stem cells, and neoblasts build what the coordinates specify (Witchley et al., 2013). **Supported.**

**Boundaries are where the reference matters; coupling cleans up small errors.** In the zebrafish neural tube, noisy Shh responses leave progenitors intermixed and cell sorting then forms sharp domains, with ectopically induced motor neuron progenitors moving to correct positions (Xiong et al., 2013) - the body's coupling filtering fine reference error, here by movement rather than diffusion. **Supported.** The polar coordinate model (French, Bryant and Bryant, 1976) explains appendage regeneration by shortest intercalation: small positional confrontations are smoothed by local growth, while large ones generate supernumerary structures. **Supported** (phenomenological, with a large grafting literature; the molecular basis is incomplete). Deliberate coarse change does move patterns: raising or lowering Bicoid shifts anterior anlagen (Driever and Nüsslein-Volhard, 1988). **Established.** But natural variation is filtered: the Bicoid gradient varies considerably between embryos while the *hunchback* boundary is far more precise and scales with embryo size, and genes known to interact directly with Hunchback did not account for the precision (Houchmandzadeh, Wieschaus and Leibler, 2002). **Supported** - and partly a contradiction of the model, taken up in Section 8.

**Hierarchical predictive coding.** Rao and Ballard (1999) proposed that feedback carries predictions of lower-level activity and feedforward connections carry residual errors; a hierarchical network trained on natural images reproduced end-stopping and other extra-classical receptive-field effects. Each level corrects at the scale of its receptive fields, as the model's site, region and sheet routes do. **Analogy**: the framework is influential, but how far neurophysiology supports its distinctive claims is contested (Walsh et al., 2020).

## 3. With a majority vote, the sensing scale decides

**Cells digitize; tissues read proportions.** In *Xenopus* oocytes the MAPK cascade responds like a cooperative enzyme with a Hill coefficient of at least 35, from intrinsic ultrasensitivity plus positive feedback: the population looks graded, each oocyte is all-or-none (Ferrell and Machleder, 1998). Plants use this for a memory: localized Polycomb nucleation switches *FLC* OFF cell by cell, and the tissue-level memory of cold is the fraction of switched cells (Angel et al., 2011). This is the model's site-sensor vote, with a proportion as the coarse readout. **Supported.**

**Coarse votes make individual actuators irrelevant.** Autoinducer concentration rises with cell density, and at a threshold concentration gene expression changes across the population (Miller and Bassler, 2001): a sign test on a pooled variable gives every cell inside the pooling volume the same verdict. **Established.** Gurdon's (1988) community effect is the blindness that comes with it: *Xenopus* muscle precursors must be in a group of many nearby cells to maintain tissue-specific expression and differentiate together, so a lone precursor, a target finer than the pooling scale, is not supported. **Supported** (the effect is documented; the mechanism less so).

**The pooling scale is set by transport, not by a label.** In the model, sensor scale is a design choice. Hense et al. (2007) argued that whether an autoinducer reports population density, the diffusive confinement of one cell, or the spatial arrangement of cells in a microcolony depends on diffusion and mixing, and that arrangement can matter more than density; West et al. (2012) replied that the key question is instead whether the trait is social. Both accept that one molecular sensor can integrate over very different spatial scales in different environments. A biological vote can therefore change scale with no genetic change, because the medium changed. **Supported** (theoretical, partly backed, framing debated).

**The dose caveat transfers.** Biological all-or-none switches combine ultrasensitivity with amplification. As in the model, crediting a vote over graded correction requires holding the total corrective drive equal. **Analogy** (methodological).

## 4. Provenance makes rewriting safe

A mismatch says only that body and record disagree, not which moved. The model's arbiter rewrote R only when pooled mismatch appeared without recent pooled body surprise, and matched or beat a privileged oracle; without the surprise gate the same rule copied body damage into the record.

**Mismatch repair is the textbook case.** After replication a mismatch is a disagreement between the old strand (the record) and the new one, and correcting it helps only if the system knows which is new. In *E. coli*, Dam methylates GATC sites some time after replication, so the new strand is briefly unmethylated. Lu, Clark and Modrich (1983) reconstituted methyl-directed repair in extracts: it depended on the methylation pattern and on MutH, MutL, MutS and UvrD (UvrE), with repair synthesis on the unmethylated strand. **Established.** Destroying the signal makes repair a mutation source: a strain overproducing Dam about 50-fold, methylating new strands too early, was hypermutable, and losing the excess enzyme restored normal rates (Herman and Modrich, 1981); a reported 10-fold overproduction did not, so the effect is dose-dependent. This is the model's provenance lesion - the machinery still runs, but with a blurred strand signal it writes errors into the template as often as it removes them. **Supported.** Eukaryotes appear to read transient features of the nascent strand instead: inactivating RNase H2, which nicks at ribonucleotides incorporated during replication, preferentially reduced repair of leading-strand errors in yeast (Lujan et al., 2013), and in human extracts a single ribonucleotide near a mismatch could serve as an initiation site (Ghodgaonkar et al., 2013). **Supported.** Two details sharpen the mapping: the mark is time-limited, like the model's latch, and different repair systems handle different damage scales, so "the trigger must know the damage's scale" is built into a division of labour.

**Copying from the wrong source.** When a cell repairs using the homologue instead of the identical sister, it can copy a mutant allele over a good one. Cavenee et al. (1983) showed that recessive alleles at the retinoblastoma locus are unmasked by chromosomal mechanisms that eliminate the chromosome carrying the wild-type allele. **Established.**

**A tunable guard on the record.** Epe1 removes H3K9 methylation from sites lacking a sequence-specific initiator, which Audergon et al. (2015) describe as preventing unauthorized inheritance of heterochromatin. Caffeine downregulates Epe1 and induces a short isoform of the acetyltransferase Mst2; heterochromatin then forms at new loci, silencing genes and producing caffeine-resistant epimutants that are cross-resistant to antifungal agents and lose resistance within a few generations without caffeine (Torres-Garcia et al., 2020). The guard against capturing body states into a heritable record is tunable by environment: stress lowers it, and what is written is unstable once the stress ends. **Supported.**

**The efference copy is a provenance signal.** Animals send copies of motor commands to sensory structures, letting the nervous system attribute self-caused sensory change to itself (Crapse and Sommer, 2008). The logic mirrors the arbiter's: the model reads body surprise as "the body moved, do not copy it into the record"; the brain reads its own command as "I moved, do not update my model of the world." **Established** (corollary discharge); **analogy** (the mapping). One difference: the model infers provenance, while nervous systems often transmit the cause explicitly.

**Setpoint changes arriving through a reference channel.** In fever, prostaglandin E2 acts on the preoptic area, and deleting EP3 receptors selectively in the median preoptic nucleus abolished fever in mice (Lazarus et al., 2007); Nakamura (2011) reviews how the preoptic area integrates skin temperature with the infection signal. The defended temperature rises only through a channel carrying evidence of the cause, never because the body is hot - a body heated by exercise activates heat loss instead. **Established** (fever as a PGE2-driven regulated rise); **supported** (the EP3/MnPO requirement). Romanovsky (2007) cautions that there may be no single thermoregulatory setpoint, only independent effector loops with their own thresholds (Section 8). During exercise the arterial baroreflex is reset upward by both the feed-forward mechanism of central command and feedback from muscle afferents (Fadel and Raven, 2012): central command is a corollary discharge telling the pressure controller that the coming rise is intended. **Supported.** Body weight shows both sides. After weight loss or gain, energy expenditure shifted to oppose the change and the shift persisted once the new weight was stable (Leibel, Rosenbaum and Hirsch, 1995), evidence of a value not simply copied from the body. But Speakman et al. (2011) argue a pure set-point model cannot explain rising body weight since the 1980s and propose settling-point and dual-intervention-point alternatives, and Hinte et al. (2024) found human and mouse adipose tissue retains transcriptional changes after substantial weight loss, with persistent obesity-induced epigenetic changes in mouse adipocytes accompanying faster regain. A sustained body state appears to have been written into a tissue record that then pulls the body back: capture without provenance, filtered only by duration. **Supported** (Leibel; Hinte); the set-point framing itself **debated**.

**Immunology: writing needs a second signal.** Bretscher and Cohn (1970) proposed that recognizing one determinant paralyses the cell while induction requires an obligatory second, associated recognition; the requirement for costimulation is now textbook. Matzinger (1994) argued the system responds to danger from injured or stressed tissue rather than to non-self as such. The immune record of what to attack is rewritten only when change arrives with evidence about its source. **Established** (two-signal activation); **analogy** (the mapping); the danger model is partly accepted.

**When both sources are damaged.** A double-strand break damages both strands at one site, so the duplex holds no intact template. As in the model's combined-damage case, only a protected copy rescues it: the sister chromatid (Section 5). Without it, end-joining restores continuity but not the lost information.

## 5. Copy beats capture; protected copies win

**The central dogma forbids capture.** Crick (1970) stated that once sequence information passes into protein it cannot flow back to nucleic acid, and noted that reverse transcription is compatible with this because it copies nucleic acid to nucleic acid. Molecular heredity takes the copy route and rules out capture. **Established.**

**The germline is a protected copy.** Weismann (1893) argued that germ-plasm passes from germ cells to germ cells, so somatic acquisitions are not transmitted. **Established** as the organizing idea of animal heredity (exceptions in Section 8). The protection is measurable: somatic mutation rates are almost two orders of magnitude above germline rates in humans and mice, with germline rates of 3.3e-11 and 1.2e-10 per base pair per mitosis (Milholland et al., 2017). **Supported.** Plants lack an early-segregated germline but still protect the lineage: axillary meristem precursors are set aside early and stay quiescent while neighbours divide, with only about 7 to 9 divisions between apical and axillary meristems, and branch number rises exponentially while divisions rise linearly (Burian, Barbier de Reuille and Kuhlemeier, 2016). Protection here is achieved by limiting how often the record is copied. **Supported.** The cell carrying the mitochondrial genome forward runs in a protected mode: early human and *Xenopus* oocytes lack assembled, active complex I and so keep a metabolism free of reactive oxygen species, and inducing ROS killed most oocytes (Rodríguez-Nuevo et al., 2022). **Supported.**

**Capture is largely erased between generations.** Heard and Martienssen (2014) review evidence that two rounds of reprogramming in mammals erase most epigenetic marks, that transgenerational epigenetic inheritance is well documented in plants and nematodes but weakly supported in mammals, and that apparent cases may reflect undetected sequence changes, behaviour, microbiome or metabolites. **Supported.**

**Protected copies inside one body.** Kadyk and Hartwell (1992) irradiated synchronized diploid yeast: G1 cells repaired mainly using the homologue, G2 cells preferred the sister chromatid, and simpler explanations (more templates, the RAD9 delay, greater homology) were ruled out; sisters were also able to repair more damage. The sister is an identical copy existing only after replication, and is how a body survives a double-strand break. **Established.** Wilson et al. (2008) identified dormant mouse haematopoietic stem cells dividing roughly every 145 days, about five times in a mouse's life, holding most long-term self-renewal activity, activated by injury or G-CSF and returning to dormancy afterwards: a reserve that is rarely copied and called on when the live mechanism is depleted. **Supported.** One candidate protected store failed: Cairns (1975) proposed stem cells retain older template strands, but haematopoietic stem cells segregate chromosomes randomly and do not retain BrdU (Kiel et al., 2007), so in this tissue protection comes from dormancy, not strand choice. **Supported** (negative result). Finally, the thymus teaches self from a centrally produced copy: under Aire, thymic medullary epithelial cells express genes for peripheral tissue-restricted antigens, Aire-deficient cells show a specific reduction in this ectopic transcription, and the mice develop autoimmunity (Anderson et al., 2002), rather than capturing "self" from peripheral tissue where infection could corrupt it. **Supported** (mechanism); **analogy** (the copy-versus-capture reading).

**Privileged timing is structural in biology.** The model's snapshot gained partly from timing that coincided with damage onset. Germ cells in many animals, and axillary meristems in plants, are set aside early, before most of the divisions in which damage accumulates. The model's privileged pre-damage copy therefore has the clearest biological counterpart, while its live rewriting mechanisms map better onto within-body physiology. **Analogy.**

## 6. The planned next steps, seen from biology

**Duration filters.** Biology has at least three. In the coherent feed-forward loop a target needs a direct and a slower indirect input; Mangan, Zaslaver and Alon (2003) showed in the *E. coli* arabinose system that this is a sign-sensitive delay, responding to a sustained ON signal only after a delay but turning OFF quickly, which filters fluctuations while allowing a fast response - the model's latch window in transcription. **Supported.** McKeithan (1995) proposed that a T-cell receptor signals only after a bound ligand persists through a series of modification steps, which greatly improves discrimination between foreign antigens and self-antigens of moderately lower affinity: brief events are discarded. **Supported** (influential; some responses fall outside its predicted dependence on binding lifetime). And vernalization integrates duration out of digital parts: longer cold switches more cells, so the record is quantitative though each cell is binary (Angel et al., 2011).

**Biological rewriting is gated by phase as much as duration.** Briefly altering bioelectric dynamics with the gap-junction blocker octanol in amputated planarian trunk fragments gave about 25% two-headed and 72% normal-looking one-headed regenerates; the normal-looking animals carried a hidden change, producing the same ratio when cut again in plain water, and the two-headed form persisted through further rounds long after the octanol had left the tissue, with no differences from wild type in histology or key polarity gene expression (Durant et al., 2017). A transient perturbation during regeneration rewrote a stored target that was then defended. **Supported** (largely one laboratory). Otsuki et al. (2025) identified a Hand2-Shh positive-feedback loop behind posterior identity in axolotl: posterior cells retain Hand2 from development, priming them to form a Shh signalling centre after amputation; after regeneration Shh shuts down while Hand2 persists, safeguarding the memory; transient Shh exposure of anterior cells started an ectopic loop giving stable Hand2 and lasting competence; and reprogramming was easier anterior-to-posterior than the reverse. **Supported.** For the model this means rewriting can be gated by a phase of the body's own cycle and can be asymmetric. Retinoids alter the proximodistal pattern of regenerating axolotl limbs, sometimes giving complete limbs from carpal-level amputations (Maden, 1982): a chemical acting on the positional record overrides its value. **Established.** Gain-of-function mutations in the two-pore potassium channel *kcnk5b* raise K+ conductance, hyperpolarize cells and act locally in fin and barbel mesenchyme to enlarge those appendages proportionally (Perathoner et al., 2014). **Supported.** Biology shows both outcomes for sustained change: adoption that outlasts its cause (Hinte et al., 2024) and adoption that tracks it (Torres-Garcia et al., 2020).

**Coarse backups versus a full copy.** Adult human fibroblasts from 43 anatomical sites retain expression programs organized by anterior-posterior, proximal-distal and dermal-versus-non-dermal origin, with key features of the embryonic HOX code persisting in vitro and in vivo (Rinn et al., 2006). Axolotl posterior memory is essentially one bit per cell (Otsuki et al., 2025), and planarian position control genes supply body-wide coordinates (Witchley et al., 2013), while the full protected copy is the germline genome. The division of labour supports the planned contrast: coarse backups suffice wherever local self-organization regenerates the detail; the full copy is needed for what a few bits cannot specify.

**Bodies as digital repeaters.** Bistable cellular memories restore a diluted regulator to an attractor at each division, with chain fidelity set by how often noise carries a cell over the barrier (Acar et al., 2005; Ozbudak et al., 2004). Chromatin shows the cost of a weak repeater: H3K27me3 is halved at each replication and decays within a few cycles without the PRE (Laprell et al., 2017). Across generations, small-RNA silencing in *C. elegans* must be re-created by RNA-dependent RNA polymerases each generation, and its duration is not a passive fade but is set by a feedback loop the authors could tune genetically (Houri-Ze'evi et al., 2016). **Supported.** The repeater's gain, and so the chain's memory length, is itself regulated.

**Evolution of the architecture.** Mutator alleles arose spontaneously and rose in frequency in *E. coli* populations adapting to a new environment, matching computer simulations of mutator evolution in adapting clonal populations (Sniegowski, Gerrish and Lenski, 1997). **Supported.** The guard on the epigenetic record is itself environment-dependent (Torres-Garcia et al., 2020). Selection can move a body response into the record: Waddington (1953) heat-shocked *Drosophila* pupae, selected flies showing a crossveinless phenotype, and after several generations it appeared without heat shock ("genetic assimilation"), with selection across generations supplying the provenance a single body cannot - evidence that the induced state was repeatedly useful. **Established** as a result, its interpretation debated. And buffering hides then releases variation: weakening Hsp90 revealed variants affecting nearly any adult structure, which became independent of the Hsp90 lesion once enriched by selection (Rutherford and Lindquist, 1998). **Supported** (debated). Together these predict strong fixed guards in stable environments and weaker or stress-tunable guards in fluctuating ones (B8).

## 7. The mathematics others have used

**Kinetic proofreading** (findings 4, 6). Hopfield (1974) and, independently, Ninio (1975) showed that a reaction driven by a strong but nonspecific energy source such as phosphate hydrolysis can discriminate better than the substrates' free-energy difference allows, by inserting an energy-consuming, effectively irreversible step that gives wrong substrates a further chance to leave. In Hopfield's words, steps that "otherwise appear to be useless or deleterious complications are seen to be essential to the proofreading function." The model's planned latch is a proofreading step: it trades speed, and in a body energy, for confidence about which source changed.

**Kramers escape** (finding 1). Kramers (1940) treated a Brownian particle escaping a potential well over a barrier and derived the escape rate, whose dependence on barrier height relative to noise is exponential. For a bistable memory this means lifetime falls steeply as noise rises or feedback weakens the barrier, which is what Acar et al. (2005) saw when Gal80p changed the switching rate. The model's regime boundary is a Kramers boundary, and this is also where its frozen soft-mode theory failed: at high noise the reference is not only steering boundaries but suppressing noise-driven escape in the bulk, which a linear local theory cannot represent.

**Error thresholds** (finding 5, planned chains). Eigen (1971) showed that a replicating population keeps a master sequence only below a per-digit error threshold that falls as sequences lengthen; past it, information is lost faster than selection restores it. A chain of captures is an Eigen problem, and the planned digital repeater plays the role selection plays in Eigen's picture.

**Reliable organisms from unreliable parts** (finding 3). Von Neumann (1956) showed that redundancy with repeated majority voting ("restoring organs") lets unreliable components compute with arbitrarily small error, provided component error stays below a threshold. The model's sign decisions on pooled sensors are restoring organs, and his analysis also predicts the dose caveat: the benefit depends on how much redundancy, and so resource, is spent.

**Keeping a bit in a noisy medium** (findings 1 to 3). Toom (1980) gave necessary and sufficient conditions for the all-one state of a monotone cellular automaton to remain stable under small random perturbations, as restated by Swart, Szabó and Toninelli (2022); the standard example is the two-dimensional north-east-centre majority rule, which erodes finite islands of the wrong phase. Bennett and Grinstein (1985) showed that irreversibility is what keeps such systems from nucleating and growing their single most-stable phase, so they can hold complex, non-ergodic behaviour over a positive-measure region of parameter space, which reversible analogues cannot. This bears directly on the lane's Ising/Allen-Cahn-like body: a detailed-balance body forgets under noise because the more stable configuration eventually wins, so holding an arbitrary pattern needs either an external bias (the model's R) or irreversible, energy-consuming correction of the Toom type. Living bodies, far from equilibrium, use both. Gács (2001) constructed an asynchronous one-dimensional fault-tolerant cellular automaton that keeps information indefinitely and can start from a near-uniform configuration; its construction is hierarchical, with blocks simulating cells of a coarser level recursively and correcting errors at every scale. In two dimensions a simple local rule suffices; in one dimension the known construction needs an unbounded hierarchy. This is the strongest precedent for the lane's question of when multi-scale correction is necessary rather than merely helpful.

**Integral feedback** (finding 1; Section 8). Yi, Huang, Simon and Doyle (2000) showed that robust perfect adaptation in bacterial chemotaxis is integral feedback control, structurally inherent in the Barkai-Leibler model, and that integral control in some form is necessary for a robust implementation of perfect adaptation. Here the setpoint is not a stored pattern but a value encoded in reaction rates, which matters for which model results transfer.

## 8. Where the analogy breaks

**1. Most biological setpoints are not stored patterns read through a gain.** They are scalars enforced by integral feedback (Yi et al., 2000), balance points of independent effector loops with no single comparator (Romanovsky, 2007), settling points of passive feedback (Speakman et al., 2011), or absent altogether, as in the bacterial adder (Taheri-Araghi et al., 2015). Patterned records exist but are coarse. The model's high-resolution R has no clear counterpart except the genome, which is not read through a proportional gain.

**2. Record and body are often the same molecules.** H3K27me3 nucleosomes are both state and memory (Coleman and Struhl, 2017); bioelectric state is both physiology and stored target (Durant et al., 2017). The model's clean separation is what makes "which source moved?" well posed; in many tissues it must be asked of one coupled system.

**3. Bodies have partly redundant references - the clearest contradiction.** The model says coarse record errors do lasting harm because nothing in its body can correct them. Yet natural variation in Bicoid is largely filtered by the time the *hunchback* boundary forms, and that boundary scales with embryo size (Houchmandzadeh et al., 2002), while large experimental shifts do move the pattern (Driever and Nüsslein-Volhard, 1988). Biology agrees about large coarse errors but has a second line of defence - cross-checking between references - that a single-reference model cannot exhibit. A two-reference variant would test whether this closes the gap.

**4. Rewriting is phase-gated and asymmetric.** The model's arbiter may rewrite at any update after a qualifying mismatch. Biological rewriting of morphological setpoints concentrates in regenerative windows and is directionally biased (Durant et al., 2017; Otsuki et al., 2025), and transient perturbations can rewrite permanently - which the planned latch is designed to prevent. The biological gate looks more like "when" than "how long".

**5. Provenance is usually an explicit mark, not an inference.** Bodies carry hemimethylated DNA, nascent-strand ribonucleotides, efference copies, immune-derived prostaglandins and costimulatory signals. Explicit marks have a failure mode the model lacks: corrupting the marking system turns the corrector into a source of errors (Herman and Modrich, 1981).

**6. Bodies are driven far from equilibrium.** The model's body is near-equilibrium and cannot hold an arbitrary pattern under noise (Bennett and Grinstein, 1985), while bodies spend energy on proofreading and irreversible correction (Hopfield, 1974). The model's reference is an external force with no energy budget, and its finding that sign decisions deliver more dose shows why the budget matters.

**7. Growth, division and turnover are the main biological noise.** Replication dilution erases chromatin marks without their DNA record (Laprell et al., 2017), and turnover is what makes dormancy valuable (Wilson et al., 2008). The model's fixed-size sheet with additive noise bundles together processes biology treats very differently.

**8. Populations resolve what one body cannot.** The model concludes that under combined damage no permitted signal identifies the trustworthy source. Within a body that matches break repair without a sister. Across generations, selection supplies the missing provenance (Waddington, 1953; Rutherford and Lindquist, 1998; Sniegowski et al., 1997). The model has no population yet, which is where the planned evolution experiments matter.

**9. "Copy beats capture" holds for sequence, not every record.** It is near-absolute for DNA (Crick, 1970; Weismann, 1893), but biology does capture other records for a few generations (Houri-Ze'evi et al., 2016; Torres-Garcia et al., 2020), and plants lack an early-segregated germline (Burian et al., 2016). The finding should be read as being about a body that is a noisy copy of its record; where the body is the better source, the model itself found reciprocal writing could help a little.

**10. One candidate protected store failed.** Template-strand retention (Cairns, 1975) did not hold in blood (Kiel et al., 2007). Protected storage is real, but its mechanisms must be shown case by case rather than inferred from the model's logic.

## 9. Testable predictions for biology

Each gives the prediction, an experiment, and the evidence already in hand. "Novel" means no direct test was found in this search.

**B1. A damaged reference should harm most where the body holds by itself.** In fission yeast, tether Clr4 to write a deliberately wrong or partial domain, then release it, in *epe1*+ cells (where the native loop forgets) and *epe1*Δ cells (where it holds). Prediction: relative to no initiator, the wrong initiator does more lasting harm in *epe1*Δ, because the wrong state is then self-maintained, while in *epe1*+ it decays. *Evidence:* both halves of the regime picture are established (Audergon et al., 2015; Ragunathan et al., 2015), and stress-induced ectopic heterochromatin shows wrong states can be locked in when the guard is lowered (Torres-Garcia et al., 2020). The contrast itself is novel.

**B2. Coarse errors in a positional record should do lasting damage; fine ones should be filtered.** In planarian body-wall muscle, perturb position control gene expression at two scales - a broad regional change against a scattered single-cell change, matched for total perturbation. Prediction: the regional change alters the regenerated body plan, the scattered one does not, because neighbour coupling and sorting absorb it. *Evidence:* consistent with sorting that sharpens noisy boundaries (Xiong et al., 2013) and with shortest intercalation smoothing small disparities (French et al., 1976). A scale-matched comparison in one system was not found.

**B3. Blurring a provenance mark should make a corrector harmful, in proportion to the blur.** Titrate Dam methylase across a range in mismatch-repair-proficient and -deficient *E. coli* and measure mutation rates. Prediction: as the strand mark degrades, the proficient strain's rate rises above the deficient one, because repair acts without reliable attribution. *Evidence:* partly in hand and partly conflicting - hypermutability at about 50-fold overproduction (Herman and Modrich, 1981) but not at a reported 10-fold, which is the dose dependence predicted. A clean titration in both backgrounds would settle it.

**B4. Transient versus sustained perturbation should separate adoption from resistance.** In the planarian bioelectric system, vary perturbation duration at constant intensity and measure the fraction of animals whose target morphology is rewritten. The planned latch predicts a duration threshold rather than a dose threshold. *Evidence:* brief exposure already produces a persistent, partly cryptic rewrite (Durant et al., 2017), which suggests the real gate is the regenerative window. A duration-by-intensity grid would distinguish them, and a null result for duration would be evidence against the latch design.

**B5. A protected copy should win precisely when both sources are damaged.** Compare repair of lesions affecting both strands at one site in cells with and without an identical sister chromatid available, holding homologue availability constant. Prediction: only sister-based repair restores the original sequence, while homologue-based repair can write the wrong allele into the record. *Evidence:* sister preference in G2 is established (Kadyk and Hartwell, 1992) and homologue copying is a known route to loss of heterozygosity (Cavenee et al., 1983). The model adds that the advantage should be largest for simultaneous damage to both copies.

**B6. Capture routes should lose information per generation; copy routes should not.** Compare transmission fidelity of a state passed by template copying against one passed through the body. Prediction: capture persists for a limited number of generations set by body noise. *Evidence:* germline rates are about two orders of magnitude below somatic (Milholland et al., 2017), small-RNA inheritance lasts a limited and tunable number of generations (Houri-Ze'evi et al., 2016), and mammalian reprogramming erases most captured marks (Heard and Martienssen, 2014). The per-generation comparison of the two routes within one organism was not found.

**B7. Rarely-copied reserves should carry fewer errors than their working descendants.** Sequence dormant and cycling cells from the same compartment in the same animal and compare somatic mutation burden. Prediction: the dormant fraction carries fewer replication-associated mutations, because protection comes from being copied less often. *Evidence:* dormant haematopoietic stem cells divide about five times in a mouse's life (Wilson et al., 2008) and plant axillary precursors pass through few divisions (Burian et al., 2016), while template-strand retention was excluded in blood (Kiel et al., 2007), leaving division count as the candidate.

**B8. Environmental variability should predict how tightly the record is guarded.** Across related strains or species from stable and fluctuating environments, measure how readily a heritable epigenetic record is written from a body state under stress. Prediction: lineages from fluctuating environments have weaker or more stress-tunable guards. *Evidence:* the guard is stress-tunable in fission yeast (Torres-Garcia et al., 2020), mutation rates rise during adaptation (Sniegowski et al., 1997), and buffering hides then releases variation (Rutherford and Lindquist, 1998). The comparative test is novel, and is the biological form of the lane's planned selection-across-environments experiment.
