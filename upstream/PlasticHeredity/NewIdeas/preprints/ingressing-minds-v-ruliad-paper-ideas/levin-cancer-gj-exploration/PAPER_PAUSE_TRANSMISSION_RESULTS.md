## What was transmitted and retained

Each readout starts from the complete 17×3 state of a saved final donor trio,
plus three fresh stock-M cells. All six cells evolve during 20000 units of
contact. The actual recipients then undergo two consecutive isolated
10000-unit holds, with no donor cells present and no recipient reset.
The archived clock continues from 1201800 to 1241800.

In every early-pause open readout, all three recipients are stable E during
contact and in both isolated holds. In the corresponding RNA-blocked and cut
readouts, all three recipients are stable M in all three phases. The early-pause
donors end contact as E under every readout. Their own E state is therefore
insufficient to convert recipients when the tested communication route is
blocked.

Stable E requires an E endpoint and at least 95% E samples in the last quarter
of a phase. The registered transmission predicate requires this in both
isolated holds; contact stability is separately reported. The result is
therefore stronger than an E endpoint during donor contact alone.

Reference readouts reproduce all 33 numeric arrays in each of their three
archived P22 phases exactly: 36 reference phases and 1,188 array comparisons.

on the historical incoming sequence and replacement length. It adds a fresh
recipient generation with isolated retention. It does not rerun renewal or
establish robustness to new incoming states, longer turnover, other pause
durations or further recipient generations. The donors were selected using
known P22 results before P23; this is a planned follow-up to that observation.

These are externally scheduled model results. They do not establish
autonomous reproduction, cell division, a unique molecular memory carrier,
an advantage of written over ordinary source history, or a biological cancer
intervention. Agreement between the histories is evidence against claiming
such a history advantage from this panel.
