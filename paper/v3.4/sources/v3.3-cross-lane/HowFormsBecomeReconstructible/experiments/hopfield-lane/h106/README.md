Copying remains a strong benchmark. With the **cue-prepared reader**, protected
original records score **98.58% at every horizon**, while the coupled
cue-prepared supplying/reading history scores **98.39% at G4**. The favorable
common-standard-reader comparison with the original record therefore does
not establish general superiority of rewriting to copying. The original
Hebbian record and later projection records differ; copying the first
high-quality projection packet was not tested. The small coupled-score
difference is descriptive, not a qualified copying advantage.

The result supports a bounded access mechanism: one cue-guided preparation
sweep followed by ordinary recurrence improves retrieval and the records that
actual suppliers subsequently write. Supplier task success stays **98.44--
98.54%**, with all **eight hidden patterns distinct** at every supplying stage.
Preparation alone gives **75.49--91.50%** supplier success, below the completed
access trajectories. The recurrent phase still contributes. These are eight
taught cues with a supplied correct cue mapping and an externally controlled
matrix gate, not recovery of an arbitrary erased interpretation or new
unseen-combination retention.

This supplies a viable four-handoff transmission setting for returning to the
original endpoint-reversal question. H106 itself improves both present access
and transmission; it does **not** establish a present-benefit/successor-cost
reversal. No next experiment has been launched.

H105 found stable patterns and mostly settled but incorrect endpoints. H106
therefore freezes a specific access intervention: **one cue-guided preparation
sweep, followed by seven ordinary sweeps**. Preparation temporarily suppresses
the free-to-free block of the recipient matrix; it uses the inherited record,
surviving cues and fresh birth only. The ordinary full matrix is restored for
the remaining seven sweeps. No captured source activity or teacher target is
available to the policy.

The equally budgeted controls are eight ordinary sweeps, and two independent
four-sweep attempts with the lower-energy endpoint selected for each query.
All use **eight free-coordinate sweeps**, twelve energy evaluations including
two full-matrix endpoint energies, and matched orders, injury and first birth.
Second births are also frozen for every condition; only two_start uses them.
Actual per-policy time is measured separately from deterministic audits.

Three actual supplying histories use these policies with the unchanged
projection writer over **four actual handoffs**. The writer receives only its
own body's final activity. Cross every record history with every reader, and
retain protected copying, other-founder records and erased records. Assessment
tapes are matched across histories, readers and G0--G4, so protected records
must have exactly constant assessments within each reader.

There are two separate primary verdicts:

- **Fixed-record access:** on identical standard-history records, preparation
  must recover the actual supplier's exact output on >=80% of queries at every
  G1--G4 and improve by >=5 points over each control, with a positive adjusted
  interval and support on >=16/32 networks.
- **Supply-policy gain:** with all histories read by the common standard
  reader, prepared suppliers' records must retain >=50% task success at every
  handoff and improve by >=5 points over both supplying controls. Adjusted
  intervals, network support, copying capability and source-control gaps also
  apply. Improved final access cannot replace this common-reader test.
