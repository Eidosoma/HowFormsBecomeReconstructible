# Observation and confidence calculations

The native calculations condition on a particular starting packet and the TT003 nuisance distribution. If W is the packet at a renewal boundary, define p(W) as its probability of ancestral-rule success in a fresh body. Two independent clone assays Y and Z satisfy E[Y|W]=E[Z|W]=p(W) and E[YZ|W]=p(W)^2. Therefore

$$E[1\{Y\ne Z\}]/2=E[p(W)(1-p(W))],$$

$$\operatorname{Var}(Y)=E[p(W)(1-p(W))]+\operatorname{Var}(p(W)).$$

For independent path pairs, the average product of pair means from distinct paths estimates E[p(W)] squared without the usual squaring bias. Subtracting it from mean(YZ) gives the between-packet variance estimate used here. A finite realization may be negative. Clipping it would change the estimator and its expectation. At depth zero, W is the identical source packet in all paths, so the true between-packet term is zero even when fresh assays vary. For the three-category disagreement interval, use Y!=Z on the full category labels; the variance decomposition separately uses binary ancestral-success indicators.

The native probability intervals use equal-tailed Clopper-Pearson limits. For a binomial count k out of n, the lower endpoint solves P_p(K>=k)=alpha/2 unless k=0; the upper solves P_p(K<=k)=alpha/2 unless k=n. Each of the 48 intervals receives error .05/48. The union bound limits the chance of any missed parameter to .05, without assuming independence between intervals. This relies on the specified independent draws within each conditional sample; it does not create a population guarantee from two source blocks. [SciPy's method documentation](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy-1.16.1/reference/generated/scipy.stats._result_classes.BinomTestResult.proportion_ci.html) identifies the exact and Wilson procedures.

In the absorbing-loss submodel, short-path survival is s=(1-q)^2 and horizon-H survival is s^(H/2). A monotone transformation of the binomial confidence interval for s preserves its coverage under that model. After n successful paths and no failures, its two-sided lower limit is (alpha/2)^(H/(2n)). For n=16 and H=32 this equals .025. Requiring that best-case lower limit to exceed a target t gives n>(H/2)*log(alpha/2)/log(t). This assumes a fixed sample size and zero observed losses; it is not a stopping rule or a general native sample-size calculation.

In the fully readable symmetric-flip submodel, the 2n observed transition indicators are independent Bernoulli(q), even though successive identities are dependent. Intersect the binomial interval with q in [0,.5], then apply [1+(1-2q)^H]/2 with reversed endpoints. Intersection with the known domain preserves coverage for parameters inside that domain. Empty intersections remain rejections. Coverage is assessed in parameter space because rounding a very small identity bias to zero can falsely make distinct forecast values look identical.

For selection adjustment, let S be the fixed set of counts whose original exact interval is nonempty and at most .10 wide. Conditional on K belonging to S, its mass is proportional to binom(n,k)*p^k*(1-p)^(n-k) for k in S. The likelihood ratio for two p values is monotone in k on this same support, including when S is disconnected. Thus upper and lower conditional tails can be inverted just as ordinary binomial tails are. Each tail contributes at most alpha/2 rejection probability conditional on selection, giving coverage of at least 1-alpha whenever selection has positive probability. Counts at the support boundaries receive endpoints zero or one before intersecting a restricted parameter domain.

The implementation solves these conditional equations in log-odds coordinates with stable normalization. Its source is frozen with each run. The audit separately evaluates finite binomial masses, selected-tail values and achieved coverage throughout the declared panel. Corrected intervals may widen. Filtering them again would define a different selection event and invalidate the stated conditional guarantee unless that further event were included in the calculation.

These derivations calibrate two restricted statistical models. They do not establish those assumptions for native full rewriting, identify the full five-parameter candidate, or control structural model error. The observation counterexample and reused unrestricted-future counterexample keep those separate questions explicit.
