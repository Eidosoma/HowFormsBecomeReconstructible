## What the coding calculation does establish

For an ideal nonzero pure-contrast record, write vertex signs as s_i = (−1)^{z_i}. The binary edge sign is b_ij = z_i + z_j modulo 2. Thus the sign vector belongs to the graph's cut space. It is orthogonal to every cycle vector: summing its edge bits around a cycle gives zero.

Before injury or pin constraints, the 16×16 periodic nearest-neighbor graph has 256 vertices and 512 edges. Its incidence rank over GF(2) is 255; the cycle-space rank is 512−255 = 257. The 256 elementary face cycles have rank 255. Two independent winding cycles are also needed on the torus; local square checks alone are insufficient to certify every sign relation.

The reproducible check (see [source availability](../SOURCE_AVAILABILITY.md)) constructs this graph, calculates the ranks, checks cut–cycle orthogonality, and verifies that two different coordinate assignments both pass every parity check. Its [receipt](V3.3_SIGN_CODE_CHECK.json) contains the source-method and script hashes. This is an analytic verification, with no simulator trajectory or new empirical sample.

For a surviving graph with n vertices, m nonzero edges and c connected components, the corresponding unrestricted cut-space dimension is n−c and cycle-space dimension m−n+c. Isolated vertices provide no edge-sign information. The full-lattice counts must not be applied unchanged to damaged support or to a pin-constrained subset.

Two distinct consistent sign vectors can differ by a cut and still have zero syndrome. A consistency-only screen cannot determine which history produced one of them. Extra historical information, constrained port relationships, independent observations or a declared prior may distinguish cases; NL003's anchors are one example of such a source, not the only possible remedy. An equally plausible alternative that is indistinguishable under all permitted inputs remains an information limit.

## What it does not establish

**A universal erasure/error ratio.** The worst-case unique-decoding condition for minimum distance d is 2t+e<d. It gives guarantees for a specified code and ideal decoding problem, not a ratio of observed reconstruction rates from different cohorts. For the unrestricted full torus cut code, the minimum distance is four: isolating one vertex gives four cut edges, and any nonempty proper vertex subset has at least four boundary edges. One way to see the lower bound is that a partial cyclic row contributes at least two horizontal boundaries and a partial cyclic column at least two vertical ones; the exceptional full-row/full-column subsets have still larger boundaries. Thus its worst-case guarantees are three erasures or one error, even though many larger particular erasure patterns can be resolved.

**Optimal decoding by TT014.** The frozen procedure uses magnitudes, supplied pins, a finite optimization budget and a specified rule for filling zeros. Ordinary-fallback and preservation mixtures need not be balanced codewords. Zero positions reveal absent connections but do not prove that every absence came from corruption of an originally nonzero valid bit. Weighted input normalization and finite-time dynamics also remain outside the binary code. E130 additionally perturbs magnitudes and uses a particular forest/screen procedure. The coding language describes a structural part of these problems, not a demonstrated optimal decoder.

**An unconditional distance between the two learned tasks.** For two particular balanced sign vectors, their Hamming distance is the size of their particular difference cut. A minimum cut is relevant only after defining classes of allowed vertex assignments. In the ideal all-nonzero model, if every input-port orientation is held fixed and every output-port orientation is required to reverse, minimizing the changed-edge count is a terminal-cut problem separating the input set from the union of the output sets. This is a possible adversarial structural test. Our measured endpoint instead uses port means, finite amplitudes, damage and noisy dynamics, and many microscopic records can implement either task. The proposed cut therefore does not yet predict the corruption needed to flip the measured response. No numerical task-port cut or efficacy prediction is reported in this revision.

## Interpretation safeguards

- Data processing requires the stated Markov/sufficient-state condition and inclusion of all additional historical sources. A protected copy preserves its input's information; it need not maximize the output of a fixed, limited native reader. A better accessible representation can improve a task without increasing mutual information about its history.
- A one-way bound does not prove geometric decay. Finite irreducible aperiodic chains admit geometric convergence bounds; reducible, periodic, changing or inadequately specified dynamics need separate analysis. Neither a nonzero plateau nor indefinite retention follows from sixteen observations.
- Retrieval-triggered biological restabilization, model-defined packet erasure and externally scheduled teaching are related experimental questions, not identical operations.
- Gurdon's community effect is not treated as a direct demonstration of P11's serial relay. Lu and Celià-Terrassa locate the regulatory motif and memory question; exact BITSEY publication-table correspondence remains unresolved as documented in SI C.7.
- Cryptic planarians strongly motivate separating current appearance from later regeneration. They do not supply TT018's exact-state restoration or independent future draws.
