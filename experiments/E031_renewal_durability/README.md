# E031 — durability through sixteen damaged renewals

**Complete and independently audited; long-term durability failed.** The
unchanged preserve-strong/refresh-weak policy retains substantially more
function than full rewriting after eight and sixteen replacements, but falls
well below the required reliability. E032's diagnosis and E033's receiving
intervention are complete; [E034](../E034_settled_rebuilding/README.md) found a
modest one-boundary rebuilding improvement that missed its predefined criterion.

The study retained all 32 fresh blocks 27000–27031: 128 founders and **512
lineages**, all ten policy conditions, both receiving rules, all terminal
controls and sixteen serial boundaries. Prior cases were not pooled. Every
renewal body lost 24 fresh non-port cells except the intact-renewal reference.
The >0.5 copying cutoff, activity-only capture and full scientific timing were
unchanged from the confirmed E030 policy.

At sixteen replacements the primary result was **114/512, 22.27%
[18.36, 26.56]**. Its lower bound fails the frozen **>75%** requirement.
The paired advantage over full rewriting was **15.23 percentage points
[11.33, 19.34]**; the prespecified noninferiority requirement passed. At eight
replacements the corresponding advantage was 15.23 points [11.13, 19.53].
These positive comparisons do not rescue the failed absolute-reliability
endpoint. Secondary intervals and qualification gates are pointwise.

Only **18/23 additional qualifications** passed. The failures were the
candidate's intact/scattered both-cue reliability at h8, intact reliability
at h16, and original 24-gate sets at h8/h16. All twelve full-rewriting
read/write necessity checks passed. Candidate original gates passed 24/24 at
h3, 21/24 at h8 and 18/24 at h16. Reading-blocked, refresh-blocked, old exact-zero
infill and cumulative-erasure-copy chains all reached **0/512** scattered
both-cue successes at h8 and h16. Blocking continued renewal processes
therefore removed the surviving function in this ensemble.

The decline also appeared without added terminal injury: candidate intact
success fell from 97.27% at h3 to 70.31% at h8 and 34.77% at h16. Square-terminal
success was 33.98% at h16. Intact-renewal and protected-copy references retained
512/512 normalized successes across injuries at every observed horizon;
finite observed perfection does not imply a population guarantee. The same
candidate records reached only 1.37% scattered success with the fixed-four
receiver at h16, so reader compensation remains consequential.

Current-body both-cue performance also declined: 55.47% at generation 8 and
28.91% at generation 16 for the candidate, versus 40.63% and 8.01% under full
rewriting. Those body and terminal measurements involve different records and
challenges; their difference alone does not identify the failure mechanism.
The useful next lead is to trace failure onset in the saved per-generation
records, distinguishing deterioration during reading from corruption or
premature preservation during record renewal, then design a targeted
intervention. No such diagnosis or new intervention was started at closeout.
