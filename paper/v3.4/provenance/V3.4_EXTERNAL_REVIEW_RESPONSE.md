## Primary literature checked

- Owen, Osmanović and Mirny (2023), [Design principles of 3D epigenetic memory systems](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11075759/): alternating chromatin and mark dynamics, restoration under erosion, and the close precedent for coupled organization/record maintenance.
- Hopfield and Brody (2004), [Learning rules and network repair in spike-timing-based computation networks](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC314186/): activity-derived, task-dependent plasticity rules for repair and learning. Checked through the primary PubMed/PMC search record when direct page retrieval was inconsistent.
- Raman and O'Leary (2021), [Optimal plasticity for memory maintenance during ongoing synaptic change](https://elifesciences.org/articles/62912): an explicit predictive compensation account. No claim that our models implement their assumptions.
- Fauth, Wörgötter and Tetzlaff (2015), [Formation and Maintenance of Robust Long-Term Information Storage in the Presence of Synaptic Turnover](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1004684): early loss, plateau and eventual mixing. The comparison motivates a mechanism test, not an inferred explanation of our plateau.

## Editorial policy for subsequent evidence

Main-text selection follows the question and the strongest discriminating evidence, rather than accumulating one paragraph for every campaign. A newer experiment may replace an older headline. Original verdicts and methods remain available in the ledger/SI; a failure stays beside the current claim whenever it changes that claim's scope. The four chapters and all twenty main/five supporting figures are retained in this pass.

The revision does not require completing all proposed experiments before human review. A compact auditable package for the selected central claims remains a separate release priority. The v3.4 PDF has not yet been built.
